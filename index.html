Five insights that shaped everything

The findings that became the foundation.

These five insights cut across every demographic and showed up again and again. Each one directly shaped a decision in the solution that follows.

01
The emotional gap

"Nobody remembers what I need."

Across every demographic — working moms, gig drivers, retirees, tech professionals — the strongest unmet need wasn't price or speed. It was being known. The same language showed up repeatedly: "nobody has patience for me," "nobody remembers."

→ Shaped: The AI memory layer as the central moat. Every customer gets a dedicated agent.
02
The white space

India has zero organized convenience stores.

Japan has 80,000+. Thailand has 13,000+. The format works in every Asian market it enters. Nobody has Indianised it. Studying 7-Eleven in Bangkok and FamilyMart in Taipei — operationally brilliant, emotionally empty — made the gap obvious.

→ Shaped: The convenience-store format itself + the "Indianise it" thesis (warmth + culture + memory).
03
The suburban gap

Quick commerce can't make suburbs work.

Zepto and Blinkit need dense urban cores and dark-store math to even approach unit economics. Tier 2-3 suburbs like Beeramguda are structurally unprofitable for them. The window is wide open for the next 2-3 years.

→ Shaped: The decision to start in Beeramguda and the Subscribe model that beats quick commerce on small-basket economics.
04
The failure pattern

Kirana-tech digitised the wrong thing.

Khatabook, BharatPe, Dukaan raised $1B+ between them and mostly failed. They digitised the ledger — the accounting layer. They should have digitised the customer relationship. The relationship is the product. The software is just plumbing.

→ Shaped: Consto OS centres on customer memory, not bookkeeping. The Agent product comes before the POS in the strategic narrative.
05
The channel reality

WhatsApp is the only zero-friction channel — except for power users.

500M+ Indians on WhatsApp, 3 hours a day, zero install friction. Any solution requiring a new app fails immediately for the 55+ demographic. But power users — working professionals, tech families — genuinely want a faster way and will install a focused app if it earns the install.

→ Shaped: Four engagement layers — Widget · App · WhatsApp · Store. Different surfaces for different customers.
06
The checkout friction

Long queues kill the daily-trip habit.

Indian retail has unpredictable checkout queues — kiranas, DMart, even premium supermarkets routinely have 8-15 minute waits. Customers stop returning for quick items because they know the line will eat the trip. 7-Eleven Japan solved this with disciplined POS + multiple parallel counters. Modern Indian retail still hasn't.

→ Shaped: AI-assisted speed billing in Consto POS — target under 90 seconds per customer even at peak. Multiple parallel counters. Self-checkout for subscribers as a Phase-2 add (opt-in, not forced — Lakshmi aunty still gets a human at the counter).
07
The feedback void

Stores don't listen. Customers absorb.

Every customer interviewed had unmet requests they'd given up voicing. Kirana owners "know" but rarely change. Supermarkets ignore. No store has a real mechanism to capture "the batter was sour today," or "I wish you stocked organic ghee." That data exists in every conversation — it's never collected.

→ Shaped: AI Agent captures every customer signal — requests, complaints, missed items. Inventory adjusts weekly based on real signal, not guesswork.
08
The culture void

Modern retail strips local soul in the name of scale.

DMart, More, Reliance Fresh use the same store template across India. Same layout in Hyderabad as in Pune. Festivals reduced to a generic "Diwali OFFERS" end-cap stack. Telugu families shop in stores that don't acknowledge Bonalu. Punjabi families shop in stores that don't know what Lohri stock looks like. Local culture is sacrificed for operational uniformity — at the exact moment when local fit is what customers crave most.

→ Shaped: 30% local soul per location. Regional festival calendar runs the store. Replicate the method across India, not the template.

05 · Product Strategy

Built for how Indian households actually shop.

Indian customers don't categorise stores by Western formats. They walk in expecting to find what they need. If Consto carries too little, they leave disappointed. If it carries too much, it becomes a confused mini-supermarket. The product strategy resolves this tension by leading with fresh, curating by frequency, and adapting by region.

"Consumers don't shop a category. They shop a household."

A Beeramguda mother walking in at 7am isn't thinking "convenience store." She's thinking: milk, coriander, idli batter, school snack for my son, agarbatti running low. Consto is designed to answer that whole sentence — not just one word of it.

Pillar A · Fresh & Healthy

Fresh leads. Always.

Indian households shop fresh every 1-2 days, not weekly. If Consto owns the morning fresh run, everything else follows. Fresh is structured into three honest tiers — no marketing-speak, no false organic claims.

Pillar C · Cultural fit

Culture-first retail. Starting Hyderabad. Scaling India region-by-region.

Most modern Indian retail copies one global template and bolts a local language on top. Consto inverts that — it starts from the regional kitchen, builds the store outward from there, and replicates the method, not the template, in every new region.

Hyderabad is store #01 because that's home, the supplier networks are mapped, and the founder's research is deepest there. But the model isn't Hyderabad-only — it's "deep local fit at every location," which scales naturally to every Indian region.

Where we start · Hyderabad / Telangana

Four examples of what "deep local fit" looks like in Hyderabad. The same depth gets built in every future region — adapted to its festivals, kitchens, and rhythms.

🪔

The Telugu festival calendar runs the store

Ugadi, Bonalu, Bathukamma, Varalakshmi Vratam, Vinayaka Chavithi, Diwali, Sankranti — each triggers a specific 8-15 SKU rotation. AI agent pre-books with families via WhatsApp the week before. Mango leaves don't arrive accidentally on Ugadi morning. They're already on the shelf.

☕

South Indian beverages, not generic tea/coffee

Filter coffee powder (Narasus, Cothas, Levista) is a per-household 3-brand decision. Decoction coffee culture matters. Buttermilk in summer is daily. The beverage shelf is sized for South India, not Mumbai.

🍚

Idli/dosa batter as a daily anchor

Fresh batter from 2-3 home-kitchen suppliers (women-run micro-businesses), delivered each morning. ID Fresh + iD batter + local. No quick commerce can match this — Zepto delivers from a dark store, Consto sells what was made at 4am that day.

🍱

Tiffin partnership for IT-corridor commuters

Beeramguda has thousands of IT employees commuting to Hitec City. Consto curates (doesn't cook) — partnering with 2-3 local home-tiffin makers for fresh breakfast (7-9am) and dinner (6-9pm). It's a relationship play, not a kitchen build.

How this scales across India

Same method. Different region. Different soul.

The system is "build the store outward from the kitchen of the region you're in." That method works anywhere. What changes is the kitchen, the calendar, and the supplier network. The brand stays one. The cultural surface adapts completely.

The same Consto OS handles all of this — the AI memory layer learns each region's calendar, the inventory module sources from local suppliers, the agent voice adapts to the local language. Building one store in Hyderabad is the same engineering effort as the second in Pune or the fiftieth in Ludhiana.

Pillar D · The decision system

Stock that adapts. Discipline that holds.

Consto doesn't claim to know on day one what every store will or won't carry. The truth is more honest — and more interesting. Stock is decided through a three-layer system: the format sets the range, customer signal moves stock inside that range, and a few categories are deliberately ceded to specialist competitors.

01
The floor

Format defines the range

Where the store sits decides what's even possible — Daily is tight, Home is broad, Hub is broadest.

A highway Daily store can't carry full grocery. A residential Home store can.

The adjustment

Customer signal moves stock

Within the format's range, AI Inventory listens to what customers actually ask for and adjusts every week.

"5 households in your colony asked the agent for ragi flour this week. Stock added Tuesday."

The store gets smarter every month. SKUs that don't move come off; SKUs customers ask for come on.

03
The discipline

Some categories, we let competitors win

A handful of categories are deliberately ceded — not because we couldn't stock them, but because picking the right fight matters.

Examples on the right →

What we deliberately don't chase

Picking the fights worth winning.

These categories belong to specialists who do them better. Trying to compete on them dilutes the convenience identity. We send customers to the right place for these — and that earns trust.

→
25kg bulk staples — DMart wins on volume price. We carry small & medium packs for top-ups.
→
Frozen meat & fish — Specialist butchers and fish vendors handle this. Cold-chain operational drag isn't worth it.
→
Loose flowers (daily) — Too perishable, low margin. We partner with a local flower vendor instead.
→
Electronics, clothing, furniture — Destination purchases. They need destination stores.

And the rest? Honestly, we'll learn. If 30 households in Beeramguda start asking for puja-only oils we hadn't planned for, the AI Inventory layer will surface that signal and we'll stock it. Discipline isn't pretending we know everything on day one — it's having a system that learns faster than the competition.

06 · Format Variants

Three formats. One brand. One memory layer.

The brand promise stays constant: "the neighbourhood store that knows you." The format adapts — the relationship doesn't.

Engagement A · Subscribe

Consto Subscribe — the daily-route moat.

Subscription pre-pays the delivery route. The marginal cost of adding "1 coconut" to a route the van is already running becomes zero. This is the same model Sid's Farm and Akshayakalpa use to beat quick commerce on small-basket economics — and it's a structural advantage Zepto/Blinkit can never replicate without owning a daily route.

Step 01
Subscribe to your daily

Milk, idli batter, curd, weekly fresh basket, weekly fruits. Choose your brands and quantities once.

Step 02
Route runs every morning

30-50 households per colony create a captive daily route. Same van, same time, same person.

Step 03
Add anything, free

Open app or WhatsApp before 9pm. Add coconut, paneer, agarbatti, anything ≤ 2kg. Arrives with tomorrow's milk. No delivery fee.

08 · Why this founder

Three perspectives most retail-tech founders never combine.

01

The Counter

15 years inside the family kirana in Hyderabad — from age 7 to 22, side-by-side with my father. Stood behind the counter at 6am. Watched customers stay loyal for decades — not because of prices, but because someone remembered them, extended credit, and asked about their children. The store closed when I moved on; the lessons didn't.

02

The Observation

After the kirana, ~18 years of structured observation across formats and countries — 7-Eleven and FamilyMart in Thailand and Taiwan, Lawson in Japan, quick commerce in Asia, hypermarkets and dark stores. Operationally brilliant, emotionally empty. Nobody knew my name. That's when the gap became obvious.

03

The Code

Ran a mobile recharge business in 2007 — that's how I understand service margins and how small-ticket services create daily footfall. Today: professional product designer and AI product builder. I can design and ship Consto OS myself.

"Most retail-tech founders come from tech and try to digitise kiranas from the outside. I come from inside the kirana — and bring design + AI capability to build the digital layer myself."

09 · Conviction

What's validated. What's still being tested.

Conviction comes from knowing the difference.

Validated
The emotional gap is real — 50+ interviews in Beeramguda
India has zero organized convenience stores — well-documented white space
Quick commerce is structurally weak in suburbs — dark-store math breaks outside dense cores
Service margins are real — ran a mobile recharge business in 2007, lived the unit economics
?
Still being tested
Whether customers respond to proactive AI-backed care from a store
Whether they'll pay 5-10% premium for care over price
Whether one agent can realistically manage 30-100 customers well
Whether hired staff can replicate founder-grade warmth at scale
