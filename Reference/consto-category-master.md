# Consto — Category Master (Everything a store like Consto could sell)

**Status:** Draft v0.2 for Sateesh's review · September 2026
**Purpose:** The complete list of every category a Consto store *could* carry, so we can decide what stays, what gets tested, and what gets dropped. Nothing is decided yet.

---

## How to read this

Every category has one tag. The tags are **my suggestions**. Change any of them.

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ★ **Core** | Fits the convenience-store idea. Stock from day one. |
| ◐ **Test** | Could work. Try 5–20 items in the pilot or first 90 days, keep only what sells weekly. |
| ✕ **Skip** | Supermarket or kirana territory, or low value for the space it takes. Listed only so the list is complete. |
| ⚖ **Licence** | Needs a government licence or has legal rules. Check before stocking. |

**Role** of each department in the store:

- **Traffic** → brings people in, earns little (milk, bread, eggs, services)
- **Profit** → pays the rent (ready food, own-brand, snacks, personal care)
- **Habit** → makes people come daily (chai, breakfast, milk)

**Telangana / Hyderabad-specific items are marked 📍.**

### Two operating rules (decided by Sateesh)

1. **No cooking, no gas.** Every food item arrives ready-made from a vendor or a central kitchen. The store only keeps it hot (hot case), cold (chiller) or on the shelf (packed). Staff may **heat** food with electric equipment only: microwave, sandwich toaster, hot-water dispenser. Same as 7-Eleven: central kitchens cook, stores only heat and serve.
2. **No staples.** Indian families pick a rice, atta, oil or dal brand they like and buy 10, 25 or 50 kg at a time. A 1 kg pack doesn't match how people actually shop, so staples are dropped. Only salt and sugar stay as emergency items.

## 1. Ready-made food (hot case + chiller) — ★ Core

**Nothing is cooked in the store, and there is no gas.** Food arrives from a vendor or a central kitchen, usually twice a day (morning and afternoon), and is sold from:

- a **hot case** (glass warmer that keeps food above 63 °C), or
- a **chiller** (display fridge that keeps food below 5 °C), or
- the **shelf** (sealed packs at room temperature).

Staff can **heat** an item for the customer with electric equipment: a microwave, a sandwich toaster, or a hot-water dispenser. About 30 seconds per item.

**The key question for every item: does it still taste good after 3–4 hours in a hot case or a day in a chiller?** Many Indian breakfast items do not. That decides the list.

**Money note:** because a vendor does the cooking, the vendor keeps part of the margin. Bought-in ready food usually earns the store **25–40%**, not the 50–65% a store earns when it cooks itself. Still the best margin in the store. With its own central kitchen later (Store 3+), Consto can move toward 50%.

**Food safety:** selling ready food needs an FSSAI licence. Label every batch with the time it arrived and throw away anything unsold after its time limit (for example, hot case 4 hours). Log what is thrown away; it is your wastage number.

**1.1 Hot case: holds well** ★
- Idli (in a covered tray) with chutney and sambar in sealed cups
- Upma, pongal, khichdi
- Pulihora (tamarind rice) 📍, lemon rice, tomato rice, bisibele bath
- Veg biryani, egg biryani 📍; chicken biryani 📍 ◐ (non-veg decision)
- Puffs: veg, egg, paneer, chicken ◐ (bakery puffs hold very well)
- Samosa, kachori
- Cutlets, spring rolls ◐
- Boiled eggs, masala eggs (India's version of Taiwan's tea eggs)
- Sweet corn cup ◐
- Haleem during Ramzan 📍 ◐ (from a known local maker)

**1.2 Hot case: does NOT hold well** ✕
These go soft, oily or cold within an hour. Leave them to tiffin centres and street stalls.
- Dosa, pesarattu, uttapam
- Puri, bajji, mirchi bajji, punugulu, pakoda, bonda
- Medu vada ◐ (test: some vendors' vadas hold 2 hours)
- Omelette, egg bhurji

**1.3 Chiller: meals and bowls** ★

**1.4 Chiller: sandwiches, wraps and salads** ★

**1.5 Chiller: desserts and dairy sweets** ★

**1.6 Shelf: bakery (room temperature, 1–5 days)** ★

## 2. Hot drinks (machine, no cooking) — ★ Core

No stove, no chaiwala. A machine makes each cup in seconds, the same way every time.

**2.1 Tea and coffee machine** ★
- Tea and coffee premix machine (lowest cost, 4–6 options: chai, masala chai, ginger chai, coffee, badam milk, lemon tea)
- Bean-to-cup coffee machine ◐ (better taste, higher price; suits the IT-office format)
- Filter coffee decoction machine ◐
- Many machine vendors rent the machine and sell you the premix.

**2.2 Soups (monsoon and winter)** ◐
- Tomato soup, sweet corn soup from the same premix machine

**2.3 Cold drinks (ready-made, in the chiller)** ★
- Buttermilk / majjiga 📍 (summer essential), sweet lassi, mango lassi (packed, from Heritage, Vijaya, Jersey)
- Cold coffee, milkshakes (bottled)
- Jeera masala soda / goli soda 📍
- Fresh juice, fresh lime soda, sugarcane juice, tender coconut ✕ (all need preparation in the store)

**What you give up:** fresh "Irani chai" 📍 feel. **What you gain:** no cook, no gas cylinder, no hygiene risk, same taste every cup, and it works in every future store.

## 8. Staples — ✕ Dropped

**Decision (Sateesh):** no rice, atta, oil or dal. Families buy these in 10–50 kg quantities from a brand they trust, so small packs don't fit how they shop, and bulk bags don't fit a convenience store.

**8.1 Stays (emergency items only)** ★

**8.2 Dropped** ✕

## What 7-Eleven and FamilyMart sell

### The big picture

| | 7-Eleven | FamilyMart |
|---|---|---|
| Home | USA brand, run by Seven & i Holdings (Japan) | Japan (majority-owned by Itochu) |
| Japan | ~21,000 stores | ~16,000 stores |
| Thailand | ~15,000 stores (run by CP All) | Small presence |
| Taiwan | ~7,000 stores (run by Uni-President) | ~4,000 stores |
| Worldwide | ~85,000 stores | ~24,000 stores |
| India | Since Oct 2021, Mumbai, with Reliance Retail | Not present |

Store counts are approximate. **Note for your website:** Japan has about 56,000 convenience stores across *all* chains. The "80,000+" figure is probably 7-Eleven's *worldwide* count.

In Japan, fresh and ready food is roughly a third of a convenience store's sales, and it earns the highest margin. That is the model to learn from.

### 7-Eleven Japan

- **Fresh food (the heart):** onigiri (rice balls), bento boxes, sandwiches, salads, noodles, pasta, desserts. Up to three deliveries a day from dedicated central kitchens.
- **Hot counter:** fried chicken, croquettes, oden (winter stew, simmered at the counter), steamed buns (nikuman)
- **Coffee:** Seven Café, freshly ground per cup, very cheap. A major traffic driver.
- **Own brand:** "Seven Premium", hundreds of products (snacks, ready meals, frozen food, drinks). Quality equal to national brands, better margin.
- **Packaged:** drinks, alcohol, snacks, ice cream, bread, dairy
- **Daily goods:** toiletries, cosmetics, underwear, socks, umbrellas, phone cables, batteries, stationery
- **Tobacco and magazines**
- **Services:** Seven Bank ATMs, utility and tax bill payment, "pay at store" for online shopping, parcel shipping and pickup, concert and travel tickets, a multi-function printer (print, copy, scan, and even government certificates), free washrooms in many stores

### FamilyMart Japan

- **Famichiki:** a fried-chicken piece that became a national favourite. One hero product can define a store brand.
- **Onigiri, bento, sandwiches, desserts**
- **Famima Café:** coffee, frappés, seasonal drinks
- **Own brand:** "Famimaru" for food and snacks
- **Convenience Wear:** FamilyMart's own socks, t-shirts, towels and underwear. A surprise hit.
- **Services:** Famiport kiosk (tickets, bill payment, printing), ATMs, parcel services

### 7-Eleven Thailand (what you saw)

- **Toasties:** ham-and-cheese sandwiches grilled for you at the counter. A national habit.
- **Frozen meals** (CP brand) microwaved by staff, with a spoon and fork
- **Steamed buns (salapao), sausages, dim sum, ready rice dishes**
- **All Café** coffee and a bakery corner
- **Drinks:** energy drinks, iced tea, soy milk, coconut water
- **Basic household medicines** (paracetamol, balms, plasters, in line with Thai rules)
- **Beauty sachets:** face creams, sunscreen, shampoo in single-use packs
- **Services:** Counter Service (pay almost any bill), mobile top-up, parcel, ATMs, open 24 hours

### 7-Eleven and FamilyMart Taiwan (what you saw)

- **Tea eggs:** simmering in a pot at the counter. A cheap, iconic snack.
- **Oden, roasted sweet potatoes, rice balls, bento, steamed buns**
- **City Café (7-Eleven) and Let's Café (FamilyMart):** coffee as a daily ritual
- **ibon (7-Eleven) and FamiPort (FamilyMart) kiosks:** tickets, printing, bill payment, lottery
- **Pay almost everything at the counter:** utility bills, taxes, traffic fines, tuition, credit cards
- **Parcel pickup:** the main way Taiwanese people collect online shopping
- **Seating areas and washrooms:** people sit, eat, charge phones and rest

## What they do **not** sell (and Consto shouldn't either)

- Bulk staples (25 kg rice, monthly grocery lists)
- Full vegetable and fruit range
- Raw meat and fish
- Large appliances, furniture, bulky homeware
- Many brands of the same thing (they keep 1–3 per item)
- Slow sellers: anything that doesn't sell every week gets removed
