## 01 — Problem: Daily Shopping Is Broken. Emotionally Empty.

In growing suburbs like Beeramguda and small towns across India, families lose significant time and mental energy daily. Existing solutions are fragmented and emotionally hollow.

### Traditional Kiranas

- 50% stockout rate on common items
- Limited variety and inconsistent quality
- No digital payments, AEPS, or modern services
- Trust exists but capability doesn't match

### Quick Commerce (Zepto, Blinkit, Instamart)

- 20–30% fresh produce quality failures
- No credit, relationships, or guidance
- Zero memory of who you are or what you need
- Predatory pricing that burns cash

### Supermarkets & Hypermarts (DMart, Reliance Smart, More)

- Far from home — 20-40 min travel each way
- Completely impersonal, zero relationship
- Staff rotates constantly, nobody knows you
- No services (AEPS, bills, recharges, guidance)
- Designed for extraction, not connection

### The Daily Result

- 3–4 apps/shops for basic daily needs
- Mental load of remembering everything
- No trusted advisor for life needs
- Zero proactive help from anyone

### The Core Emotional Gap

> Nobody "owns" the customer relationship — not kiranas, not apps, not supermarkets. DMart knows your bill amount but not your name. Zepto knows your address but not your allergies. The kirana knows your face but can't stock what you need.

## 16 — Brand Identity

### Tone of Voice
Caring older sibling — warm, respectful, never pushy. Telugu + English.

**Good**: "Priya ji, Ugadi coming — want me to keep pachadi ingredients? Fresh bundle ₹120."
**Bad**: "Dear Customer, Ugadi SALE! 20% OFF! LIMITED STOCK! ORDER NOW!"

**Rules**: Always use name. Never ALL CAPS. No "Dear Customer." Telugu phrases: "Baagunnara?" before pitch, "Dhanyavaadalu" after purchase.

### Brand Principles
1. Care first, sell second
2. Remember always — forgetting is a brand failure
3. Positivity, not spirituality — all backgrounds welcome
4. Honest by default — yellow tag near-expiry, skip bad products
5. Store is the neighbourhood's living room

### What Consto Is NOT
Not a discount store. Not a tech startup (tech is invisible). Not rigidly uniform (30% local soul). Not a wellness brand. Not religious.

## 21 — AI Architecture: Where Haiku, Where Sonnet

### The Rule

**Haiku** = Speed tasks. Fast math, quick lookups, real-time responses at checkout, simple alerts.
**Sonnet** = Thinking tasks. Customer understanding, message personalisation, demand analysis, strategic insights.

| Product | AI Model | Why |
|---------|----------|-----|
| P1 POS | **Haiku** | Checkout speed. Can't wait 3 sec for a suggestion. |
| P2 Agent CRM | **Sonnet** | Understanding customer context needs deeper reasoning |
| P3 Inventory | **Haiku** | Stock math is fast calculation, not deep thinking |
| P4 Notify | **Sonnet** | Message personalisation needs emotional intelligence |
| P5 Loyalty | **Haiku** | Points and tier math is simple |
| P6 Ops | **Haiku** | Checklist flagging is pattern matching |
| P7 Predict | **Sonnet** | Demand forecasting needs analytical depth |
| P8 HQ | **Sonnet** | Cross-product reasoning and natural language queries |

### How They Share Data

```
All 8 products share:
├── Supabase (single database + auth + real-time sync)
├── Claude API (Haiku for speed, Sonnet for reasoning)
├── WhatsApp Cloud API (all customer communication)
└── Single auth (one login across all products)
```

### Decision Framework

| Decision Type | Who Decides | Example |
|---------------|-------------|---------|
| **AI acts alone** | AI (Haiku) | Stock alert, receipt generation, points calculation |
| **AI suggests, you approve** | AI (Sonnet) → You tap Yes/No | Reorder quantities, promo messages, credit adjustments |
| **You decide, AI gives data** | You (AI provides context) | New vendor, complaints, hiring, layout, capital spend |
