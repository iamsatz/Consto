# Consto — Portfolio Case Study

**Concept Design · Product Strategy · AI-Driven Retail**

---

## Overview

Consto is a concept for India's first Indianised convenience store — a neighbourhood hub where every customer has a personal AI-backed agent who remembers them, anticipates needs, and solves daily life beyond just shopping.

**My Role**: Founder · Product Designer · Brand Designer · AI Product Architect

**Timeline**: 2026 (ongoing)

**Scope**: End-to-end — research, strategy, personas, service design, store layout, digital product suite, brand identity, business modelling

---

## The Origin Story

I grew up running a kirana store with my father in Hyderabad. I've stood behind the counter, watched how customers stayed loyal for years — not because of prices, but because someone remembered their name, gave credit when times were hard, and asked about their family.

Later, I travelled to Thailand and Taiwan. I walked into 7-Elevens and FamilyMarts — efficient, clean, multi-service. But nobody knew me. Nobody cared.

That gap became Consto: **What if a convenience store had the warmth of my father's kirana, the efficiency of a FamilyMart, and AI that makes it all scale?**

---

## The Problem I'm Solving

In Indian suburbs and Tier 2-3 towns, daily shopping is broken across every option:

**Traditional Kiranas** — Trust exists, but 50% stockouts, limited variety, no modern services. The warmth is real but the capability is outdated.

**Quick Commerce** — 10-minute delivery, but faceless. 20-30% fresh quality failures. Zero memory of who you are. Speed without soul.

**Supermarkets** — Variety and price, but 20-40 minutes away. Completely impersonal. Staff rotates. Designed for transactions, not relationships.

**The core gap**: Nobody owns the customer relationship. Nobody remembers them, anticipates their needs, or genuinely cares beyond the transaction.

## Research & Discovery

### What I Did

- 50+ in-person interviews with families, gig drivers, retirees, and professionals in Beeramguda, Hyderabad
- Observational research at 7-Eleven (Thailand), FamilyMart (Taiwan), DMart, and local kiranas
- Competitive landscape mapping across traditional retail, quick commerce, modern retail, and kirana-tech startups
- Market sizing and white space analysis for Indianised convenience stores

### Key Findings

- **400M+ consumers** in Tier 2-3 have smartphones and UPI but still depend on outdated kiranas
- India has **zero organised convenience store chains** (vs 80K+ in Japan)
- Quick commerce is structurally weak in suburbs — coverage gaps are our opportunity
- **88% of Indian retail** is still unorganised (12M kiranas)
- Kirana modernisation studies show **135% revenue growth** when tech and services are added
- The emotional need to "be known by name" is universal and unserved at scale

## Design Process

### 1. Persona Development

I created four research-backed personas representing the primary customer segments:

**Priya (Working Mom, 35)** — Time-poor, quality-anxious, needs someone who knows her family's needs. Represents 40% of target users.

**Raju (Gig Driver, 28)** — Needs cash in 90 seconds, a charged phone, and a snack. No time for anything slow. 25% of users.

**Lakshmi Aunty (Retiree, 55+)** — App-overwhelmed, values dignity and personal service. Needs credit flexibility and someone who respects her rituals. 20% of users.

**Arjun (Tech Professional, 30)** — Wants premium organic quality with 3-minute efficiency. Won't tolerate cluttered kirana aesthetics. 15% of users.

### 2. User Journey Mapping

I mapped a full week for each persona — from first WhatsApp message to becoming a brand evangelist. Key design moments:

- **Priya**: Agent messages "Milk running low — reserved for you?" before she realises → she tells her family WhatsApp group about the store
- **Raju**: Rests on the chair outside with chai, buys nothing, nobody pressures him → tells 3 other drivers
- **Lakshmi Aunty**: Pension delayed, agent says "Take what you need, pay when it comes" → tells the entire colony
- **Arjun**: Pre-packed organic order ready when he arrives, in and out in 3 minutes → cancels BigBasket subscription

### 3. Service Blueprint

Mapped the complete service ecosystem:

**Frontstage**: Personal agent greeting → zoned store layout → multi-service counter → community wall → warm goodbye

**Backstage**: AI-powered customer memory → demand forecasting → proactive notifications → preference tracking → daily operations system

**Support**: Shared database across all products → AI models for speed tasks and thinking tasks → WhatsApp as primary communication layer

### 4. Store Layout Design

Designed a 600-800 sq ft space with three intentional zones:

**Front — Magnet Zone (~150 sq ft)**: Community notice board, festival calendar, powerbank rental, chai counter, "Come rest" poster. Pulls people in even when they don't need to buy.

**Middle — Shop (~350 sq ft)**: Daily groceries, OTC medicines, phone accessories, gifts. Agent desk visible — not hidden in the back.

**Back — Services (~150 sq ft)**: AEPS cash counter, bill payments, xerox/print station, pickup shelf.

Design principle: **Not a kirana's clutter. Not DMart's sterility. A well-organised living room.**

## Digital Product Design: Consto OS

The most ambitious part of this project — designing an entire product ecosystem from scratch. 8 integrated AI-powered products that form the operating system for the store.

### The Product Suite

**Consto POS** — Billing & checkout with AI-powered product suggestions and customer recognition at the counter.

**Consto Agent** — The core product. A WhatsApp CRM dashboard where agents see each customer's full profile — preferences, family, festivals, allergies — and get AI-drafted personalised messages.

**Consto Inventory** — Smart stock management that predicts what to reorder, flags expiring items for dynamic pricing, and tracks waste daily.

**Consto Notify** — A visual rule engine for customer notifications. "If customer hasn't visited in 7 days → send care check-in." Festival reminders, restock alerts, win-back campaigns.

**Consto Loyalty** — Points, tier progression (Regular → Gold → Family), referral tracking, community event RSVPs, and a credit ledger designed with dignity.

**Consto Ops** — Digital daily checklists, shift handovers, incident logging, and quality tracking. The franchise playbook in app form.

**Consto Predict** — AI demand forecasting at SKU level using sales history, festival calendar, and weather patterns.

**Consto HQ** — Owner's single dashboard. Natural language queries ("How did milk sales do this week?"), weekly reports, multi-store view for franchise scaling.

### Design Decisions

**AI split**: Speed tasks (billing, stock math, checklist flags) use a fast AI model. Thinking tasks (message personalisation, demand forecasting, cross-product insights) use a deeper reasoning model. Each product uses the right model for its job.

**WhatsApp-first**: The owner and staff interact with AI through WhatsApp — not a complex dashboard. This was a deliberate choice because Indian retail workers are WhatsApp-native, not enterprise-software-native.

**Progressive complexity**: Products are designed to be deployed in 4 phases over 8 months. Phase 1 (billing + CRM) works standalone. Each phase adds capability without breaking what came before.

**Shared data layer**: All 8 products read from and write to one database. A sale in POS instantly updates inventory, triggers notifications, adjusts predictions, and updates the owner dashboard. No silos.

## Brand Identity

### Core Principles

1. **Care first, sell second** — help even if they don't buy
2. **Remember always** — forgetting a preference is a brand failure
3. **Positivity, not spirituality** — warm energy, all backgrounds welcome
4. **Honest by default** — yellow tag near-expiry, skip bad products aloud
5. **Neighbourhood's living room** — "this place is yours, not ours"

### Tone of Voice

Caring older sibling — warm, respectful, never pushy. Telugu + English depending on customer.

**Good**: "Priya ji, Ugadi coming — want me to keep pachadi ingredients? Fresh bundle ₹120."

**Bad**: "Dear Customer, Ugadi SALE! 20% OFF! LIMITED STOCK! ORDER NOW!"

### Unique Policies as Brand Expression

- "Come rest even if you don't buy" — a poster on the door
- Waste-to-Wealth soup — free EOD soup from near-expiry vegetables
- Credit with dignity — no embarrassment, no interest
- Warm goodbye to every customer — never just silence

Each policy is designed to be worth talking about. The marketing budget is the customer's mouth.

## Business Thinking

### Why This Works Financially

Traditional kiranas average ₹150 basket size at 10-15% margins. Consto targets ₹250-400 baskets through trust-based recommendations and service bundling (bill payment while buying groceries). The 40% non-grocery revenue (AEPS, bills, recharges) at 50-60% margins transforms the P&L from surviving on thin grocery margins to a diversified community platform.

### Scaling Model

Store 1 proves unit economics → FOCO franchise model (franchisee owns + operates, Consto provides brand + tech + training) → Consto OS offered as standalone SaaS to independent kiranas.

The store proves the products. The products become the scalable business.
