## Key Question 1 — What is Seven & i Holdings' main goal after the 2024–2025 restructuring, and what happened with Couche-Tard?

### Takeaway
Seven & i's stated goal is now a single-minded one: become a pure-play global convenience-store company, exit everything else (superstores, Seven Bank, specialty retail), IPO the North American arm, and return ~¥2 trillion to shareholders by FY2030. Couche-Tard's ¥2,600/share (~US$47bn) bid was withdrawn on 16 July 2025, leaving the standalone plan in force under the first foreign CEO, Stephen Dacus.

### Cited Findings — Seven & i Holdings (group)
- 6 Mar 2025 plan: appoint Stephen Hayes Dacus (lead independent director, fluent Japanese speaker) as President & CEO after the May 2025 AGM; pursue an IPO of 7-Eleven Inc (SEI, North America) "by the second half of 2026" on a major US exchange while retaining a majority; sell the Superstore Business Group (York Holdings: Ito-Yokado, York-Benimaru, Denny's Japan, etc.) to Bain Capital for ¥814.7bn (US$5.37bn) while rolling over 35% equity; return "approximately JPY 2 trillion" via buybacks by FY2030; progressive dividend; exit Seven Bank by selling below 40% and deconsolidating; "continue to pursue its Convenience store business performance acceleration plan and execute disciplined M&A." — [Seven & i release, 6 Mar 2025 (PDF)](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_material_/localhost/en/release_pdf/2025_0306_ir01en.pdf)
- The same release says the Ito-Kogyo (founding family) MBO proposal was withdrawn 28 Feb 2025, and that the "consistent threshold issue" with Couche-Tard was US antitrust, requiring a divestiture package "unprecedented in scope and size." — [Seven & i release, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_material_/localhost/en/release_pdf/2025_0306_ir01en.pdf)
- Couche-Tard withdrew its proposal on 16 July 2025 (offer ¥2,600/share cash, 47.6% premium to the unaffected price), citing "lack of constructive engagement" and "a calculated campaign of obfuscation and delay", especially limited information on SEI. — [Couche-Tard press release, 16 Jul 2025](https://corporate.couche-tard.com/2025-07-16-ALIMENTATION-COUCHE-TARD-ANNOUNCES-WITHDRAWAL-OF-PROPOSAL-TO-ACQUIRE-SEVEN-I-HOLDINGS-DUE-TO-LACK-OF-ENGAGEMENT); Seven & i shares fell ~7% the next day; the bid was valued at ~US$47bn — [CNBC, 17 Jul 2025](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/17/trading-in-japans-seven-i-halted-after-couche-tard-withdraws-47-billion-bid-to-acquire-the-retailer.html)
- Seven & i's response: it "remained fully committed to our standalone value creation plan... including the sale of its superstore business and the North American IPO." — [CStore Dive, Jul 2025](https://www.cstoredive.com/news/alimentation-couche-tard-ends-takeover-bid-seven-and-i/753301/)
- York Holdings sale to Bain closed 1 Sep 2025; Seven & i retains ~40% (Wikipedia says 40%, the March release said 35% roll-over — treat as "35–40%"). Dacus became CEO May 2025, "the first foreigner" to hold the post. The October-2024 restructuring announced an intended rename to "7-Eleven Corp"; Wikipedia does not confirm the rename has taken effect. — [Wikipedia: Seven & i Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_%26_I_Holdings)
- Group FY2030 ambition (as reported): convenience-store revenue to ¥11.3 trillion by FY2030, "up more than ¥1 trillion from 2024." — [Mass Market Retailers, 2025](https://massmarketretailers.com/7-eleven-parent-plans-1-300-new-u-s-stores-eyes-2026-north-america-ipo/)
- Q2 FY2025 (Oct 2025) slides: revised FY2025 net income guidance ¥265.0bn (+153% YoY, inflated by the York sale gain); SEJ operating-income forecast ¥215.0bn; buyback 58.8% complete; SEI IPO "planned for second half of 2026"; an "EBITDA target: 400 billion yen" for FY2030 appears in the slide summary (context unclear — may be SEI or a segment). — [Investing.com summary of Seven & i Q2 FY2025 slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/seven--i-q2-2025-slides-net-income-surges-233-amid-transformation-efforts-93CH-4326644)
- Global scale: 86,869 7-Eleven stores at end-Dec 2025 (Japan 21,857; North America 14,891; APAC 49,758; Europe 363), "approx. 60M customers per day globally"; the deck charts "Competitor A ~29,000" and "Competitor B ~17,000" stores; the 7-Eleven brand turns 100 in 2027. — [7-Eleven International IR Day, 23 Apr 2026 (PDF)](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)
- 7-Eleven International's stated global goal: "Maintain Position as Market Leader", "Enter new markets while accelerating business growth in existing markets", "Develop Europe into the fourth pillar following Japan, North America, and Asia-Pacific"; a 30-metric screen (S&P risk index + growth + maturity) picks "Top Priority Markets (2026): Europe 9 countries, Other regions 2 countries", then selects a local partner on "compatibility with 7-Eleven know-how." Core competencies named: "Excellent Store Operations / Differentiated Product Offering / Profitable Store Expansion." — [7-Eleven International IR Day, Apr 2026](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)
- 7-Eleven Australia (acquired 2024) case in the same deck: items per store 1,700 (2023) → 2,600 (2025); sushi & onigiri ADS/store +70% (2024→2025); loyalty-app scan rate 20.1% → 25.7%; 7NOW deliveries per store per day 7.1 → 19.3 in two years; FY25 merchandise sales +0.2% vs competitor average −8.9%; targets 1,000+ stores and A$380m+ EBITDA by 2030 under the slogan "My Convenient Neighborhood Store." — [7-Eleven International IR Day, Apr 2026](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)

### Inferences
- The "main goal" has shifted from Japanese-conglomerate diversification to a shareholder-value, CVS-only thesis forced by activist pressure (ValueAct) and the Couche-Tard bid. Everything is now measured against "does it raise CVS EBITDA and buyback capacity by FY2030."
- The 100-year-old brand's global playbook is explicitly know-how transfer (fresh-food counters, Japanese products, loyalty app, delivery) into partner-run markets — the Australian slide is the template they want to repeat.

- Could not confirm whether the SEI IPO completed in 2H 2026.

## Key Question 2 — Seven-Eleven Japan (SEJ): goals, how it makes money (franchise economics), KPIs, and criticisms

### Takeaway
SEJ's business model is a gross-profit-sharing franchise: HQ takes a "Seven-Eleven Charge" of 43% of store gross profit under Type A (franchisee owns the premises) or a sliding scale (higher, generally cited as 56–76%) under Type C (HQ provides premises), in exchange for product development, logistics, POS/ordering, counselling, accounting, 80% of utilities and a minimum gross-profit guarantee. SEJ wins on the single KPI it has obsessed over for 50 years — average daily sales per store, ~¥699k in FY2025 vs ~¥585–598k for rivals — but it is now fighting declining customer counts, a saturated 56,000-store market, and a "high price" brand perception.

### Cited Findings — SEJ franchise economics (how money is made)
- SEJ describes its aim as "modernizing and revitalizing small- and medium-sized retail stores through the franchise system"; income is allocated by "a gross profit-sharing method... Seven-Eleven Japan and its franchisees work together on improving gross profits instead of net sales." — [Seven & i IR, SEJ Franchise System (PDF, FY2006 data)](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_template_/_res/en/ir/library/co/pdf/p24_33.pdf)
- Role split (same source): HQ — build product development and delivery system; provide POS ordering system; provide merchandise information; provide consulting service (OFC field counsellors); cover electronic data-processing cost; accounting service; promotion cost; install sales equipment; cover 80% of utility cost; audit service. Franchisee — manage store operations, recruit employees, order products, clean stores, customer service, cover 20% of utility cost. — [SEJ Franchise System PDF](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_template_/_res/en/ir/library/co/pdf/p24_33.pdf)
- Contract types (same source):

| Term | Type A | Type C |
|---|---|---|
| Land & buildings | Franchisee provides | SEJ provides |
| Display cases, fridges, computers | SEJ provides | SEJ provides |
| Contract period | 15 years | 15 years |
| Utilities | SEJ 80% / franchisee 20% | SEJ 80% / franchisee 20% |
| Seven-Eleven Charge (royalty) | 43% of gross profit | Sliding scale on gross profit |
| Incentives | 5-year and 15-year renewal fee reductions | same |
| Minimum guarantee (annual gross profit after charge) | ¥19 million | ¥17 million |
| Note | Applies to 24-hour stores; gross profit = net sales − net COGS (after inventory loss, bad merchandise, rebates) | same |

  — [SEJ Franchise System PDF](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_template_/_res/en/ir/library/co/pdf/p24_33.pdf)
- "In the majority of franchise agreements in Japan, Seven & i arranges the real estate and the store construction and owners return between 56 per cent and 76 per cent of their profits to it as royalties." — [Globe and Mail (2024–25 coverage of franchisee discontent)](https://arc-dev.theglobeandmail.com/business/international-business/article-some-7-eleven-owners-in-japan-fed-up-with-strategy-welcome-foreign-bid)
- Historical benchmark: in 1994, 45% of store gross profit went to SEJ. — [Kellogg case study (PDF)](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/EMP/intranet/preenroll/Seven-Eleven_Final_0205.pdf)
- SEJ merchandise gross-profit margin: 31.8% in Q2 FY2025 (−0.3 pt YoY); operating income ¥121.4bn in the period vs ¥127.6bn prior year; existing-store sales +0.8%, customer traffic +0.5% in Q2; "just-made" counter merchandise APSD +5.5%. — [Investing.com, Seven & i Q2 FY2025 slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/seven--i-q2-2025-slides-net-income-surges-233-amid-transformation-efforts-93CH-4326644)
- SEJ's own claim: it "continues to exceed major competitors' APSD by over 20%" (FY2024 all-store average, two major chains). — [SEJ IR Day Autumn 2025 deck (PDF)](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2025_1031kse_04.pdf)

### Cited Findings — SEJ goals and 2025–2030 initiatives (from IR Day, 31 Oct 2025)
- Diagnosed problems: "Aging population with declining [customers]", "Conservative promotions and defense due to cost-push inflation", "High merchandise prices", consumer perception of the brand, "Intensifying competition". Stated fix: "Reestablish SEJ as the leader in innovation", "Enhance customer engagement", revisit "the value of 7-Eleven with franchisees and staff, and declare it to customers", a new-concept TV campaign ("What's in store at 7-Eleven?"), and targeting younger customers via marketers/creators. — [SEJ IR Day Autumn 2025](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2025_1031kse_04.pdf)
- Fresh-made counters: SEVEN CAFÉ in c.4,000 stores (+0.2% daily-sales uplift, +0.1 pt GPM on total-store basis); "just-made" programmes (Japanese sweets, bento lunchboxes, fresh bakery, soft-serve) rolling from c.8,000 stores by FY2025-end to c.18,000 by FY2026-end for one line and c.2,100 → c.10,100 stores for another (+0.9% daily sales, +0.3 pt GPM on installed-store basis). — [SEJ IR Day Autumn 2025](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2025_1031kse_04.pdf)
- Store network: "Accelerate store openings... through expansion of various store formats tailored to each commercial area" — Standard, Small, Unit, Satellite formats, including depopulated areas with self-service laundry; plan of ~1,000 stores (2025–2030) and net increase of ~1,000 stores; a multi-store ownership contract ("Contract model that makes it easy to manage multiple stores") to start FY2027 to "improve franchisees' profits and promote new franchisees." — [SEJ IR Day Autumn 2025](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2025_1031kse_04.pdf)
- 7NOW (Japan delivery): "Achieve 120 billion yen in 7NOW sales by FY2030 (with a 2–3% uplift effect)"; seamless ordering integrated with 7-Eleven apps, in-store pickup from FY2026, bundles. — [SEJ IR Day Autumn 2025](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2025_1031kse_04.pdf)
- Summary KPI table in the deck (SEJ column): "APSD CAGR at 2.0%", "Net increase of approx. 1,000 stores", "Approx. ¥120bn in [7NOW] sales", "Enhance capability in 5,000+ stores". (SEI column: "Merch APSD CAGR at 2.4%", "PB sales growth CAGR 6.5%", "OSG&A% < 12%", "Add 1,300 new stores", "Add 1,100 restaurants", "Add 200 [7NOW] stores/year; $1.25B delivery sales".) — [SEJ IR Day Autumn 2025](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2025_1031kse_04.pdf)
- Value/price moves: SEJ ran a ¥100 onigiri campaign — [Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/blogs/news/world/40050755); SEJ began discounting unsold food — [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/business-trends/japan-s-seven-eleven-to-start-discounting-unsold-food); discount chains attack with "$2 tonkatsu" — [Bloomberg, 7 Nov 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-07/discount-chain-takes-aim-at-7-eleven-in-japan-with-2-tonkatsu).

### Cited Findings — Japan market KPIs and criticisms
- Average daily sales per store, FY2025: Seven-Eleven ¥699,000; Lawson ¥598,000; FamilyMart ¥585,000; YoY gains ¥7k, ¥24k, ¥12k respectively. (search summary; page not verified — appears in results from [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/japan-convenience-stores-hit-record-sales-face-declining-foot-traffic-and) / [RealGaijin](https://realgaijin.substack.com/p/japans-convenience-store-exodus-why))
- 2025 industry: transactions at the seven major chains fell 0.2% to 16.34bn; average spend +2.5% to ¥737.9. (search summary; [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/japan-convenience-stores-hit-record-sales-face-declining-foot-traffic-and))
- Industry sales 2025 ¥13 trillion (record); 56,659 stores (record); food >60% of sales; customers aged 50+ now 38% of patrons vs 22% in 2003; traffic down for multiple consecutive months; author's thesis: "sales growth does not equate to demand growth. It is inflation passing through the system." — [RealGaijin, 27 Mar 2026](https://realgaijin.substack.com/p/japans-convenience-store-paradox)
- Market size ¥13.5tn (US$86.7bn) in 2025; Seven-Eleven >21,500 stores, FamilyMart ~16,000, Lawson ~14,000; big three ≈90% of stores. (search summary; [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1264889/seven-eleven-store-number-japan/), [Piece of Japan](https://piece-of-japan.com/investing/convenience-store/market-share.html))
- Franchisee disputes: SEJ moved to terminate the Higashi-Osaka franchisee who cut 24-hour opening (2019) — [Japan Today](https://japantoday.com/category/business/seven-eleven-to-end-contract-with-franchisee-who-cut-opening-hours); "Operating 24/7... has become an unbearable burden for many store owners" — [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/in-depth/d00649/); franchisee profits "fall for second straight year", forcing labour-saving push — [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/SNKopJ4BrX5PFN7BvC5V); Japanese franchisees worried about food-waste losses from the fresh/hot-food push since they bear disposal cost — [CSP Daily News](https://cspdailynews.com/company-news/us-7-eleven-franchisees-meet-japanese-counterparts)
- Drugstores as the new competitor: Welcia and Tsuruha (both Aeon-linked) merged in Dec 2025 into Japan's largest drugstore group, ~5,500 stores, >¥2tn sales, targeting ¥3tn sales and ¥210bn operating profit by FY2032. — [Jiho](https://pj.jiho.jp/article/252856), [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/business-deals/japanese-drugstores-speed-up-merger-in-bid-to-be-asian-leader)

### Inferences
- SEJ's royalty is on *gross profit*, not sales, which aligns HQ and franchisee on margin mix (fresh food, private label) rather than volume — and explains why HQ pours money into product development and logistics it "gives away" to stores.
- The 43% / 56–76% split only works because HQ carries land, fixtures, 80% of utilities, IT and a guaranteed minimum; a founder copying the royalty rate without the services would simply be charging rent.
- The 2025 agenda (value pricing, fresh-made counters, small formats, multi-store franchisees, delivery) is a defensive response to a shrinking, ageing customer base — not an offensive one.

## Key Question 3 — 7-Eleven Inc (US/Canada): Speedway integration, fresh food, 7NOW

### Takeaway
SEI's plan ("Transformation of 7-Eleven") is to fix the US "consumer perception re: food" problem by building 1,300 large, food-forward new stores and 1,100 in-store restaurants by 2030, pushing proprietary/private-brand products, scaling 7NOW to >50% US population coverage and $1.25bn delivery sales, and cutting OSG&A below 12% — while closing weak legacy stores.

### Cited Findings — 7-Eleven Inc
- Store counts: 7-Eleven Inc 12,414 US stores (NACS Top 100, Mar 2025) — [NACS Magazine](https://www.nacsmagazine.com/Issues/March-2025/Top-100-Convenience-Retailers-of-2025); 12,700 per CSP's 2025 ranking — [CSP Top 202](https://www.cspdailynews.com/top-202-convenience-stores-2025); North America (incl. Canada/Mexico licensees) 14,891 at Dec 2025 — [7-Eleven International IR Day](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)
- Strategy frames three shifts: "more value-conscious consumers, rising demand for fresh, high-quality food, and growth in digital ordering and delivery"; 7NOW to add ~200 stores/year through FY2030 to cover >50% of US population; 7NOW digital sales reached $979m in FY2025. — [CSP Daily News](https://www.cspdailynews.com/company-news/seven-i-plans-7now-expansion-convenience-trends-shift)
- Speedway/Stripes upgrades: hot-food cases to 1,600 Speedway locations, in-store bakery to 660, commissary food to 550 by year-end. — [CStore Dive](https://www.cstoredive.com/news/7-eleven-outlines-upgrades-for-speedway-stripes-stores/704697/)
- ~1,300 new large-format food-focused stores by 2030; 1,100 restaurants by 2030. — [CSP Daily News](https://www.cspdailynews.com/company-news/transformation-7-eleven-includes-opening-1300-new-us-convenience-stores), [Restaurant Business](https://www.restaurantbusinessonline.com/operations/7-eleven-plans-open-1100-new-restaurants-2030)
- 7NOW economics (FY2024): $725m sales, +38% YoY, same-store 7NOW sales +24%, AOV ~$16 ≈1.7× in-store basket, ~28-minute average delivery, 40+ cities, Gold Pass $5.95/month; 25% of 7NOW top sellers are fresh food/proprietary beverages; FY2025 target $1bn. — [CStore Dive](https://www.cstoredive.com/news/7-eleven-1-billion-7now-sales-2025/737088/)
- Q2 FY2025: SEI merchandise same-store sales −0.9%; merchandise GPM 33.2% (+0.2 pt); operating income $905m vs $858m; 50 new restaurants planned; 200+ new private-brand items. — [Investing.com, Seven & i Q2 FY2025](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/seven--i-q2-2025-slides-net-income-surges-233-amid-transformation-efforts-93CH-4326644)
- SEI's key challenges as listed by the group: "Consumer perception re: food", "Fuel demand", "Franchisee profitability", "Channel shift", "Cost inflation"; fixes: "Expand proprietary products and Private Brand", "Maximize fuel vertical integration". — [7-Eleven International IR Day](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)
- SEI is "closing hundreds of stores in US, Canada, Mexico in focus on food sales" (2024). — [CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/1686124310/7-eleven-to-close-hundreds-of-stores-in-us-canada-mexico-in-focus-on-food-sales)
- "Consumer perception of fresh food remains a key challenge for 7-Eleven." — [RetailWit](https://retailwit.com/7-eleven-fresh-food-forward/)

### Inferences
- The US unit earns a higher merchandise margin (33.2%) than Japan (31.8%) but has weaker like-for-likes and a fuel dependency; the IPO story depends on proving the food/7NOW mix shift.

### Gaps
- Exact number of 2024 US closures (widely reported as 444) was not confirmed from a fetched page.

## Key Question 4 — CP All (7-Eleven Thailand): goals and model

### Takeaway
CP All's goal is relentless density — 700 new 7-Eleven stores every year (15,595 in Q2 2025, ~51% company-owned / 49% franchise + sub-area) — backed by the CP Group food supply chain, with the store repositioned as a ready-to-eat food and O2O service hub (food 76% of sales, O2O 11%, All Member 20M) and gross margin ~28%.

### Cited Findings — CP All
- Q2 2025: 15,595 stores in Thailand (7,636 corporate; 7,959 partner/sub-area); +165 stores in Q2, +350 in 1H25; same-store sales −0.8% (Q1 was +3.0%); average daily sales per store ฿85,690; food share 76.4% (73.7% in 2021); O2O 11% of sales; gross margin 27.8%; consolidated revenue ฿256.6bn (+3.4%), net profit ฿6.77bn (+8.5%); 2025 guidance: 700 new Thai stores, >10 Cambodia, <10 Laos, capex ฿12.0–13.6bn, renovation ฿2.9–3.5bn; CP Axtra (Makro + Lotus's) contributed 66% of 1H25 pre-tax earnings vs 24% from CP All's own CVS despite equal revenue shares. — [Investing.com, CP All Q2 2025 slides](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/cp-all-q2-2025-slides-strong-profit-growth-despite-negative-samestore-sales-93CH-4196691)
- FY2024 average daily sales ฿83,906/store; All Member app ~20 million members; 7App ~10% of sales, 7-Delivery ~11%. (search summary; sources listed: [CP All FY2024 MD&A PDF](https://www.cpall.co.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/MDA-EN-FY2024-V2.pdf) — fetch blocked 403, [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/thailandnews/cp-all-profit-climbs-11-as-7-eleven-drives-growth-with-700-more-stores-planned-561387))
- As of 13 May 2025: 15,430 stores, 51% company-owned, 49% franchise and sub-area licence; strategy "developing ready-to-eat product categories and offers comprehensive services, alongside expanding into O2O via the 7App"; international 138 stores (112 Cambodia, 26 Laos) by 2025; 2025 year-end ~15,945 stores (figure from a fan site — treat as indicative). — [Wikipedia: CP All](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CP_All), [711thaisnacks.com](https://www.711thaisnacks.com/how-many-7-eleven-in-thailand/)
- CP All's annual report brands its culture "24-Hour Goodness DNA". — [CP All 56-1 One Report 2024 (PDF)](https://www.cpall.co.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/CP_ALL_56-1_-OneReport_2024_EN_25MAR-Final.pdf)
- Thailand is the second-largest 7-Eleven market after Japan; CP All "eyes China licence". — [Reuters via CP Malaysia](https://www.cpmalaysia.com/1/update-1-thai-cp-all-plans-7-eleven-expansion-eyes-china-licence/)
- Lotus's go fresh (also CP Group, via CP Axtra) "focus on fresh meats and produce" — "a neatly complementary focus to its main competitor, 7-Eleven, which is more weighted towards ready-to-eat and ready-to-cook foods." — [Inside Retail Asia, 21 May 2026](https://insideretail.asia/2026/05/21/why-are-thailands-retail-giants-facing-diverging-fortunes/)

### Inferences
- CP All's franchise split (~49%) is far lower than Japan's (~98% franchised), and the group's profit now comes mostly from Makro/Lotus's wholesale — the 7-Eleven network's value is as a captive channel for CP's food factories.
- Negative SSS in 2025 while still opening 700 stores/year signals a cannibalisation-for-density strategy: owning the corner matters more than per-store growth.

### Gaps
- Thai franchise fee/royalty terms (the two CP All franchise packages) were not retrieved.
- Exact 2025 year-end store count from a primary source not obtained (MD&A PDF blocked).

## Key Question 5 — President Chain Store Corp (7-ELEVEN Taiwan)

### Takeaway
PCSC's goal is to keep its ~52% share with ~200 openings a year (7,300+ stores by mid-2025), by converting stores into large "combination" formats, automation (ninth unmanned store, AI ordering) and the OPENPOINT loyalty ecosystem (19M+ members tied to Starbucks, Cosmed, fuel) — and by exporting the model to the Philippines, where it owns Philippine Seven Corp.

### Cited Findings — PCSC

## Key Question 8 — What do they say the convenience store is FOR, and what should an Indian founder take from it?

### Takeaway
In Japan the industry's self-description has moved from "close and convenient" to "social infrastructure" (disaster response, ageing society, last-mile services); in Thailand/Taiwan the store is a "community service hub" (bill pay, parcels, coffee, delivery); Lawson's owners call it a "hub of refreshment in every community". The transferable lessons are structural, not cosmetic: share gross profit (not sales) with the operator, carry the fixed costs and the supply chain centrally, measure daily sales per store, and sell food and services the kirana cannot.

### Cited Findings
- SEJ's founding purpose statement: "modernizing and revitalizing small- and medium-sized retail stores through the franchise system" with HQ and franchisee "on an equal footing with clear role assignments." — [SEJ Franchise System PDF](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_template_/_res/en/ir/library/co/pdf/p24_33.pdf)
- 7-Eleven's three competencies: "When you want" (store operations), "What you want" (differentiated products), "At a convenient location" (profitable store expansion). — [7-Eleven International IR Day 2026](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)
- 7-Eleven Australia's 2030 slogan: "My Convenient Neighborhood Store — Making everyday life more convenient... Becoming a destination store that customers visit for convenient, high-quality food and beverages." — [7-Eleven International IR Day 2026](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/file/library/ks/pdf/2026_0423kse_04.pdf)
- Lawson/KDDI/Mitsubishi: enhance "convenience stores' roles as 'Hub of refreshment in every community' by solving social issues that stores and communities face" (labour shortage, food loss). — [Mitsubishi Corp release](https://www.mitsubishicorp.com/jp/en/pr/archive/2024/html/0000054507.html)
- Taiwan's stores are praised for "Service Is Our Second Name" — bill payment, parcels, ticketing, printing, coffee. — [Taiwan Panorama](https://www.taiwan-panorama.com/en/Articles/Details?Guid=0ba0c61c-46eb-46af-b3f6-7d37a547c39d&CatId=9)

### Inferences — lessons for an Indian (Beeramguda) founder
1. Royalty on gross profit, not sales: SEJ's 43% (Type A) buys the operator product development, logistics, POS/ordering, accounting, counselling, 80% of utilities and a ¥17–19M floor. If Consto ever franchises, price the HQ take against what HQ actually carries.
2. One KPI: average daily sales per store (SEJ ¥699k; CP All ฿85,690; 7-Eleven Australia tracks merchandise ADS ex-tobacco). Everything in the Japanese IR decks is expressed as "daily-sales uplift / GPM uplift per store."
3. Food is the moat — SEJ >60% of sales from food, CP All 76.4% — but it is also the franchisee's biggest risk (waste, labour). The 2025 Japanese answer is fresh-made-in-store counters (coffee, bakery, bento, sweets) with measured +0.2–0.9% ADS uplift.
4. Services beat SKUs in emerging markets: OXXO, Alfamart, Taiwan 7-Eleven win on bill-pay, cash-in/out, parcels; the India failures (7-Eleven, 24Seven) had none of this and died against kirana prices and 10-minute delivery.
5. Small, cheap, residential, grocery-heavy formats scale (Indomaret, 99 Speedmart, WinMart+, SuperK, New Shop); premium metro 24h hang-outs do not (7-Eleven Indonesia, 7-Eleven India).
6. Loyalty ID + retail media is the second margin stream (FamilyMart: 40M POS-linked IDs, ¥10bn OP target; CP All 20M All Members; PCSC 19M OPENPOINT; 7-Eleven Australia 25.7% app scan rate).
7. Delivery is additive, not cannibalistic, when run from the store: 7NOW AOV 1.7× in-store basket, 24% same-store delivery growth; SEJ targets ¥120bn with only a 2–3% uplift — i.e., it is a retention tool more than a revenue engine.
