# Persona review: Product / Retail Experience Designer

**Reviewing:** `Reference/Products.md` (6 Oct 2026), with `09-store-space-layout-equipment-capex.md`, `07-food-sourcing-vendor-vs-kitchen.md` and `consto-rulebook.md`.
**Lens:** the customer's journey from the footpath to the till and back home. The store is the product. I judge every recommendation by what a woman in slippers at 6:40am, a scooter rider at 20 km/h, a school-bus mother at 7:45, an auto driver at 3pm and a tired father at 9:15pm actually see, smell, wait for and remember.
**Ground rule for this note:** Sateesh cannot pilot. So there are no "test it" answers below. Every pick is a stated assumption he can overrule in one line.

## 1. Where I disagree with the report

The report is a very good trade study. Its weakness is that it designs the store from the P&L inward, not from the footpath inward. Twelve places where a designer would do something else.

### 1.1 Hero product

> "make A (machine chai + Osmania biscuit) the hero for Store 1 and B (the toastie) the signature item … Test all four for 60 days"

**What I'd do instead:** one hero, decided now: the **₹30 morning plate** (3 idli, ghee, karam podi, sambar in a sealed cup, hot, handed over in 20 seconds), with chai as the "always" item next to it, not the hero. Drop the toastie as signature.

**Why:** a hero has to be the thing the store is *known for*, visible and smellable from the footpath, and tied to what only Consto sells (the podi is our own brand; the plate is the podi's advertisement). Premix machine chai has no theatre and every tea stall in the suburb beats it on taste; it is a habit item, not a reason to cross the road. The toastie is a Bombay café item; the evening snack in a Telugu suburb is samosa, puff, punugulu, mirchi bajji. Worse, a toastie "grilled to order" takes 3–4 minutes of a 2-person crew at the 6pm peak and blocks the hot-case line. The report's own worry about idli ("competes head-on on price") is answered by the experience, not the price: the tiffin bandi is a 5-minute wait, standing, no seat, no sealed cup, no pre-order. Consto's plate is 20 seconds, sealed, hot, same every day, reserved on WhatsApp the night before.

### 1.2 Chai machine

> "premix for Store 1; bean-to-cup in the IT-office format"; "Premix machine chai at ₹10–15 undercuts Irani chai at ₹25"

**What I'd do instead:** a **fresh-brew electric chai bot** (Chai Point-type, milk + decoction, no gas; rent ~₹2,500/month), ₹10 for 90 ml, ₹15 for 150 ml, poured in view of the seats.

**Why:** premix tastes like office chai; the suburb knows the difference in one sip and never comes back for chai. A fresh brew at ₹10 that is *visibly* milk and tea is the one thing a tea stall cannot do cleanly. The report prices the win (50–70% margin) but not the loss (a bad first cup ends the daily habit). Cup cost rises from ₹5 to ₹7; margin is still 40%+.

### 1.3 Hours

> "Open 6am–11pm, log sales by hour"

**What I'd do instead:** **5:30am–10:30pm.**

**Why:** the first customers are milk, walkers and night-shift pharma workers from Patancheru; the kirana opens around 7, so 5:30–7:00 is the one window Consto owns outright and where trust is built (nobody else is open, lights on, chai ready). At the other end, nobody in a family suburb walks in at 10:45pm; the last 30 minutes of an 11pm close is staff cleaning with lights on. Same 17 hours, shifted earlier. Two shifts of 8.5 h.

### 1.4 Milk at the back

> "milk/curd/eggs on the back wall but in a straight line of sight from the door, with bread on the end cap … the milk run is ~8 steps"

**What I'd do instead:** one **express dairy door at the front, beside the till** (milk, curd, eggs, bread end-cap). Cold drinks, juices, paneer, frozen go on the back wall.

**Why:** the "walk them to the back" rule is for a browsing customer. The 6:40am milk customer is in slippers with a scooter idling at the kerb; she wants in-and-out in 30 seconds and the kirana already gives her that over the counter. Eight steps past gondolas is eight steps she will not take twice. Cold drinks are the browse item and can sit at the back; milk is the trust item and sits at the door. The report's own cooler data (20–35% uplift at entry/queue) argues for this, it just applied it to cola instead of milk.

### 1.5 Seating

> "8 ft window bar + 4 stools + one 2-top (2 chairs) = 6 seats"

**What I'd do instead:** a **built-in wall bench, 10 ft, 45 cm high with a back, seats 5–6**, plus 2 stools at the window and one marked priority seat on the bench end. 50 sq ft, not 60–80.

**Why:** elders, pregnant women and mothers with a child do not climb bar stools; auto drivers and delivery riders want to sit back. A bench costs ₹8–10k of carpentry, takes 7 sq ft per seat, hides lockable storage underneath, and cannot be dragged around. Stools at the glass still advertise occupancy to the street. The bench faces the hot case and the chai bot (rulebook RS5) with a 3 ft gap for a stroller.

### 1.6 Washroom

> "Washroom … 4 × 6 ft; exhaust; customer use optional"

**What I'd do instead:** customer washroom is **mandatory, 30 sq ft (5 × 6), western WC, hook, shelf, mirror, sanitary-pad bin, key on a wooden tag at the till**, hourly check sheet on the door that customers can read.

**Why:** a rest corner without a washroom is a bus stop. For women on scooters, delivery riders and elders there is no clean washroom within 2 km of most suburbs; this alone is a reason to visit weekly. "Optional" means it becomes a store cupboard by month 2. The visible check sheet is what Japanese stores do and it is the cheapest trust signal in the building.

### 1.7 Services on the same counter as the till

> "Services counter scope? Default bill pay, recharge, AEPS, courier drop, Xerox — all on the main L-counter, no separate desk"

**What I'd do instead:** same L-counter, but a **second position 4 ft from the till with its own screen and its own queue mark on the floor**, at the end nearest the bench so the AEPS customer sits while the OTP comes.

**Why:** an AEPS withdrawal or an electricity bill is 2–4 minutes with ID, fingerprint and OTP. One such customer at 8:05am behind three chai customers and two milk customers is a lost morning. Two queues, not two counters: the floor mark costs ₹300 of tape.

### 1.8 The 8pm public markdown

> "Fixed window: 8pm, 30–35% off, tagged at pull-by minus 5 h, announced on WhatsApp"

**What I'd do instead:** **no visible markdown sticker in Store 1.** The Agent privately offers the last trays to the 15–20 nearest opted-in regulars ("Lakshmi aunty, 4 puffs left, ₹40 for all four, 15 min?"), and whatever remains goes to the donation partner at 9:30pm.

**Why:** a public 30% sticker at 8pm teaches the suburb to wait for 8pm, makes the 7pm buyer feel cheated, and puts a "stale" label on the brand that killed 7-Eleven India in its reviews. Taiwan can afford a discount culture after 40 years of trust; Store 1 has zero. Private, named, small-batch offers clear the tray without a sticker and give the Agent a daily reason to be useful.

### 1.9 Pooja at the entrance

> "Pooja near entrance [A]: common Indian supermarket practice"

**What I'd do instead:** the first 3 seconds inside the door are **hot case, chai bot, today's board**. Pooja goes mid-store on the right wall with the celebrate shelf.

**Why:** the entrance is the only place where smell, steam and a handwritten board can work together; agarbatti at the door kills the ghee-and-chai smell that is the store's best advertisement. Pooja is a planned purchase; it will be found.

### 1.10 Paid membership on day one

> "₹300 for 6 months, SuperK-style"

**What I'd do instead:** membership is **free and is the WhatsApp number**; the ₹300 card only from month 7 if ever.

**Why:** a store whose wall says "sit, no need to buy" cannot ask for ₹300 at the till on week one. SuperK's uptake is for a grocery discount card; Consto's promise is "we remember you", and a paid tier splits customers into remembered and not-remembered. The Agent *is* the membership.

### 1.11 Item count and facings

> "1,500 at opening, hard cap 2,000"; "Facings per SKU? Default 1 (2 for top-20 movers)"

**What I'd do instead:** **1,100–1,200 items at opening, minimum 2 facings per item**, 3 on the top 50.

**Why:** one facing per item is what a kirana looks like: a wall of different packets. A store that is "known for" something must look full and calm; two facings reads as stocked, one facing reads as cluttered and half-empty by 6pm. Fewer items also means the 2-person crew can keep every facing fronted every hour (rulebook H2).

### 1.12 The toastie's job: evening signature

> "B (the toastie) the signature item it sells alongside"

**What I'd do instead:** the evening signature is the **"saayantram pack": chai + 2 pieces (samosa/puff/egg) for ₹30**, assembled in 10 seconds from the hot case, handed over in a paper tray.

**Why:** the evening customer is in a hurry, usually on a scooter, often buying for two or three people at home. A to-order griller item for one person does not fit that moment; a fixed ₹30 pack in a tray that survives a scooter ride does.

## 3. Decision questions from my lens

Format: question, why, options, my pick, the assumption behind it.

### Entrance and the first 3 seconds

1. **What does a customer see in the first 3 seconds inside the door?** Why: this decides whether the store feels like food or like a shop. Options: [hot case and chai bot / gondola of snacks / pooja and festival shelf]. My pick: hot case and chai bot at the left window, chalk board above (assumption: smell and steam convert walkers better than any shelf; pooja is a planned buy).
2. **Door type?** Why: a sliding glass door with a sensor keeps the AC in but scares older customers; an open door invites. Options: [open doorway with air curtain / manual glass door / sensor sliding door]. My pick: manual glass door propped open 5:30–9:30am and 5–8pm, closed with AC the rest (assumption: an open door at peak doubles walk-ins; an air curtain is ₹15k and loud).
3. **Where do helmets and umbrellas go?** Why: 60% of customers arrive on two-wheelers and carry a helmet through the aisles. Options: [12 hooks by the door / nothing / a shelf under the chalk board]. My pick: 12 hooks at 1.6 m beside the door plus an umbrella bucket in monsoon (assumption: a helmet in hand means no basket; hooks cost ₹1,200).
4. **Is there a step at the door?** Why: a 15 cm step keeps out strollers, wheelchairs and elders. Options: [step / ramp / flush]. My pick: flush or a 1:12 ramp (assumption: the landlord's slab is at road level ±15 cm; the ramp is ₹5k of civil work).

### Hot counter: position and visibility

5. **Hot case at the window or deep inside?** Why: the hot case is the only thing in the store that can advertise itself. Options: [left window, visible from road / beside the till inside / back wall]. My pick: left window, 10 ft run, glass on the street side kept spotless (assumption: a visible steaming case is worth 30–50 extra plates a day; staff-served from the inside face).
6. **Who serves the hot case?** Why: hygiene, speed and the 7-Eleven India "stale" complaint. Options: [staff only with tongs / customer self-serve with tongs / mixed]. My pick: staff only, tongs and gloves, cup sealed by the vendor (assumption: self-serve hot cases in India get touched; staff-served costs 10 seconds per plate).
7. **How is "fresh" shown?** Why: customers need to see time, not be told. Options: [tray tag with load time visible to the customer / only staff-side / none]. My pick: a small clock card on each tray facing the customer, "loaded 7:10, out by 11:10" (assumption: visible clocks turn the 4 h rule from a chore into a brand).
8. **What is on the chalk board?** Why: a handwritten board is the only sign customers read every day. Options: [today's plate and count / prices / nothing]. My pick: "Today: 120 plates. Left: 38. Chai ₹10." updated hourly in Telugu (assumption: scarcity written by hand sells out the tray and shows honesty).

### Chai pickup flow

9. **Chai bot self-serve or staff-poured?** Why: 250 cups a day at 30 seconds each is 2 staff-hours. Options: [self-serve press-and-take / staff-poured / self-serve with UPI at the bot]. My pick: self-serve: press, UPI QR on the bot with sound box, take; staff pour only for seated elders (assumption: 80% of chai customers are repeat and learn it in one visit).
10. **Cup?** Why: paper cups spill on scooters; glasses need washing. Options: [paper 90 ml / paper 150 ml with lid / steel glass for seated]. My pick: paper 90 ml ₹10 and 150 ml with lid ₹15; no glass washing in Store 1 (assumption: lids are ₹0.60 each and stop the scooter spill that ends a habit).
11. **Where is the chai queue?** Why: it must never cross the hot-case or till queue. Options: [bot at the bench end / bot at the till / bot by the door]. My pick: bot at the far end of the hot counter, by the bench, with its own floor mark (assumption: three short lines beat one long one at 8am).
12. **Sugar and masala?** Why: everyone wants it differently. Options: [fixed sweet / sugar sachets and a masala shaker / two bot buttons]. My pick: bot buttons for "regular" and "less sugar", ginger and masala as ₹5 add-ons from a shaker on the counter (assumption: "less sugar" is now 30% of orders in any Indian suburb).

### Queue and checkout speed

13. **Target time from "next" to receipt?** Why: speed is the promise. Options: [under 20 s / under 40 s / no target]. My pick: under 20 seconds for a 3-item basket, measured by the POS from first scan to payment (assumption: barcode on 100% of items and a UPI sound box make 20 s normal).
14. **Cash or UPI first?** Why: change-counting is the slowest act in Indian retail. Options: [UPI first, cash accepted / cash first / UPI only]. My pick: UPI first; cash accepted; no coins under ₹1 handed back, rounded down on cash and written on the counter (assumption: 70% UPI by month 3 in this suburb).
15. **One till or two at peak?** Why: 8:00–9:00 and 6:00–7:30 pm carry 35–40% of the day. Options: [one till + services position / two tills / one till + self-pay screen]. My pick: one till plus the services position acting as a second till at peak (assumption: the second staff member can switch screens in 5 seconds).
16. **Baskets or hand-carry?** Why: baskets double the basket size and slow the aisle. Options: [12 small baskets / 20 baskets / none]. My pick: 12 small (8 L) baskets at the door, no trolleys (assumption: the average basket is 3–4 items; a big basket makes a small store feel like a mall).
17. **Receipt?** Why: FSSAI number on every bill, DPDP consent on first visit. Options: [printed always / WhatsApp receipt for opted-in / none under ₹50]. My pick: printed by default, WhatsApp receipt replaces paper once the customer opts in (assumption: the receipt message is the cheapest consent moment).

### Seating and washroom rules

18. **Is a purchase required to sit?** Why: it is the brand. Options: [no / after 30 min / yes]. My pick: no; written on the wall in Telugu and English; staff never ask (assumption: fewer than 5% of sitters never buy over a month; the rest become regulars).
19. **Seat time at peak?** Why: 8 seats, 300 customers an hour. Options: [none / polite 30-min sign / staff reminder]. My pick: none written; the bench is uncomfortable enough after 40 minutes (assumption: a 45 cm bench with a straight back self-limits dwell).
20. **Washroom for customers: open or on request?** Why: open washrooms get dirty; locked ones get unused. Options: [open / key at the till / staff only]. My pick: key on a wooden tag at the till, no questions asked, logged on the hourly sheet (assumption: the key adds 10 seconds and halves the cleaning load).
21. **Who cleans the washroom and how often?** Why: one dirty visit ends the habit. Options: [hourly by staff on checklist / twice a day / outsourced]. My pick: hourly wipe by staff, full clean at 2pm and 10:30pm, sheet on the door the customer can read (assumption: 3 minutes an hour; the visible sheet is the trust).
22. **Priority seat?** Why: rulebook RS6. Options: [one marked / none / all]. My pick: one bench end marked "Peddavaallaki, ammalaki" with a cushion (assumption: a marked seat is used by the right people 80% of the time without staff).

### Lighting and smell

23. **Light colour?** Why: 6500K panels make food look grey. Options: [3000K warm everywhere / 4000K neutral everywhere / 3000K over food and bench, 4000K over shelves]. My pick: the mix: 3000K over the hot counter, chai, bench and celebrate shelf; 4000K over gondolas; nothing cooler than 4000K (assumption: ₹40k of LED either way; the mix sells food).
24. **Light level?** Why: bright enough to feel safe at 9pm from the road, not an operating theatre. Options: [300 lux / 500 lux / 750 lux]. My pick: 500 lux at shelf, 700 at the hot counter, glass front lit from inside after dark (assumption: the store should be the brightest thing on the street at 9pm).
25. **What should the store smell of?** Why: smell is the first memory. Options: [ghee and chai / agarbatti / nothing (AC)]. My pick: ghee and fresh chai; no agarbatti burning, no room freshener, exhaust only in the washroom (assumption: the vendor's ghee-podi cup opened at the counter and the chai bot do the job).
26. **Music?** Why: a quiet store feels like a chemist. Options: [none / low Telugu film songs / radio]. My pick: low Telugu radio (FM) at 5:30–9am and 5–9pm, off at other hours (assumption: royalty-free is not needed for FM playback at low volume; confirm with a CA).

### Signage language

27. **Fascia language?** Why: the name is read from a scooter. Options: [Telugu first, English second / English first / three languages]. My pick: "కన్‌స్టో" large, "Consto" below at 60% size, no Hindi on the fascia (assumption: 85% of walk-ins read Telugu first; English keeps the brand portable).
28. **In-store signs?** Why: zone names and rules. Options: [Telugu + English / English only / icons only]. My pick: Telugu + English on every zone sign, icons for washroom, charging and water (assumption: the rest-corner wall text in Telugu is what makes the rule feel like a person said it).
29. **Where does Hindi appear?** Why: Patancheru's industrial belt has north Indian workers who use bill pay and AEPS. Options: [nowhere / services sign only / everywhere]. My pick: the services sign only, three languages (assumption: 10–15% of service-counter customers are Hindi-first; nobody else needs it).

### Price display

30. **How are hot-food prices shown?** Why: no MRP on food; customers fear being overcharged. Options: [chalk board only / printed menu card / price on the tray tag]. My pick: chalk board plus the price on every tray clock card; round numbers only (₹10/₹20/₹30/₹40) (assumption: round, visible, hand-written prices feel fairer than printed ones in this type of place).
31. **Shelf price strips?** Why: MRP is on the pack; a strip repeats it. Options: [MRP strip on every facing / no strips / strips only on the 50 known-value items]. My pick: strips on the 50 known-value items only, with "same as MRP" in Telugu (assumption: the strip's job is to say "we don't overcharge", not to inform).
32. **Combo signs?** Why: PR3. Options: [one combo board at the till / on every shelf / none]. My pick: one board at the till with three combos, never more (assumption: three choices sell; eight confuse).

### Organic and healthy items without the word

33. **What is the section called?** Why: "organic" reads as expensive. Options: ["Mana Ooru" / "Healthy" / "Organic"]. My pick: "Mana Ooru" (our village) with a hand-drawn map pin of the maker's village on each shelf talker (assumption: provenance beats certification here).
34. **How is "healthy" shown?** Why: a green leaf logo means nothing here. Options: [maker's name and village / "no colour, no preservatives" line / nutrition table]. My pick: maker's name + village + packed-on date + the no-colour line in Telugu; no nutrition table (assumption: trust comes from a person, not a table).
35. **Price premium cap?** Why: one overpriced item taints the shelf. Options: [0% / up to 10% / up to 25%]. My pick: 10% over the nearest national brand, hard cap (assumption: above 10% the shelf reads as "for rich people").
36. **Millets, A2 ghee, cold-pressed oil?** Why: these are the "organic" asks that do exist. Options: [small packs on the Mana Ooru shelf / skip / full range]. My pick: ragi malt, jowar flakes, A2 ghee 200 ml, 500 ml cold-pressed groundnut oil as the one oil exception on this shelf only (assumption: 6–8 items, each under ₹250, 10–15 units a week).

### The WhatsApp agent: greet, remind, pre-order, credit

37. **How does the Agent first get a number?** Why: DPDP consent first. Options: [ask at the till / services counter captures it / QR on the receipt]. ## 4. Three things nobody has thought of

### 4.1 The bandi window

A 2 ft sliding hatch in the hot-counter glass, at scooter-seat height, with a UPI QR on the sill. From 5:30 to 9:30 in the morning and 5 to 9 in the evening a rider gets chai, two boiled eggs, a milk packet or the morning plate without dismounting, in under 30 seconds. Paan shops have run this hand-through-the-window trade for a century; no convenience format in India has built it on purpose. It doubles the morning throughput of a 2-person crew, it is what a store "visible from a scooter at 20 km/h" actually means, and after 10:30pm it is the only window that needs to open if Sateesh ever wants a late hour without a late store. Cost: about ₹6,000 of glazing.

### 4.2 The school-bus bench

In every apartment suburb of this type, between 7:30 and 8:30, mothers stand on the road with children waiting for school buses, and again at 3:30–4:30 waiting for them to come back. Put the bench where it sees the bus stop, keep a folding high chair on the wall, and sell the ₹10 chai and a ₹20 kids' snack to the people who are already standing there every single school day. The Agent learns the child's name and the bus time. This is a 200-day-a-year habit that costs nothing but the position of a bench, and it makes the store the women's store first, which in this suburb is what decides where a family's money goes.

### 4.3 The cubby wall

Twelve numbered insulated boxes on the wall behind the till. Order the morning plate or the evening pack on WhatsApp by 9pm, pay by UPI, get "cubby 4, ready 7:30, Lakshmi aunty" at 7:25, walk in, take it, walk out in ten seconds without a queue. Japan does parcel pickup; nobody does hot-food pickup by name in a neighbourhood store. It converts the Agent from a messaging channel into a physical object the customer touches every day, it pre-sells 40–60 plates before the case is loaded (which is the single biggest waste cut available), and it gives the vendor a firm order by 9pm the night before. Cost: about ₹8,000 of boxes and a shelf.
