# What the customers say — four neighbours react to the Consto plan

Written 6 October 2026, in the voices of four people who live within 500 m of the planned store: a growing suburb or district town where new apartment blocks sit next to older independent houses, 5–20 km from the city, with kiranas, two tiffin centres, a medical shop, a bakery and a chicken shop already there, and Blinkit/Zepto absent or unreliable. They have read `Reference/Products.md` and the two notes `06-india-cost-conscious-landscape.md` and `08-organic-natural-healthy-demand.md`. Quotes in Part 2 are from those documents.

Prices are what these four pay in their area today (2026), with the assumptions stated in Part 3. Nothing here is a survey; it is four people talking honestly, and the founder should treat each as one vote, not a majority.

**The four**

| | Who | Household | What they want from a store |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lakshmi, 34 | Working mother; kids 6 and 11; husband in an IT park 14 km away | ₹80k–1.2 lakh/month, 2BHK flat | Eleven minutes at 7am and eleven at 7pm; nothing that breaks the routine |

## Part 1 — Each customer's honest reaction

### Lakshmi, 34 — "I have eleven minutes at 7am"

Two tiffin boxes, one husband, school bus at 7:40 and my own cab at 8:10. That is my morning. If your idli is hot and boxed at 6:30 and I can pay and leave in under a minute, I am there every weekday. Not "90-second checkout" — I count the queue in front of me too. One slow aunty with cash and I am late.

Idli: the tiffin centre near the bus stop is ₹30 for three with chutney, ₹40 with a vada. I will pay ₹30 for three with chutney and podi if it comes in a box that goes straight into the 11-year-old's tiffin without leaking by lunchtime. At ₹40 I make upma myself. Curd rice at ₹40 for 250 g: yes, twice a week, for the elder one's lunch on the days I can't cook; ₹50 no. Puffs at ₹20: the kids will demand them at 5pm, twice a week, and I will give in. Chai: I drink at home; maybe one ₹10 cup on a Saturday.

Would I trust a hot case? I will look at the time tag every single time. If it says loaded 6:15 and it is 9am, fine. If I see the same tray at 11, I don't come back, and I tell the apartment WhatsApp group. Tell me whose kitchen it is. A name on the glass — I trust iD and Vijaya because I know them; an unnamed vendor I don't.

Organic: I do not care about the word. I care that the six-year-old eats ragi (she only will if it's chocolate-flavoured), that there are no-maida biscuits, and that when my father visits for a month there is sugar-free Horlicks and something he can snack on. ₹5–10 more than the regular pack is fine; double is not. If you call it "healthy for kids" I buy it; if you call it "organic" I assume you've added ₹40.

WhatsApp from a shop: one message a day at most, and only if it does something — "milk kept aside", "parcel arrived", "Ekadashi tomorrow, tulsi kept". Never "OFFER!!". If it asks once what my kids like, I'll answer. If it sends a poll every week, I mute, and once I mute I never unmute.

Seating: no time on weekdays; Saturday evening with the kids and a puff, maybe. Washroom: yes, with kids, and it must be clean or it is worse than none. Credit: I pay UPI, I don't need khata — but my maid would, and if you let her put ₹300 of things on khata in my name with my OK, that's one thing off my list.

Delivery: yes, this is the thing. Order by 5pm, delivered 6:30–7:30pm, ₹20 charge, free above ₹300: curd, bread, eggs, the thing I forgot. Not 10 minutes — nobody here has got used to 10 minutes yet. Services: parcel pickup is huge; Amazon and Meesho come when nobody is home and the gate security refuses them. Xerox for school projects, yes, weekly in exam season. Bill pay and recharge, no, I do that on my phone.

Never buy here: rice, atta, oil, big dal (DMart once a month, one trip, 15% cheaper), vegetables (the pushcart man comes to the gate), chicken (the shop near the mosque, cut in front of me), school uniforms, anything above ₹500. Sanitary pads, yes, if the boy bags them without a face.

### Ravi, 26 — "Where is the chai at 11:30pm?"

Three of us in the flat, nobody cooks. Office canteen for lunch, tea stall at 6 (₹12, small cup, good), Zomato or Maggi at night. The kirana shuts at 10:30 and after that this whole suburb is dark. If you close at 11 you are the kirana with brighter lights. Midnight on weekdays, 1am Friday and Saturday, and I am yours.

Chai at ₹10, daily, two or three times, if it is not watery premix. I know the difference; premix tastes of sugar and powder. A boiled egg ₹10, masala egg ₹15 — daily. Idli at ₹30 at 8:15 because I am late every day: yes, three times a week. Curd rice ₹40 at 10pm: yes, twice a week, with pickle. Puff or samosa ₹20 at 6pm. Egg toastie ₹40: yes, that's new here; a toastie with no egg and no chicken is bread with cheese and ₹40 is too much for that.

Hot case: I eat at tea stalls, I will eat a four-hour-old samosa. But I'll notice a sad-looking puff sitting alone in a big empty case. Keep the case small and full, not big and half empty.

Organic: zero. Protein: eggs, ₹10 chikki, a ₹50 bar once a week. Do not stock ₹2,000 whey tubs; I buy that online and so does everyone at my gym.

WhatsApp: fine, if it is useful. "Closing in 30 minutes, curd rice half price" — I will come down in my shorts. Let me order on WhatsApp — "2 eggs, 1 Maggi, 1 Sprite" — and pick up in five minutes without talking to anyone. Don't call me "Ravi garu" in every line; from a machine that's creepy. Don't message me before 10am.

Seating: yes, with a charging point and Wi-Fi; I would sit 20 minutes with a chai at 10pm instead of in my room. Washroom: yes. Credit: no. Delivery: at 11pm, yes, ₹20; "order after 10pm, delivered next morning by 6:30" is useless to me — I'm asleep at 6:30. Services: parcel pickup, big — I get three Amazon parcels a week and the watchman loses one a month. Recharge, no. Print, rarely.

Never buy here: detergent and big shampoo (Amazon subscription), anything above ₹200 except deodorant and a razor. Cigarettes: I don't smoke, both my flatmates do. They'll go to the paan shop anyway, and buy the chips, the cold drink and the Maggi there too. Your call, but know what it costs.

### Venkatamma, 62 — "Vijaya milk, Vijaya only"

I wake at 5. Temple at 5:45, back by 6:30, and the milk has to be bought on the way back. Vijaya toned, half litre, ₹29. If you stock only Heritage or Amul I walk 200 m further to the kirana, and so do the other aunties in my lane. Every day, 365 days. I count the paise; if the bill says ₹28.55, give me ₹29 and tell me so, or round it my way; don't make me do arithmetic at the counter.

Curd I set at home; I buy a Vijaya 200 g cup only when guests come. Idli: on the two days my daughter-in-law leaves early for office, I will buy for me and the grandson — ₹20 for two, ₹30 for three if they are soft. I know yesterday's batter. Pulihora ₹30 on Saturday, yes. Upma ₹20 when my sugar is high. Chai ₹10 in the afternoon — the same small cup as the tiffin centre is fine — if there is a chair with a back and a fan. One Osmania biscuit, ₹8.

Hot case: I will ask the boy what time it was made. If he says "fresh madam" without looking, I know. Put a clock on the tray in Telugu, big letters. Separate the egg things from my idli — different tongs, different corner — or I won't take idli from the same hand.

Organic ante ekkuva dabbulu — organic means more money. I buy jaggery from the woman at the Sunday santha for ₹55 a half kilo; if yours is from a named village at ₹60, all right. Ragi I need every day for my java — ₹80 for 250 g is fine. Sugar-free biscuits, a ₹30 pack, yes; my sugar is 180. Horlicks Diabetes is too dear; my son buys it when he feels like it.

WhatsApp: it is my son's phone, I don't read it. Tell me at the counter, or ring the landline. If the boy at the counter knows my name and that I take Vijaya toned and the Cycle agarbatti, that is your agent, not the phone.

Sitting: this is the reason I would come. A chair with a back, under the fan, near the door, not a plastic stool. Let me sit half an hour with a chai and talk to whoever comes. Washroom: yes — my knee — the kirana has none and the temple's is far. Credit: no, I pay cash, exact. Delivery: when the knee is bad, milk and curd, ₹10 is all right for a ₹100 order; nothing more.

Services: my pension — I go 2 km to the CSC to withdraw it with my thumb. If you do that here, I come every month and tell every aunty. Electricity bill I pay in cash; if you take it with a printed receipt, good. Parcel, no. Medicines: ORS and bandages are not medicines; I need Dolo, Eno, Vicks, Amrutanjan and my Metformin. The medical shop has them and gives me credit till pension day, so you won't get that from me unless you have the same.

Pooja: Cycle agarbatti, camphor, cotton wicks, deepam oil, kumkum, and fresh flowers at 6am — loose, not in plastic — ₹10 or ₹20. Flowers at 6 and I come daily. Never buy here: rice, vegetables, sweets (I make them), and anything in a pack I cannot read; write Telugu on the shelf.

### Shaik Imran, 41 — "Khata and chicken puff, or I'm at the bakery"

I buy for the shop and for the house. Shop: two boys, customers who wait, chai twice a day for everyone, puffs in the morning, cold drinks for the customers who buy a phone, water cans, Xerox of Aadhaar copies all day, bills, and parcels of spare parts three times a week. House: a tray of eggs, bread, milk, biscuits for the kids, my mother's sugar-free things, and Friday sweets.

Khata is the whole thing. I run a khata with the kirana and the bakery and settle both on Friday evening on UPI. ₹3,000–5,000 a month, no interest, one reminder on Thursday, and never a "PAY NOW" message. If I have to pay on the spot for every chai flask, I stay with the bakery.

Puffs: veg ₹20, egg ₹25, chicken ₹30 — the bakery sells chicken puff at ₹25–30 and that is where my boys are at 9am. If there is no egg and no chicken in your case, it is a snack shop for children. Keep it in a separate tray with separate tongs and a clear label so my Hindu customers also buy from you; that's good for both of us. Tell me who the chicken supplier is; a name is enough. I would buy chicken 65 at 7pm if it were hot and ₹60, but I understand there's no space; I won't buy raw chicken from you, I have my own shop for that.

Chai: a flask of six cups at ₹60, twice a day, and I'll leave a deposit for the flask. Idli ₹30 for three for the boys, three days a week. Curd rice, no; we eat rice at home. Hot case trust: fine, if it is hot. I eat at the Irani hotel; I'm not scared of a case.

Organic, no. "Healthy": my mother is diabetic and my wife wants no-maida biscuits for the kids. Sugar-free biscuits, yes. ₹5 more, fine.

WhatsApp: yes, I live on it. I'll send the list at 8am — "1 tray eggs, 2 bread, 6 cold drinks, 1 can" — delivered to the shop by 10, on khata. Thursday: "Imran bhai, this week ₹1,840, settle Friday?" That's all. Never message my wife's number about what I bought. "Imran bhai" is right; "Imran garu" is not me.

Seating: no time. Washroom: yes, for my boys; I'd pay ₹5. Delivery to the shop, free above ₹500, two times a day. Bill pay: shop electricity, three phones, DTH — if it's ₹0–5 and I get a printed receipt, I stop going to the PhonePe outlet. AEPS: my boys are from the village; they withdraw on Sunday. Parcel: the courier won't wait at my shop; a drop point 300 m away is gold. Xerox ₹2 a page, every day.

Festivals: Ramzan — dates, sheer khurma kit, sewai, by pre-order; Eid — gift boxes for the boys; Diwali — laddu boxes for my Hindu customers. If you take the pre-order and deliver on the day, I stop going to the city for it.

Never buy here: rice (25 kg from the trader), mutton, shop consumables (wholesale market), anything electronic, Eid clothes.

## Part 2 — Where we disagree with the report

1. **Report:** "make A (machine chai + Osmania biscuit) the hero for Store 1 and B (the toastie) the signature item it sells alongside." **Us:** chai is the hero for sitting; the 6:30 idli box is the hero for walking in. Lakshmi and Venkatamma never heard of a toastie and Ravi only wants it with egg. Put idli and chai on the board, the toastie on the counter.

2. **Report:** "no bajji or dosa (they go soggy, Dept 1.2)." **Us:** the evening in this suburb *is* a mirchi bajji at 5:30. If the case can't hold it, take a 5:30 delivery and sell out by 7 on a 90-minute clock; otherwise the cart outside your door takes Ravi's ₹20 and Venkatamma's company.

3. **Report:** "eggs only in Store 1; test chicken puffs in a separate labelled tray after 90 days." **Us:** Imran: 90 days is 90 days at the bakery. Ravi: egg puff day one. Lakshmi: fine if separate. Venkatamma: separate tongs, separate corner, or no idli from that hand. Separate tray from day one is what all four actually asked for; the 90-day wait serves nobody.

4. **Report:** "Bargaining — Remove; replace with a transparent member price" and "₹300 for 6 months, SuperK-style." **Us:** nobody here bargains at a kirana; Venkatamma asks for a round-down and gets it. ₹300 up front from a pensioner, no. Lakshmi pays it only if it buys delivery; Imran says khata customers who settle on time should get it free; Ravi would rather have ten ₹10 chais. Membership must be optional and never said at the counter.

5. **Report:** "Chai price [₹10 / ₹12 / ₹15]. My pick: ₹10 regular, ₹15 masala." **Us:** the price is right, the report is silent on the cup. Ravi and Imran want 100–120 ml at ₹10, not 80 ml; Venkatamma wants exactly the tiffin-centre cup. Imran wants a six-cup flask at ₹60. The cup, not the price, decides whether the ₹25 Irani cup loses.

6. **Report:** "Hot-case list at launch [idli, pulihora, puffs, samosa, boiled and masala eggs] … upma and pongal in week 3." **Us:** no vada? Idli-vada is one word. The report's own 4-hour fried clock allows it. And upma on day one, not week 3; it is Venkatamma's high-sugar-day breakfast and the ₹20 item.

7. **Report:** "Fixed window: 8pm, 30–35% off … announced on WhatsApp to opted-in customers." **Us:** Venkatamma will come at 8 every day and buy nothing before. Ravi gets home at 9; 8pm is before his dinner. Lakshmi does not want to hear it daily. Make it 9pm, tell only people who bought hot food this week, and keep the morning idli at full price so the 7am habit is not taught to wait.

8. **Report:** "Staples [stay out / 500 g Consto dal packs only / 1 kg top-ups]. My pick: stay out." **Us:** the 7pm "dal finished" emergency happens every fortnight in Lakshmi's and Venkatamma's homes. A 250 g and 500 g Consto toor and moong pack at the per-kg price of the big pack plus ₹5 is the whole ask. Imran agrees with the report; he buys 25 kg from a trader.

9. **Report:** "2 km, two batches a day, never 10-minute" and "orders after 10pm delivered next morning by 6:30." **Us:** two batches work if they are 7–8am and 6:30–7:30pm (Lakshmi) and a shop run by 10am (Imran). Ravi's 11pm order delivered at 6:30am is useless; he wants "order on WhatsApp, pick up in five minutes" until close. Three windows plus pickup, not two batches.

10. **Report:** "Open 6am–11pm." **Us:** the kirana's milk crates land at 5:30 and Venkatamma is at the door at 6 on the way back from the temple; Lakshmi's cab is at 8:10 and she wants milk before the kids wake. Ravi loses you at 11. The report's own Japanese figure (9pm–midnight = 8–10%) is his argument. 5:30am (milk and flowers only) to midnight, 1am on Friday and Saturday with chiller only.

11. **Report:** "Schedule K shelf, no licence: ORS, bandages, cotton, repellents, petroleum jelly…" **Us:** that is a first-aid box, not a medicine shelf. Venkatamma needs Dolo, Eno, Vicks, Amrutanjan and Metformin, and the medical shop gives her credit till pension day. The law is the law; so put the pharmacy pick-up partner in month 1, not month 3, and stock every balm and digestive that is legally a cosmetic or ayurvedic product on day one.

12. **Report:** "6–10 seats facing the chai machine and hot case; Indonesia's lesson is seats that face the food." **Us:** Venkatamma wants a chair with a back under a fan near the door; Ravi wants a charging point and Wi-Fi; Imran wants a washroom for his boys. The report never decides on a washroom. Facing the food is your problem; the chair, the fan, the plug and the washroom are ours.

## Part 4 — Decision questions from the customers' side

Format: question, why, options, our pick, the assumption behind it. "Our pick" is what the four of us would vote for, with the name of whoever cares most.

### Morning food and the hot case

1. **What is the idli plate — 2, 3 or 4 pieces, and at what price?** Why: we price it by the piece against ₹20–40 at the tiffin centre. Options: [2 pcs ₹20 / 3 pcs ₹30 with chutney and podi / 4 pcs ₹40]. Our pick: both 2 for ₹20 (Venkatamma) and 3 for ₹30 (Lakshmi, Ravi), same chutney cup (assumption: landed cost ₹5–6 a piece plus ₹3 chutney leaves 30%+).
2. **Does the idli come in a box that goes straight into a school tiffin?** Why: Lakshmi's whole use case is the 7am box. Options: [foil tray / paper box with a lidded chutney cup / reusable tiffin with deposit]. Our pick: paper box with lidded chutney cup (assumption: ₹3–4 packaging is covered inside the ₹30).
3. **Must vada be in the case?** Why: idli-vada is one word here; the report's 4-hour fried clock allows it. Options: [no / yes all day / yes 6:30–10:30 only]. Our pick: 6:30–10:30 only, ₹12 each, idli-vada combo ₹40 (assumption: the snack fryer vendor delivers at 6:15).
4. **Upma and pongal on day one or week 3?** Why: upma is the ₹20 item and Venkatamma's high-sugar breakfast. Options: [both week 3 / upma day one, pongal week 3 / both day one]. Our pick: upma day one, pongal week 3 (assumption: upma is from the same kitchen as idli).
5. **Evening mirchi bajji and punugulu — yes or no?** Why: the 5:30pm habit here is a bajji; the cart outside will sell it if you don't. Options: [no / 5:30 delivery on a 90-minute clock / tie up with the cart outside]. Our pick: 5:30 delivery, ₹20 for 4, sold out by 7 (assumption: the snack fryer does a second run; unsold goes to the 9pm discount).
6. **How is the age of the hot-case food shown to us?** Why: all four of us look; Venkatamma asks. Options: [back-office tag only / customer-facing "loaded hh:mm, pull by hh:mm" tag in Telugu and English / none]. Our pick: customer-facing, Telugu first (assumption: a laminated tray card and a marker cost nothing).
7. **Is the kitchen's name on the glass?** Why: Lakshmi trusts a named kitchen; Imran wants a named chicken supplier. Options: [no / name and FSSAI number on the case / plus the story once on WhatsApp]. Our pick: name and FSSAI number on the case, story once (assumption: the vendor agrees to be named).
8. **Curd rice: 250 g at ₹40 or 200 g at ₹30?** Why: Ravi at 10pm, Lakshmi for a lunch box. Options: [200 g ₹30 / 250 g ₹40 with pickle / both]. Our pick: 250 g ₹40 with a pickle sachet, 8-hour chilled pull (assumption: chilled life is at least 24 h from the vendor).
9. **Is 6am early enough?** Why: milk crates land at the kirana at 5:30; Venkatamma is at the door at 6 after the temple; Lakshmi wants milk before the kids wake. Options: [6 / 5:30 milk and flowers only, hot case from 6:30 / 5]. Our pick: 5:30 with milk, curd, flowers and chai; hot case from 6:30 (assumption: the milk route can drop by 5:15).
10. **What is the closing time?** Why: the kirana shuts at 10:30 and Ravi is awake till 1. Options: [11 / midnight / midnight, 1am Fri–Sat]. Our pick: midnight, 1am Fri–Sat, chiller and chai only after 11 (assumption: one staff after 11 with the shutter half down and UPI only).

### Chai

11. **Cup size at ₹10?** Why: price is right; Ravi and Imran want a real cup, Venkatamma the tiffin-centre cup. Options: [80 ml / 100 ml / 120 ml]. Our pick: 100 ml at ₹10, 150 ml at ₹15 (assumption: milk and premix cost under ₹4 a 100 ml cup).
