## What this document is

A working strategy doc for **the product company that doesn't yet have a name**. The product company is the second half of the two-sided opportunity that Consto has been quietly building toward from day one.

This document is **internal**. It is for me (Sateesh), future co-founders, and trusted advisors. It is not pitch-ready and it is not for the Consto portfolio. Consto-the-retail-brand and the product-company-the-platform are deliberately kept separate in everything customer-facing.

If you are reading this from outside the founding circle, treat it as private working thinking, not a public commitment.

## The two-sided thesis

What we are actually building is two related businesses:

### Side A · Consto · The retail brand

A neighbourhood store in Beeramguda, then a small franchise chain across Hyderabad and eventually Tier 2-3 India. Physical stores. End consumers. Linear scale (one store at a time, then franchise).

This is what the portfolio describes. This is what the brand is. This is what customers know.

### Side B · The product company (name TBD)

A design-led software company that sells agentic AI products to Indian retail and SMBs. POS as the flagship. The same 8 products that power Consto OS, packaged and sold to other retailers who are not Consto.

This is what *most of the equity value lives in over a 10-year horizon*. Software scales infinitely. Retail scales linearly. Both are needed, but the product company is the bigger long-term business.

### Why both, not one

| If you only do Side A (Consto retail) | If you only do Side B (the product company) |
|---|---|
| Capped at ~50-100 stores realistically. Linear capital intensity. The software you build is locked inside one brand. Most of the strategic value never escapes the operating company. | No real-world proof that the platform works. No design constraints from running an actual store. No anchor customer to dogfood every feature. Sales conversations sound like every other "AI for SMBs" pitch. |

Doing both is the strategic unlock. Consto proves the thesis. The product company sells the thesis. Each side de-risks the other.

This is the same pattern serious operators have used before:
- Amazon retail → AWS
- Shopify (merchant) → Shopify (platform)
- Toast (restaurant operators) → Toast (POS)
- Square (one coffee shop) → Square (the platform)

The pattern works. We are following a well-trodden path, applied to India.

## What the product company is

A **design-led, agentic AI software company for Indian retail and SMBs**.

Three words doing the work:

**Design-led.** Every existing POS / inventory / CRM product in India is engineer-built. Functional, ugly, learned-helplessness UX. The product company is design-led: every product feels like it was made by people who actually thought about the customer. This is genuinely white space. No one is competing here.

**Agentic.** The differentiator is not "AI features." It is the memory-knowledge-wisdom architecture that compounds across years. Every product has an embedded agent that learns the customer's business over time and produces actual strategic recommendations. Static SaaS will not compete with this.

**For Indian retail and SMBs.** The target market is not Western. Not even global. It is Indian first. The cultural fit (festivals, languages, credit-with-dignity, family context) is built into the products. Westerners trying to sell into India will not match this.

## Product portfolio

The 8 products in `PRDs/01-CONSTO-POS.md` through `PRDs/08-CONSTO-HQ.md` ARE the product company's product portfolio. They just happen to also be the operating system of Consto stores.

Reframed as B2B products:

| Product | Internal name | External positioning |
|---|---|---|
| 01 POS | Consto POS | **The flagship.** Design-led, agentic, India-first point-of-sale. Hardware integration. Embedded customer-memory panel. For any small Indian retailer. |
| 02 Agent | Consto Agent | **Customer-memory CRM.** Real-time profiles, in-store nudges, drafted messages with human review. For retailers, restaurants, clinics. |
| 03 Inventory | Consto Inventory | **Stock + waste prediction.** AI-driven reorder, expiry routing, customer-signal-based catalog adaptation. For any FMCG retailer. |
| 04 Notify | Consto Notify | **Customer communications layer.** SMS + WhatsApp with consent gate, drafts, human review. For any SMB with a customer list. |
| 05 Loyalty | Consto Loyalty | **Retention + churn prediction.** Tier programs, real benefits, AI churn forecasting. For retailers, restaurants, salons. |
| 06 Ops | Consto Ops | **Daily ops + checklists.** Photo proofs, incident logs, shift summaries. For any multi-staff small business. |
| 07 Predict | Consto Predict | **Demand forecasting.** SKU-level, festival-aware, weather-aware. For FMCG and food businesses. |
| 08 HQ | Consto HQ | **Owner dashboard with natural-language queries.** Single-screen business view + quarterly Wisdom recommendations. For any multi-location SMB. |

These are sold as a **suite** with a base subscription plus per-product modules. Pricing TBD but anchor: **₹2,000-₹5,000/month per location** for kiranas (matches existing kirana-tech price points). Restaurants and clinics priced higher.

POS is the wedge product. Customers come for POS, expand to Agent + Loyalty within 6 months, then add Inventory and Predict by year 2.

## Target verticals beyond Consto

The product company is **not** locked to convenience-store kiranas. The architecture generalises. Verticals worth pursuing:

| Vertical | Why it fits | Size of market |
|---|---|---|
| **Kiranas** | Direct fit. Same problems as Consto. Same memory thesis. Same cultural fit. | ~12M stores in India. 5% penetration = 600K customers. |
| **Restaurants** | Customer memory + order patterns + festival demand all matter. Same agentic logic, different domain. Toast-equivalent for India. | ~600K formal + 1.5M informal restaurants. |
| **Clinics + pharmacies** | Memory layer is enormous in medical context. "Doctor remembers your last visit, what worked, your family history." DPDP compliance + medical privacy = harder, but defensible. | ~400K clinics, 850K pharmacies. |
| **Salons + service businesses** | Customer rhythm + preferences + special occasions. Loyalty + retention are huge. Lower technical complexity. | ~1.2M salons, plus tailors, repair shops, etc. |
| **Agentic CRM for SMBs** (cross-vertical) | The Agent product, packaged as a standalone CRM. Sold to any small business with a customer list. | The widest market — millions. |
| **Festival/cultural intelligence as an API** | The cultural-calendar engine licensed to other Indian retail tech. Smaller revenue, but a moat-building product. | Few but high-value enterprise customers. |

Start with kiranas (closest to Consto, easiest to dogfood). Restaurants by year 2-3. Clinics + salons by year 3-4. Cross-vertical CRM as a parallel product line.

## Differentiation — why this wins

Five things differentiate this from every other India-focused SMB software:

1. **Design-led, not engineer-led.** No existing POS in India is designed well. We can compete on this alone.

2. **Agentic, not static.** Memory + Knowledge + Wisdom compounds with the customer's business over years. Switching away gets harder every month.

3. **Built from inside a real store.** Consto Store 1 is the dogfood. Every product is battle-tested against actual cashiers, actual inventory, actual customer messages. Sales conversations sound different when you've lived the problem.

4. **India-first cultural fit.** Telugu festival calendar runs the store. Hindi, Telugu, Tamil bilingual switching is native. Credit-with-dignity is built into Loyalty. None of this is in Western-translated software.

5. **Honest about WhatsApp.** Phase 1 ships without WhatsApp Cloud API. We do not lock customers into Meta's leash. SMS first, WhatsApp opt-in. This is unusual positioning that respects the customer.

The combination of all five is white space. The market is huge. The fight is winnable.

## Why Consto is the showcase

Consto stores are not a separate business that happens to use the same software. They are the **operating proof of the product company's thesis**, on permanent public display.

**What Consto Store 1 produces for the product company:**

- A real, working installation. Sales calls can say "come see it running." Investors can visit. Future customers can see what it looks like in practice.
- A reference customer with real metrics. "Consto Store 1 cut waste by 30% and lifted average basket by 27% in 90 days." Hard data, not slides.
- Feature requests from a high-quality user. Sateesh-the-store-operator is the most demanding QA the product will ever get.
- Brand halo. Consto-the-retail-brand becomes a credibility signal for the product company even though they share no name.
- Recruiting bait. Designers and engineers want to work on products that have a clear, tangible "this changed real people's lives" story.

**What Consto loses by being the showcase:**

Almost nothing. Running the OS on Consto is what Consto needs to be a serious modern store anyway. The dogfooding is free.

This is why the two sides reinforce each other. Most B2B SaaS companies have to fabricate dogfood. This one has actual customers walking in every day.

## Branding

The single most important branding decision: **the product company is NOT called Consto.**

### Why a separate brand

- Consto is the retail brand. Customers say "I shop at Consto." If the same name shows up on a kirana's point-of-sale, it's confusing — is the kirana now a Consto store? No.
- A separate brand lets the product company sell to retailers who are competitors of Consto-the-store (Tier 1 city kiranas, restaurants, etc.) without conflict.
- Different brands let the two operations have different visual identities, tones, marketing channels.
- If one side underperforms, the other isn't dragged down.

### Naming criteria

When the product company gets a name (post-Consto-Store-1 validation), the name should be:

- **Short** — under 7 letters ideally, easier to type, easier to remember
- **India-friendly** — recognisable across Hindi/Telugu/Tamil contexts, not Western-coded
- **Not literal** — "Consto OS" is descriptive but limiting. The product company will eventually sell more than the OS.
- **Available** — .com, .in domains; trademark clear in India; not used by another retail-tech company
- **Distinct from Consto** — not visually or phonetically similar (so they can stand side by side without confusion)

Working placeholder in this document: "the product company." When a name is chosen, find-and-replace.

### Until then

In all internal documents, treat the product company as a placeholder. No marketing material. No domain registrations. No LinkedIn page. The name decision should come after:

- Consto Store 1 has operated for 6+ months
- 3 external pilot customers are actively using the OS
- A small founding team for the product company exists
- The team has had time to live with the unnamed reality

Rushing the name is a common mistake. Hold the line.

## Build sequence

A staged plan that lets each phase validate before the next.

### Year 1 (Months 1-12) · Consto-only

- Consto Store 1 opens in Beeramguda
- Consto OS Phase 1 (POS + Agent) built and dogfooded
- 500 customer pilot, real unit economics
- The product company **does not exist** as a separate entity yet

### Year 2 (Months 13-24) · Quiet pilot

- Consto OS Phase 2-3 built (Inventory, Notify, Loyalty, Ops)
- 3 external pilot customers added — kiranas in nearby colonies who use the OS without being Consto-branded stores
- Pilots are free or near-free; the goal is product-market-fit signal, not revenue
- The product company is formed as a legal entity by end of Year 2
- A small design + engineering team is hired for the product company
- A name is chosen for the product company

### Year 3 (Months 25-36) · Public launch

- The product company launches publicly with a website, brand, and sales motion
- First 50 paying customers across kiranas and select restaurants
- Consto OS Phase 4 built (Predict, HQ)
- Consto Store 2 opens (still under Consto-the-retail-brand)
- Separate fundraising for the product company

### Year 4-5 · The product company is the main business

- The product company becomes 70%+ of the total enterprise value
- Consto stores continue as the showcase brand, growing slowly (5-10 stores by Year 5)
- Verticals expand (restaurants, salons, clinics)
- Consto-the-retail-brand may eventually be spun off or licensed

This is a slow, careful build. Not a "blitzscale" plan. The dogfooding requires patience.

## Fundraising paths

The two sides take different money.

### Side A · Consto retail

- **Seed:** ₹30-50L. From friends, angels, retail-focused investors, possibly a small fund.
- **Series A:** Only if franchise model is proven (Year 3+). Otherwise self-fund from store profits.
- **Investors are different:** retail investors care about store-level unit economics, real estate, supply chain. They don't care much about software equity.

### Side B · The product company

- **Pre-seed:** Internal capital + maybe a small angel round (₹50L-1Cr) once the company is formed (end of Year 2). Used for the founding team and initial product polish.
- **Seed:** ₹3-5Cr in Year 3 when the product launches publicly. SaaS-focused investors (Lightspeed, Accel, Matrix in India have SMB-software theses).
- **Series A:** ₹15-30Cr in Year 4 once $1-2M ARR is real.
- **Investors are different:** SaaS investors care about ARR, NRR, CAC, churn. Not about retail metrics.

### Cap-table implications

Because the two businesses have different investors, different metrics, and different scaling models, **they should likely be separate legal entities with separate cap tables**, both owned by a common parent (or by Sateesh as the common founder).

Specifics to decide with a lawyer in Year 2.

## What needs to be true before launching the product company publicly

Hard gates. Do not launch the product company publicly until ALL of these are true:

1. **Consto Store 1 has positive unit economics** at the store level (not counting the OS as a cost). Demonstrates the thesis works in the wild.
2. **3 external pilot customers are actively using the OS** in production. Not pilots-in-name. Actually transacting.
3. **The product company name is chosen, trademarked, and the domain is owned.**
4. **A small founding team for the product company exists** — at minimum, one designer + two engineers besides Sateesh.
5. **A separate legal entity is formed** with appropriate IP transfer agreements between Consto and the product company.
6. **Consto OS Phase 1-2 are battle-tested** through 6+ months of dogfooding plus 3+ months of pilot customer use.
7. **Pricing and packaging are validated** through actual money exchanged in pilots (even if discounted).

Launching before these gates is launched will produce a credibility hit that the product company will not recover from.

## Open questions

Things to decide later, not now:

- **Parent company structure.** Holding company with two subsidiaries? Or two independent companies with Sateesh as a common shareholder? Decide with a lawyer at end of Year 2.
- **Equity split with future co-founders.** Almost certainly need a product-company co-founder different from a Consto-store co-founder. How does equity work across both?
- **When (if ever) to spin Consto off entirely.** If the product company succeeds, Consto stores might become a small revenue line and a brand asset. Could be franchised more aggressively or sold to a franchise operator. Way too early to decide.
- **International expansion.** The thesis applies to other Asian / emerging markets eventually. Indonesia, Vietnam, the Philippines all have similar SMB software gaps. Not relevant before Year 5.
- **Open-source posture.** Some components (the Memory architecture, the consent layer) might benefit from being open-sourced for credibility. Others (the Wisdom engine) should stay proprietary. Decide product-by-product.
