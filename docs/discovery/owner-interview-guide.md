# Kirana Owner Interview Guide

**Goal:** Find out if remembering customers is a real, painful problem for kirana owners today, without selling them anything.
**Who:** 5 kirana owners near Beeramguda. Talk to the owner, not the helper.
**Time:** 15 minutes each. Go at a quiet hour (about 2–4 pm). Never during the morning or evening rush.
**Pass mark (stage 2 gate):** 3 of 5 owners describe the pain **before** you mention it.

---

## Golden rules

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "When did that last happen?" beats "Would you use…?" People are polite about the future and honest about the past.
2. **Don't pitch.** Don't say "AI", "app", "Consto" or "memory" until the last 2 minutes, and only if they ask.
3. **Listen 80%, talk 20%.** When they stop, wait 3 seconds. The best answers come after the pause.
4. **Dig into stories.** When they say something interesting, ask "Tell me more" or "What happened next?"
5. **Write their exact words.** A quote like "Madam stopped coming, I don't know why" is worth more than your summary.
6. **Buy something small.** It builds goodwill and you see the shop working.

---

## Opening (1 minute)

> "Namaste anna/akka. I grew up in my family's kirana in Gadwal. I'm studying how small shops are changing with Zepto, DMart and all. I'm not selling anything. Can I ask you a few questions for 10–15 minutes? I'll just write notes, no recording, no phone numbers."

Only continue if they say yes.

---

## Questions

### A. The shop today (warm-up, 2 min)
1. How long have you run this shop?
2. Roughly how many customers come in a day? How many are regulars you know by face?
3. What has changed most in the last 2–3 years?

### B. Losing customers (the core, 5 min)
4. **Tell me about the last regular customer who stopped coming.** Who were they? When did you notice?
5. Do you know why they stopped? What did you do about it?
6. How do you usually find out a regular has stopped coming? *(Listen: do they notice at all?)*
7. Which customers are you most afraid of losing? Why them?

### C. How they remember today (3 min)
8. How do you remember what each regular usually buys? *(Notebook, memory, helper, WhatsApp?)*
9. Tell me about a time you forgot something a customer wanted, or ran out of their usual item. What happened?
10. Do you keep a credit (khata) book? How do you track who owes what?
11. Do you ever message customers? About what? When did you last do it?

### D. Money and effort spent (2 min)
12. What do you already pay for to run the shop? *(Billing software, UPI device, WhatsApp Business, delivery boy?)*
13. Have you ever tried any app or software for the shop? What happened? Do you still use it?
14. If you could fix one thing about running this shop, what would it be?

### E. Close (2 min)
15. Who else should I talk to? Another owner nearby?
16. Can I come back in a few weeks to show you something simple and get your opinion?

**Only now, if they ask what you're working on:** describe it in one sentence and watch their face, not their words.

---

## What not to ask

| Don't ask | Why | Ask instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Would you use an app that remembers customers?" | Everyone says yes to be polite | "How do you remember customers today?" |
| "Is losing customers a problem?" | It leads them to the answer | "Tell me about the last regular who stopped coming." |
| "How much would you pay?" | Guesses, not facts | "What do you pay for today?" |
| "Don't you think AI could help?" | A pitch dressed as a question | Don't mention AI at all |

## After all 5 interviews

1. Count how many raised the pain **unprompted**. **3 or more** passes the gate; fewer means park or rethink the idea.
2. Write the top 3 quotes and 3 patterns into `research.md`.
3. Bring the Sheet back to discovery (Question 4 onward). The answers will replace guesses in the decision register.

**If they say "I know all my customers":** that's a real finding, not a failure. Ask what they *do* struggle with. The real problem may be credit tracking, stock, or competition, and the product can follow that.
