# Store operator's note on Products.md: the daily grind

**Who is writing:** a store operations manager who has run convenience stores and food counters (7-Eleven-style boxes with hot cases and chillers, dairy-parlour and bakery counters in Hyderabad). My lens is receiving, labelling, holding times, temperatures, cleaning, staffing, shrink, waste, shelf discipline, and what breaks at 6am and 9pm.
**What I read:** `Products.md` in full; notes `04-fresh-food-ops-and-waste.md`, `07-food-sourcing-vendor-vs-kitchen.md`, `09-store-space-layout-equipment-capex.md`, the Tanpin Kanri and 24h sections of `03-brand-selection-24hr-pharmacy.md`; skimmed `consto-rulebook.md`.
**Date:** 6 October 2026.

**One-paragraph verdict.** The report is right about the shape of the store (hot case + chiller + shelf, vendors not a kitchen, a pull-by clock, a planned waste budget, 6am–11pm). It is wrong about the hours of the day. It was written from Japanese chain documents, where food arrives from a factory with a printed hour-stamp, holds for 48 hours at 5°C, and a franchisee has a computer telling him when to mark down. Consto's food will arrive at the shutter in a steel dabba from a tiffin kitchen at 6:10, warm, uncounted and unlabelled, and one person will be receiving it while the milk boy shouts and the first chai customer waits. Everything below is about that gap. Where the report says "test", I state the number I would run on and why.

**Assumptions I run on throughout** (change these and the numbers move with them): 700 sq ft, 6am–11pm, ~300 customers a day, ~₹30,000 a day in sales, 100–130 hot or chilled food plates a day, two tiffin vendors within 5 km, one bakery, one dairy distributor, no tobacco, eggs but no meat in Store 1, a founder who visits twice a week and runs the rest on WhatsApp.

## Part 1. Where I disagree with the report

### 1. "Two deliveries a day, by 6:30am and by 3pm" does not survive one person on the morning shift

> "Two deliveries a day. Morning (by 6:30 am) and afternoon (by 3 pm). Two vendors, so one late vendor doesn't empty the hot case." (rulebook F2; repeated in Products.md 8.7 and decision 4)

**What an operator knows.** Between 5:30 and 6:45 the opener is doing five jobs alone: shutter and lights, hot case warm-up (20–30 minutes to temperature), milk and bread crates dumped at the shutter by a distributor's boy who will not wait, the first chai and milk customers, and now a hot-food drop that has to be counted, probed, tagged and loaded. A drop "by 6:30" means the case is empty for the first 30–45 minutes of the breakfast run, which is exactly when the morning walker and the school-run parent come. And the report's "two vendors" are a tiffin kitchen and a bakery, which is two products, not redundancy. If the idli man does not come, puffs do not fill the idli tray.

**Why.** Breakfast in a Telangana suburb is 6:45–9:30. The first drop must be in the case before the door opens. So: tiffin drop window 5:45–6:10, doors at 6:15 if that is what it takes; the evening drop at 16:00–16:20, not 15:00 (a samosa dropped at 15:00 is four hours old at 19:00, the heart of the evening); a third small drop at 11:30 only for biryani, only if biryani is in the range. Redundancy is a second idli maker on a standing 30% order, not a bakery.

### 2. "Fried 4 h, steamed/baked 6 h" is a Famichiki rule; Indian items each have their own clock

> "Hot-case discard clocks [fried 4 h, steamed/baked 6 h / a flat 4 h for everything]. My pick: 4 h fried, 6 h steamed and baked, printed on the tray tag." (decision 5)

**What an operator knows.** Idli dries and forms a skin in a dry glass warmer in 60–90 minutes; in a covered tray over a water pan it is fine for 2.5–3 hours, never 6. Vada is crisp for 45–60 minutes, soft and oily at 2 hours, leather at 3. Samosa holds 3 hours, then the pastry goes limp. Puffs like dry heat: 5–6 hours, and they re-crisp in a griller. Biryani in a covered container above 60°C holds 4–5 hours; the rice dries at the edges but sells. Pulihora and lemon rice do not belong in a hot case at all: sealed cup, ambient, 6 hours. Sandwiches sweat inside the pack within 20 minutes of a hot case; they are an ambient-shelf or chiller item, same day. Boiled eggs go rubbery under a lamp; warm covered tray, 4 hours.

**Why.** Holding clocks are about texture as much as safety. A dry idli at 9am is the "stale food" review that the report itself says killed 7-Eleven India's name (Section 3.3). The tray card carries a per-item clock (Part 3), not a per-cooking-method one.

### 3. "65°C, probe logged twice a shift" is the wrong temperature, measured in the wrong place, too rarely

> "Hot-case temperature [60°C FSSAI minimum / 63–65°C]. My pick: 65°C, probe logged twice a shift." (decision 6; note 04 KQ4)

**What an operator knows.** A ₹10–20k glass display warmer is a bulb, a base element and a dial. Set the dial to 65 and the bottom tray reads 70 while the top reads 55; the dial is air, not food. Hold idli or vada at a true 65°C core and they are ruined in an hour. What works is two zones: a wet zone (a 2-pan bain-marie or a steam tray over water) at 60–63°C for idli, upma, pongal, sambar and chutney; a dry zone at 65–70°C for samosa, puffs, cutlets. Probe the food, not the air, at load and then every 2 hours (six readings a day), written on the tray card with initials. "Twice a shift" becomes one entry written at 2pm from memory.

**Why.** An FSSAI inspector asks for a temperature log with times; a log that reads "65, 65, 65" every day for a month is what they treat as fake. The bain-marie (₹8–15k, listed in note 09 as "buy later, only if hot meals") is not optional. It is the idli tray.

### 4. "Chilled curd rice has the longest life" assumes a chiller the store does not have and a vendor that does not exist here

> "chilled curd rice has the longest life" (Section 7); "Needs a microwave counter and a vendor with ≥24 h chilled life" (Section 7); "Chiller: vendor's use-by date." (rulebook F4)

**What an operator knows.** A visi cooler is a drinks fridge: glass door opened 200 times a day, standing near a glass shopfront, running 8–12°C on a May afternoon in Hyderabad. Curd rice from a tiffin kitchen is packed warm at 5am and cooled on a bench; nobody pasteurises it; Indian curd keeps souring. In a visi cooler it is sour by 6pm. Sandwiches in a cold fridge harden; taken out into 35°C air they sweat inside the pack. No local vendor will sign for "24 h chilled life"; the Japanese 48-hour bento is a factory product with a cold chain from the mixing bowl.

**Why.** Day-food needs its own chiller: a small under-counter or glass-front food chiller with a real thermostat at 2–4°C, separate from the drinks visi, a dial thermometer inside at the warmest point (top shelf, door side) read at 8, 13, 18 and 22. Curd rice is a same-day item: sold until 21:00, then binned, no exceptions. "Vendor's use-by" for a tiffin kitchen is whatever they feel like writing; the store sets the clock.

### 5. "Every vendor pack carries an hour-level use-by" and "POS blocks sale after pull-by": the vendor will not label and the idli has no barcode

> "Every vendor pack carries an hour-level use by and an earlier pull by (use by minus 2 h); POS blocks sale after pull-by." (Section 8.7); "Who labels? Default: vendor labels every sealed pack (name, ingredients, veg/non-veg, net qty, packed-on date and time, use-by date and time, vendor FSSAI number)." (note 07, KQ9 Q13)

**What an operator knows.** The tiffin kitchen is two cooks and a helper working from 3:30am. They do not own a label printer and they will not write hour-level times on 60 cups at 5:30. Bakeries that do print labels print the date. Loose hot-case items have no barcode for a POS to block. Who labels is the store: the receiver writes load time and discard time on a coloured day-dot sticker (₹200 a roll) on every sealed cup and on a tray card for every hot tray. The realistic version of "POS blocks sale" is a time block per hot-case SKU, triggered by the receiver tapping the load time on the POS once per tray.

**Why.** Vendor labelling is better for liability, so insist on what a small kitchen can actually do: a rubber stamp with vendor name, FSSAI number, veg mark and packed-on date on every sealed cup, from month 1. The hour clock lives in the store for at least the first six months.

### 6. The per-item, per-reason POS waste log will be filled in at 22:15 by someone who wants to go home, and the 3–5% budget is a Japan number

> "Per-item, per-reason daily log in Consto POS; it is the ordering input." (12.4); "5% ceiling for 90 days, then 3%; zero waste means the case was empty." (12.4)

**What an operator knows.** The log that works is the closing count: at 22:00, before anything is binned or eaten, count every fresh item left, write it on the paper sheet pinned above the waste bin, photograph it into the WhatsApp group, then discard. Reasons are four, not twenty: clock expired, damaged or returned, temperature failure, left at close (over-ordered). The manager reconciles the photo with the POS the next morning, not the closer that night. On the budget: in month 1 every order is a guess and you cannot "sell out, then order more" of idli at 8:30. Hot-food waste will run 15–20% by value in month 1 whatever the plan says. Japan's 2–3% is after 40 years and with 87% of items having more than 24 hours of life.

**Why.** Plan 15% in month 1, 10% in month 2, 6–8% in month 3; a steady state of 5–8% on hot-case items is good. A founder who budgets 5% and sees 18% in week 2 will cut orders to zero and empty the case, which is worse.

### 7. "5 people, 2 on each shift, 1 relief, staff under 8% of sales" does not add up, and 24h is a roster question, not a fee question

> "The goal: run a 6 am–11 pm store with 5 people, with 2 on each shift and 1 for relief. Staff cost under 8% of sales." (rulebook 6)

**What an operator knows.** 6am–11pm is 17 hours, plus 30 minutes of opening and 45 of closing: about 18.25 hours of staffed time. Two people on the floor all day is 36.5 staff-hours a day, 255 a week. Telangana's Shops & Establishments rules cap a person at 48 hours a week with a weekly off. 255 ÷ 48 = 5.3 bodies with zero sickness, zero festival leave, zero training. Five people means somebody works a seven-day week by month 2 and quits in month 3. Real options: six on payroll for two-up all day; or a three-shift pattern (opener, split-shift floater, closer) with four on payroll, where one person is alone 5:30–7:30, 12:00–14:00, 14:30–17:00 and 21:30–23:15. Alone at the shutter counting milk at 5:45 is normal kirana life. Alone at 22:30 with a cash drawer and AEPS cash is not something I would do. On money: 8% of ₹30,000 a day is ₹72,000 a month, which is four people at ₹18,000 or five at ₹14,400. A person you trust with cash, AEPS, food hygiene and the WhatsApp agent costs ₹16–20k a month in the Hyderabad suburbs, plus a meal and a uniform. Budget 10–11% in year 1.

**Why.** On 24h the report's conclusion (6–11 for Store 1) is right, but for the wrong reason. 24h is not a ₹10,000 exemption; it is an eight-person roster and a single night worker holding cash and the AEPS float in a suburb with no other lit shop. No operator I know would run that alone.

### 8. Services on the same counter will stall the food queue at 8:15am

> "Services counter next to the snacks and tea machine." (rulebook SV1); "AEPS, BBPS, recharge, parcel holding and pre-orders should be live on day one, not Phase 2" (12.7); "all on the main L-counter, no separate desk" (note 09 Q24)

**What an operator knows.** An AEPS withdrawal takes 2–4 minutes: the fingerprint fails twice, the OTP, the cash count, the customer counts again. A bill payment is 2–3 minutes while the customer finds the consumer number on his phone. A parcel hand-over needs an ID check. At 8:15 with six people wanting chai and idli and one till, one AEPS customer stops the food line dead and the man at the back walks to the tiffin centre, and he does not come back tomorrow.

**Why.** Services live on the same counter but not during food peaks. A board: "Bill pay, AEPS, parcels: 10am–12:30 and 3–6pm; other times if the counter is free." A second POS position (tablet plus UPI stand) that the floater opens for food-only during the two peaks. AEPS cash in its own drawer so it never mixes with sales cash.

### 9. "Come rest even if you don't buy" will be taken literally by the people you least want in the seats

> "No purchase needed. Written on the wall. Staff never ask people to buy." (rulebook RS2); "6–10 seats facing the chai machine and hot case" (decision 59)

**What an operator knows.** In a Beeramguda-type suburb the six seats with AC, charging points and Wi-Fi are taken by 10am by delivery riders between orders, two auto drivers, and three college boys sharing one chai, and they are still there at 6pm. The mother with a child who would buy will not sit next to them. The washroom becomes the public toilet for the road; the hourly check is ticked on paper and the room is filthy by 11. Indonesia's 7-Eleven (Section 10.3) is exactly this story. Expect 60% of seat-hours to be non-buyers.

**Why.** Keep the promise, build friction. Stools, not chairs. Charging through a cable issued at the counter against a name. Washroom key at the counter. One line on the wall: "30 minutes at busy times, please." A greeting script ("Anna, chai?") that is hospitality, not pressure. The number to watch is chai cups per seat-hour, not footfall.

### 10. Vendors, yes; a dark kitchen, no; and the report misses the one exception worth making

> "Vendor or kitchen at store 1? Default: vendor (A). Trigger to revisit: 300–400 plates/day of combined demand or store 3 within 8 km." (note 07, KQ9 Q1); "Own kitchen fixed cost ₹1.15 lakh/month ÷ ₹9 = 12,800 plates/month ≈ 425 plates/day to break even" (note 07, KQ3)

**What an operator knows.** The report's direction is right. Its reason is incomplete. A dark kitchen for one store is two cooks; one quits in week 6 or goes to his village for a month at Sankranti, and the founder is at an idli steamer at 4am. That is the whole story of every small kitchen I have seen. The break-even maths also ignores that a kitchen serving three stores needs a rider doing three drops before 6:15, so the first store gets food at 5:30 and the third at 6:45, and the kitchen opens at 2:30am. On the vendor side, the report is too soft: the tiffin centre's morning batch is for its own counter first and you are its second priority; the price will be their retail (₹10 an idli) until you show you can walk; quality drifts by week 3 (smaller idli, less ghee) once they know you are locked in.

**Why.** Two tiffin vendors from day one at 70/30, not 80/20 (a 20% share is too small for the backup to care about your 6am). A one-page spec with weights (idli 60–70 g, vada 45–50 g, chutney 40 ml, sambar 80 ml), a weigh check on three pieces at every drop, 15 days' notice either way, daily UPI settlement. And one exception to "no cooking" that deserves a founder decision: an electric idli steamer (₹25k, no gas, vendor or iD batter) steaming a tray every 45 minutes during the breakfast run. No cook, no raw prep, no cutting. It is the single change that turns "idli from a case" into "idli from a tiffin centre," and I would take it over a dark kitchen every time. It breaks rulebook F1 by the letter, not the spirit.

### 11. "Vendors fill their own fridges and shelves" is two visits a week, and the "free" cooler is not free

> "Vendors fill their own fridges and shelves. Coke, Pepsi, Amul, ice-cream and bread reps restock their own space. Staff only check." (rulebook P3); "Expect 1–2 from Coke/Pepsi/Campa (free, with stocking exclusivity)" (note 09 KQ3)

**What an operator knows.** The Coke or Pepsi salesman comes twice a week with a delivery boy; he fills the cooler on his visit and your staff fill it from backroom crates on the other five days. Milk and bread arrive at 5:45 as crates at the shutter; the distributor's boy does not come inside. The free visi comes with a 100% exclusivity letter and a rep who photographs any Vijaya curd or Paper Boat inside and threatens to pull the unit.

**Why.** Budget staff time for all filling. Buy one own cooler for dairy and local drinks, take the brand units knowing what they are, and keep the food chiller (point 4) separate from both.

### 12. An 8pm markdown trains the suburb to shop at 8:05, and Robin Hood Army will not come for four puffs

> "Fixed window: 8pm, 30–35% off, tagged at pull-by minus 5 h, announced on WhatsApp" (12.4); "Donation at 10pm of still-safe food to a Robin Hood Army or IFSA partner, with a signed hand-over log" (9.5)

**What an operator knows.** In a working suburb 7–9pm is the evening peak: people coming home from Patancheru and the IT corridor stop for snacks, milk and a chai. A 30% sticker at 8pm teaches that customer to come at 8:05, and he tells his neighbour. Taiwan's 8pm window works in a city that eats dinner at 6. Robin Hood Army collects from restaurants with 50 meals; they will not send a volunteer to Beeramguda at 10pm for four puffs and two curd rice.

**Why.** Markdown at 21:00, 30% off, only on items with clock life left (puffs, buns, sweets cups, cut fruit, biryani), never on an item whose clock has expired: the clock beats the sticker. Unsold hot food at the 22:00 count becomes a logged, capped staff meal and a standing arrangement with the night watchmen of the two nearest apartment blocks; the vendor's cattle take-back covers rice. A donation call is for a festival over-order only.

## Part 2. The daily SOP

**Roster used here (4 on payroll for a "3-person" store):**
- **O = Opener**, 5:30–14:30
- **F = Floater**, split shift 7:30–12:00 and 17:00–21:30
- **C = Closer**, 14:00–23:15
- **M = Manager/founder**, on WhatsApp all day, in store Tuesday and Friday 10:00–12:00
- Each person works six days; the fourth person rotates through all three shifts as relief and does the weekly deep clean.

## Part 4. Decision questions from the operator's lens

Format: question, why it matters in one line, options, my pick with the assumption behind it.

### Staff and shifts

1. **How many on payroll for a 6am–11pm store?** Why: the rulebook's five people cannot cover 255 staff-hours a week under a 48-hour cap. Options: [4 with a split-shift floater and one-up at the edges / 5 / 6 with two-up all day]. My pick: 4 on payroll plus the founder trained on every job, moving to 5 when sales pass ₹40,000 a day (assumption: a 9-hour opener, a 9-hour split floater, a 9.25-hour closer, 6-day weeks; staff cost 10–11% of sales in year 1, not 8%).
2. **Shift pattern?** Why: two people are needed only at the two peaks. Options: [two straight 8.5-hour shifts / three overlapping shifts / opener + split floater + closer]. My pick: opener + split floater + closer as in Part 2 (assumption: the floater lives within 15 minutes of the store; a split shift is normal in Hyderabad retail and bakery work).
3. **Who is the shift lead?** Why: somebody signs the challan, the cash sheet and the waste sheet. Options: [the opener and closer are leads by default / one designated lead per day / the founder]. My pick: the opener is lead until 14:00, the closer after; the floater never signs (assumption: the two leads are paid ₹1,500 a month more and are the two people the founder trusts with the safe key).
4. **Handover format?** Why: an unlogged handover is where cash and clocks get lost. Options: [verbal / paper checklist / fixed WhatsApp template]. My pick: the six-point paper sheet at the till, both initial it, then the same six points as a fixed WhatsApp template in the group (assumption: 10 minutes at 14:00, never skipped, even on a busy day).
5. **Weekly off and festival leave?** Why: Sankranti and Dasara will empty the roster if nothing is agreed in writing. Options: [rotating off, festival leave by lottery / fixed off days / founder covers festivals]. My pick: rotating weekly off, festival leave requested 30 days ahead, maximum one person off per festival day, founder covers the gap (assumption: staff are from the district and will want 3–5 days at Sankranti; price that in).
6. **Early-shift premium?** Why: the 5:30 opener is the hardest seat to fill and the one that matters most. Options: [none / ₹50 a day / a fixed ₹1,500 a month]. My pick: ₹1,500 a month for the opener role (assumption: it cuts opener churn, which is the churn that breaks the morning drop).
7. **Uniform and hygiene rule?** Why: an inspector and a customer judge the case by the hands over it. Options: [t-shirt only / t-shirt + cap + apron / full uniform with gloves]. My pick: Consto t-shirt, cap, apron, two sets each, hair tied, no rings or watches at the hot case, nails short, hand-wash on arrival and every hour, gloves or tongs for every hot item, no phone at the counter except the store phone (assumption: ₹1,500 per person per year; the cap is the item people forget and the one inspectors notice).
8. **Medical fitness certificates?** Why: FSSAI Schedule 4 asks for them and a Hyderabad inspector does check. Options: [skip / once at hiring / annual]. My pick: at hiring and annually, from a named local clinic, ₹300 each, copies in the FSSAI file (assumption: 4–5 certificates a year).
9. **Who is FoSTaC trained?** Why: the Food Safety Supervisor certificate is asked for at a State Licence inspection. Options: [founder / both shift leads / everyone]. My pick: founder plus both shift leads before opening (assumption: one-day course, about ₹1,000 per head).

### Receiving and vendors

10. **Who receives food and signs?** Why: the person who signs is the person who can be asked why there were 54 idli, not 60. Options: [whoever is free / the shift lead only / the founder by photo]. My pick: the shift lead on duty, and only after the count, probe and weigh; photo of the signed challan to the group within 5 minutes (assumption: a vendor who learns the store counts every drop stops short-counting in week 2).
11. **Receiving temperature rule?** Why: a lukewarm idli at 6:10 is a 9am complaint. Options: [no probe / probe one item / probe two items + weigh three]. My pick: probe two (one wet, one dry) and weigh three pieces at every drop; accept ≥60°C hot and ≤10°C chilled; 55–60°C accepted with a 1-hour shorter clock and a scorecard mark; below 55°C refused (assumption: ₹1,500 digital probe, two of them, calibrated weekly in iced water).
12. **Delivery windows and the late rule?** Why: the window is the whole breakfast. Options: [any time before 6:30 / 5:45–6:10 with a penalty after 6:30 / the store collects]. My pick: 5:45–6:10 and 16:00–16:20; after 6:30 the drop is accepted but the day is marked "late" on the scorecard; three lates in a month moves 10% of share to the other vendor (assumption: the penalty is share, not money; small vendors respond to share).
13. **Vendor split?** Why: a backup that gets 20% will not wake up for you. Options: [one vendor / 80/20 / 70/30 / 50/50]. My pick: 70/30 from day one, reviewed monthly on the scorecard, able to go to 50/50 (assumption: two tiffin kitchens within 5 km will each take a ₹2,000–3,000 a day order).
14. **Vendor scorecard format?** Why: "he is unreliable" is not something you can show a vendor. Options: [none / monthly summary / weekly five-line score]. My pick: weekly, five lines (lates, short counts, temperature fails, weight fails, quality complaints), shared on WhatsApp every Tuesday with a number out of 10 (assumption: the scorecard is also the record if you ever need to end the contract).
15. **What is in the one-page vendor spec?** Why: without weights, the idli shrinks in week 3. Options: [verbal / a menu with prices / a signed spec]. My pick: signed spec: item, weight per piece, chutney and sambar volumes, oil and ghee, packing (sealed cup or steam tray), vendor stamp with FSSAI number and veg mark, drop windows, temperature at drop, price per piece fixed 12 months with 5% escalation, 10% return allowance for the first 30 days then firm sale, daily UPI settlement, 15 days' notice (assumption: the vendor is a tiffin centre with a Basic FSSAI registration; ask for the copy).
16. **Payment terms?** Why: a small vendor paid monthly pads the price for the credit. Options: [daily UPI / weekly / monthly]. My pick: daily UPI by 22:00 for the first 60 days, then weekly on Monday (assumption: daily settlement gets you the best price and the first batch).
17. **Backup for a no-show?** Why: it will happen in week 1 or on the first festival morning. Options: [empty case and apologise / second vendor standing order / chilled branded fallback]. My pick: all three in order: the second vendor is called at 5:30 if the first has not confirmed; iD batter plus the electric steamer if the founder allows it; otherwise puffs, buns, boiled eggs and a WhatsApp message to opted-in customers saying "no idli today, chai + puff ₹30" (assumption: a message that admits it keeps the customer; silence loses him).
18. **Hot boxes: who owns them?** Why: the vendor's dabba is the vendor's hygiene. Options: [vendor's own / Consto buys and lends / Consto buys and collects]. My pick: Consto buys six insulated boxes, lends four against a ₹2,000 deposit, keeps two; boxes come back washed or the drop is marked (assumption: ₹4,000 a box).
19. **Unannounced vendor visit?** Why: you are selling their kitchen's hygiene under your name. Options: [never / quarterly / monthly]. My pick: the founder visits each kitchen before signing and then monthly, unannounced, at 4:30am once (assumption: a kitchen that will not let you in at 4:30 is not a kitchen you want).

27. **Re-order trigger per item?** Why: the tray card is the order. Options: [gut feel / POS suggestion / tray-card rule]. My pick: tray-card rule until the POS has 4 weeks of data: sold out before 8:30 on 3 of 7 days = +20%; more than 30% left at the clock on 3 of 7 days = −25%; never cut an item to zero without a week's notice to the vendor (assumption: the Agent's weather and festival prompts layer on top from month 2).

## Part 5. The ten things that will go wrong in month 1, and the pre-agreed fix

1. **The tiffin vendor is late or absent on day 3 and again on the first festival morning.** Fix (agreed before day 1): vendor 2 is called at 5:30 if vendor 1 has not sent "leaving" by 5:20; the standing 30% order means vendor 2 is already awake; the fallback case is puffs, buns, boiled eggs and chai; the Agent sends one message to opted-in customers by 6:30 ("Lakshmi aunty, no idli today, chai + puff ₹30, idli tomorrow"); the late day goes on the scorecard and the month's share moves.

2. **The idli is dry by 8:15 and a customer says "stale", in front of the queue.** Fix: the wet zone (bain-marie with lids) is installed before opening, not after; the 3-hour clock is on the tray card; dried idli comes out on sight whatever the clock says; the complaint gets a replacement plate and a logged return; if it repeats in week 2, the steamer exception (Q53) goes to the founder for a decision that week.

3. **Hot-food waste is 20% in week 1 and the founder wants to cut orders by half.** Fix: the month-1 budget is 15% in writing; cuts are made per item by the tray-card rule (−25% on items with 30% left on 3 of 7 days), never across the board, never to zero; the case must look full at 7:30 even if it means waste at 9:15.

4. **The visi cooler reads 11°C at 15:00 and a curd rice cup comes back sour at 19:00.** Fix: day-food never lives in the visi; the food chiller is on the counter run from day 1; readings at 8, 13, 18, 22; if any reading is above 8°C, day-food moves to the freezer top shelf and curd rice sales stop at 15:00 that day; the refund is immediate and the vendor and batch are logged.

5. **Cash is short ₹300–800 on three nights in week 2.** Fix: the drawer rule (opens only on a sale), the three drop-safe times, the AEPS drawer and the written variance policy are all in place before day 1; the founder reviews CCTV for the three nights and has the conversation on day 4 of the pattern, not day 30.

6. **A staff member quits in week 3, or does not come in on a Sunday.** Fix: four on payroll for a three-shift store; the founder is trained on every job and can open; the opener premium is paid; notice is 15 days in writing with the final pay on the last day; the next hire is in the pipeline from week 1 (one trial shift a week for a candidate, paid).

7. **The morning food queue stalls behind an AEPS withdrawal and regulars walk out.** Fix: services hours are on a board on day 1; the second POS position (tablet plus UPI stand) opens at 7:30 and 17:00 for food only; AEPS cash is in its own drawer so the till is not opened twice.

8. **A two-hour power cut at 14:00 on a 41°C day.** Fix: the power-cut rule (Q31) is laminated on the wall; chiller doors are taped at minute 0; the hot case clocks are halved; at 60 minutes day-food goes to the freezer; at 120 minutes curd rice, cut fruit and salad are binned and logged as "temperature"; the inverter keeps the POS, router, lights and the food chiller running; the founder decides on a generator only after the outage log shows more than four cuts over an hour in a month.

9. **The rest corner has four non-buyers all afternoon, the washroom is dirty at 11, and a customer complains on WhatsApp.** Fix: friction is in the design before day 1 (stools, cable from the counter, washroom key, the 30-minute line); the washroom check is logged with the time and the three cleans are in the SOP; the closer's photo set includes the washroom every night; the founder answers the complaint himself within the hour.

10. **A cockroach or flies at the hot counter, and someone photographs it.** Fix: the pest contract starts before opening with a gel treatment and a certificate in the file; glue boards weekly; the fly screen on the back door; lidded bins emptied at 14:30 and 22:30; no cardboard overnight; cut fruit covered in the chiller; if it happens anyway, the counter is cleared and cleaned on the spot, the contractor is called the same day, and the reply to the photo is an apology with the pest certificate date, not a denial.

Three more I would keep on the list without a number: the vendor asks for a price rise in week 3 ("your volumes are small"), answered by the signed 12-month spec; the municipal or food inspector visits in week 5 and asks for the file, answered by Part 2's monthly list; the chai machine jams on a Monday morning, answered by the rental vendor's same-day service clause and a kettle-and-premix fallback behind the counter.
