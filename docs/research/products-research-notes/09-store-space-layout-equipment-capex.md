# 09 — Store space, layout, fixtures, equipment and capex for a 600–800 sq ft Indian convenience store

Research note for Consto (Beeramguda pilot). All ₹ figures are India, 2024–2026, with year and source marked. Three labels are used throughout:
- **[S]** sourced — a cited listing, press report or official document (listing prices are MRP/online prices, not negotiated prices).
- **[E]** estimate — my derived figure or an industry rule-of-thumb I could not verify with a citation in this session (web-search quota ran out mid-research; see Gaps).
- **[A]** assumption — a planning parameter the founder should confirm or override.

Scope: no cooking inside; hot case + chiller + shelf; small seating/rest corner; services counter.

## KQ1 — Space maths: SKU density, SKUs per gondola bay, running feet of shelf in 700 sq ft

### Takeaway
Indian small formats run 2.5–5 SKUs per gross sq ft (SuperK ~2,500 SKUs in 500–1,000 sq ft; Twenty Four Seven 3,500 FMCG SKUs in 500–2,000 sq ft), versus 7-Eleven Japan's ~1.9 SKUs/sq ft (~3,000 SKUs on a 150 sq m floor). A 700 sq ft Consto that gives ~160 sq ft to seating, hot food and a services counter should plan for **1,500–1,900 SKUs on ~85–90 linear ft of ambient shelf face plus 4–5 cold doors**, not 2,500.

### Cited Findings
- 7-Eleven Japan: standard new-store size changed in 2004 from 125 sq m to 150 sq m (≈1,345 → 1,615 sq ft); HQ offers ~5,000 SKUs and each store carries on average ~3,000 SKUs chosen for local demand; one source says stores "displayed about 2,500 items" — [Kellogg Seven-Eleven Japan case](https://kellogg.northwestern.edu/EMP/intranet/preenroll/Seven-Eleven_Final_0205.pdf); [Lal 2011 HBS note](https://gwern.net/doc/japan/2011-lal.pdf)
- A featured 7-Eleven "offers about 5,000 different items" on "only about 1,600 square feet" of selling area — [TJF Takarabako konbini article](https://www.tjf.or.jp/takarabako/japaneseculture/28konbini.htm)
- SuperK runs franchise-owned/operated stores "no bigger than 800 sq ft" (2023) — [Third Eyesight / SuperK playbook](https://www.thirdeyesight.in/tag/convenience-stores/)
- SuperK "operates compact 500–1,000 sq ft walk-in stores stocking roughly 2,500 SKUs" — [Weekday / SuperK job page](https://jobs.weekday.works/superk-category-lead-staples)
- Twenty Four Seven (K.K. Modi group) stores "range between 500 sq ft and 2,000 sq ft"; a Gurgaon outlet is 1,000 sq ft; stores stock 3,500 branded FMCG SKUs plus 3,500 pharmacy SKUs and ~300 private labels — [Retail4Growth 500-stores plan](https://www.retail4growth.com/news/twenty-four-seven-convenience-store-chain-to-open-500-stores-in-3-years-2437); [Retail4Growth Gurgaon outlet](https://retail4growth.com/news/twenty-four-seven-opens-gurgaons-first-outlet-with-new-design-2580); [Exchange4Media launch](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/k.k.-modi-group-forays-into-retail-venture-with-convenience-stores-16443.html)
- Reliance Smart Point stores are planned at 500–1,500 sq ft with ~45-day launch time from concept — [ITLN](https://www.itln.in/reliance-looking-for-thousands-of-spaces-to-set-up-small-grocery-stores-across-india-trade-e-commerce); [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/news/indias-reliance-retail-plans-to-open-small-grocery-stores/)
- Double-sided mild-steel gondola bays (4–6 shelves, 5 ft high) list at ₹5,950–10,000 per bay on IndiaMART/Aajjo (2025–26) — [IndiaMART double-sided display rack](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/double-sided-display-rack.html); [Aajjo centre gondola](https://www.aajjo.com/product/center-gondola-racks-in-surat-prayosha-steel-industries); [Aajjo Big Bazaar-type gondola](https://www.aajjo.com/product/big-bazaar-supermarket-gondola-rack)

### Inferences (derived; the geometry is [E]/[A], the inputs above are [S])
- **SKU density benchmarks (gross sq ft):** 7-Eleven Japan 3,000 ÷ 1,615 ≈ **1.9 SKU/sq ft**; Twenty Four Seven 3,500 FMCG ÷ 1,000 ≈ **3.5**; SuperK 2,500 ÷ 500–1,000 ≈ **2.5–5.0**. Indian small-box density is higher because facings are narrower (sachets, ₹5/₹10 packs, pegged items), ceilings allow 6–7 shelf levels, and there is no eat-in space. [E]
- **SKUs per 4 ft gondola bay [E]:** at an average facing width of 4 in (3 facings/ft) and 1 facing per SKU: 4 ft × 5 shelves × 3 = **~60 SKUs per side, ~120 per double-sided bay**. Dense kirana-style (6 shelves, sachets/small packs, ~4 SKUs/ft): ~70–75 per side, **~150 per double-sided bay**. Wide-facing categories (atta, oil, detergent, 2 facings/SKU, 4 shelves): **~25–35 per side**. Planning midpoint: **100–120 SKUs per double-sided bay, 50–60 per 4 ft wall section (5 shelves)**.
- **Running feet in 700 sq ft [E/A]:** modelled as 20 ft frontage × 35 ft depth (confirm with the actual shop). Back zone 5 ft deep (100 sq ft = 14%: back room 60, washroom 24, hand-wash/staff 16). Sales floor 20 × 30 = 600 sq ft. Chiller run across the back-room partition (20 ft, 2.5 ft deep, 50 sq ft). Left wall: hot-food counter 8 ft at the front + 18 ft of wall racking; right wall: seating corner 8 ft at the window + 14 ft of wall racking; front: door 4 ft + checkout/services counter 8 ft. Two 3-bay island gondolas (12 ft × 2 ft each) leave three aisles of ~4.3 ft each between 1.5 ft-deep wall racks (17 ft free width − 4 ft of islands = 13 ft ÷ 3). **Ambient shelf face ≈ 32 ft (walls) + 48 ft (two islands × 2 sides) + 8 ft (four end caps) = ~88 linear ft**; at 5 shelves ≈ 440 shelf-ft; at 6 shelves ≈ 530 shelf-ft.
- **SKU capacity [E]:** 88 ft × 5 shelves × 2.5–3 SKUs/shelf-ft = **1,100–1,300 ambient SKUs** (conservative, 1.2 facings/SKU) or 88 × 6 × 3.5 = **~1,850** (dense, 1 facing/SKU). Add 150–250 cold SKUs (3 visi doors ≈ 40–60 SKUs each + freezer 40–60) and 80–120 counter/impulse/tobacco SKUs → **1,350–1,600 (conservative) to 2,100–2,200 (dense)**. Plan the Consto master catalogue at ~1,800 and let the POS prune.
- **Aisle width [A]:** plan 3.5–4.5 ft (1.07–1.37 m) main aisles; absolute floor 3 ft (0.9 m) between fixtures for a single shopper with basket. (I could not fetch the NBC 2016 / Harmonised Guidelines accessibility clause this session — see Gaps; the commonly quoted minimum clear width for wheelchair passage is 900–1,200 mm.)
- Each 4 ft double-sided bay plus its share of aisle consumes ~4 ft × (2 ft + 3.5 ft) ≈ **22 sq ft of sales floor**. So every 60–80 sq ft of seating displaces ~3 bays ≈ 300–360 SKUs.

## KQ2 — Layout rules used by 7-Eleven / FamilyMart / Korean CVS / Indian small formats

### Takeaway
The konbini playbook is: pull traffic to the back (chillers/drinks), monetise the walk (snacks in the middle), convert at the till (hot snacks in a counter-side case, impulse at the register). The one evidence-backed caveat is that an impulse-beverage cooler placed at the entry or checkout queue moves 20–35% more units than one on the back wall — so Consto should do both: a destination chiller wall at the back and one small branded cooler at the till.

### Cited Findings
- 7-Eleven layout: fresh food and beverages "showcased right at the entrance"; snacks/drinks/essentials in the central aisles; a back wall for high-demand restricted items (tobacco, lottery in the US description); layout "designed to maximize efficiency while ensuring customers can easily find what they need" — [CSP Daily News](https://cspdailynews.com/technologyservices/bona-fide-c-store-expert-reveals-industry-secrets); [TJF konbini article](https://www.tjf.or.jp/takarabako/japaneseculture/28konbini.htm)
- Branded cooler placement "in the primary door-entry or checkout-queue position typically delivers 20–35% higher unit movement compared to coolers at the back wall or behind the counter" — [Rework.com cooler space management](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/fmcg-distribution-growth/cooler-chiller-space-management)
- FamilyMart's hot-food counter "is located next to the convenience store tills"; the Famichiki hot-snack display cases "found beside store registers" were restyled in 2026 for the snack's 20th anniversary — [Japan Travel Planning](https://japantravelplanning.com/family-mart-japan/); [Essential Japan](https://essential-japan.com/news/familymart-gives-famichiki-a-fresh-new-look-for-its-20th-anniversary/); [SoraNews24 2026](https://soranews24.com/2026/07/11/family-mart-changes-its-famichiki-packaging-for-the-first-time-in-nine-years/amp/)
- Hot snacks in Japanese CVS are ordered at the register from the counter-side case (staff-served, not self-serve) — [Tabimania Japan](https://tabimaniajapan.com/culture/food-culture/how-to-buy-hot-snacks-at-japanese-convenience-stores)
- Stores stock an "impulse zone" of common impulse purchases near checkout — [CSP Daily News](https://cspdailynews.com/technologyservices/bona-fide-c-store-expert-reveals-industry-secrets)
- 7-Eleven Japan is piloting larger-format stores (double the standard size) and a "SIP" next-generation store aimed at women and families with expanded fresh/eat-in offer (2024) — [P2PI](https://p2pi.com/7-eleven-format-doubles-size-pilot-store-japan); [NACS](https://www.convenience.org/Media/Daily/2024/Feb/29/5-Japan-7-Eleven-Tests-Store-Women-Families_Global); [SoraNews24 2024](https://soranews24.com/2024/03/15/7-eleven-opens-next-generation-sip-convenience-store-in-japan/amp/)
- Twenty Four Seven explicitly copied "the 7-Eleven formula" and sells conveniences such as movie tickets and ATM banking alongside FMCG — [Retail4Growth](https://www.retail4growth.com/news/7-eleven-formula-for-twenty-four-seven-547); [Exchange4Media](https://www.exchange4media.com/marketing-news/k.k.-modi-group-forays-into-retail-venture-with-convenience-stores-16443.html)
- Indian modern convenience formats carry "30 percent higher costs on rent, energy and other overheads like security guards, bar codes, ACs and bright lights" and throughput per sq ft often "does not justify the expenses" — [Fruitnet / Asiafruit](https://fruitnet.com/asiafruit/traditional-retailers-rebuff-big-chains/1418.article)

### Inferences
- **Consto zone order, front to back [E]:** (1) door + window seating bar on the right; (2) checkout/services counter immediately left of the door, with the hot case, chai/coffee machine and microwave on the counter run so hot items are staff-served FamilyMart-style; (3) impulse rail at the till (chocolates, gum, mints, batteries, sachets, tobacco behind the counter); (4) one branded single-door cooler beside the till (Coke/Pepsi/Campa-supplied) for the 20–35% uplift; (5) island gondolas: snacks and instant food on the aisle nearest the counter, household/personal care in the middle, pooja/baby/stationery on the far island; (6) destination chiller wall at the back: milk, curd, bread, eggs, juices, 3–4 doors + glass-top freezer.
- **Milk/bread/eggs:** the konbini rule puts them at the back to force the walk; the Indian speed-shopper counter-argument is that the ₹30 milk run must take <60 seconds. Recommended compromise [A]: milk/curd/eggs on the back wall but in a straight line of sight from the door, with bread on the end cap facing the door, so the milk run is ~8 steps with snacks on both sides.
- **Pooja near entrance [A]:** common Indian supermarket practice (agarbatti/camphor/oil at a front-of-store or entrance-side wall) — treat as an untested placement; A/B it after launch using POS basket data.
- **Seating by the window [A]:** Korean CU/GS25-style window counters and Japanese eat-in corners put seats at the glass to advertise occupancy to the street. I could not fetch a CU/GS25 design source this session (Gap); the placement is a design convention, not a measured finding.

### Gaps
- No source fetched on CU/GS25 eat-in corner sizing or on Indian store rules for pooja/tobacco placement (quota). Tobacco behind the counter is a legal requirement (COTPA point-of-sale display rules) — not verified this session.
- The "back wall = tobacco/lottery" line in the CSP piece describes US stores; in Japan tobacco is sold at the counter.
