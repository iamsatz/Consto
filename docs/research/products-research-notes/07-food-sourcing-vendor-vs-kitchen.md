# Food sourcing for a no-cook convenience store: vendors (A) vs own kitchen (B) vs hybrid (C)

Research date: 6 October 2026. Scope: a 600–800 sq ft store with a hot case and chiller, no cooking inside, selling idli/vada/upma/pulihora/curd rice/puffs/samosa/sandwich/biryani/sweets daily. Hyderabad/Telangana first, then India. Every number carries its year and source. "Est." = derived by me from cited numbers, not a published figure. "Anecdotal" = single-person or forum-grade source. "Search snippet" = the figure came from a search-engine summary of a page I could not open.

Related notes already in this folder (not repeated here): `04-fresh-food-ops-and-waste.md` KQ1 (7-Eleven Japan/Thailand/Taiwan central-kitchen structures), KQ4 (hot-case temperatures, 4 h/6 h holding clocks, Schedule 4 hygiene), KQ on waste (2–3% planned waste, vendor take-back); `06-india-cost-conscious-landscape.md` (Hyderabad tiffin retail prices ₹20–45, iD Fresh Hyderabad plant, batter micro-suppliers serving 15–20 shops each).

## KQ1. Who supplies ready hot food to shops/offices/canteens in India today, and at what price?

### Takeaway
Daily ready-food supply to third parties exists at four layers — batter/ready-idli micro-vendors (₹60/kg batter, ₹10 per ready idli with chutney and sambar at bulk-retail), contract canteen caterers (₹63 per breakfast plate and ₹31.50 per evening snack-plus-tea on a 2025 bank tender), branded chilled/frozen makers (iD Fresh serves ~30,000 outlets daily from four plants including Hyderabad), and chain central kitchens (Chai Point, Wow! Momo, 24Seven). Nobody publishes a shop-level B2B price list for idli/vada/samosa/puffs; the per-piece prices below are the closest published anchors, and the ₹5–8 idli / ₹6–10 samosa B2B range in the brief remains an unverified assumption.

### Inferences
- **Price ladder that the published anchors support (est., 2025–26):** a vendor's bulk-retail price for a ready idli with chutney/sambar is ₹10 and a vada ₹15; a canteen caterer delivers a full breakfast plate for ₹63 and a two-samosa-plus-tea plate for ₹31.50. A shop buying 100–300 pieces a day on firm sale should expect to land **idli at ₹6–8, vada ₹9–12, samosa ₹7–10, puff ₹10–14 (bakery), pulihora/curd-rice 250 g ₹22–32** — these are my inferences from the anchors above, not quoted prices; confirm with three quotes before using them in a model.
- At a Hyderabad retail ladder of idli plate (3 pcs) ₹30–35, vada ₹15–20 each, samosa ₹15, puff ₹20, pulihora 250 g ₹40, a bought-in plate costs the store **55–65% of retail** (gross margin 35–45%) if the vendor prices land in the ranges above, and 0–10% margin if the vendor charges its own retail-bulk price (₹10/idli = ₹30 per plate of 3). The margin exists only if the store negotiates a true wholesale rate.
- Batter arithmetic (est.): at ₹60/kg and ~25 idlis/kg, batter is ₹2.4 per idli; with the Quora ₹2 "visible cost" per idli and ₹4–5 of chutney/sambar per plate, a self-steamed 3-idli plate costs ₹12–15 in materials (34–43% of a ₹35 retail), which is the "25–35% food cost when self-made" band the brief assumed, at the high end.
- The batter/ready-idli trade is organised around returns: iD's early history (90% unsold returns) and its later 12.5% return rate show that fresh-food makers in India absorb unsold stock as a cost of entering a shop. Expect the same offer from a local batter or idli vendor; expect the price to be padded for it.

### Gaps
- No published shop-level B2B price list for idli, vada, samosa, puffs, pulihora or curd rice from a Hyderabad tiffin centre, mess caterer or bakery; the per-piece B2B ranges above are inferred. IndiaMART category pages (samosa, veg puff, ready idli) did not render through fetch tools.
- No source on Hyderabad-specific "mess" or tiffin-centre supply to kiranas/petty shops (only Bengaluru/Kerala batter examples). No evidence found that Sodexo-type corporate caterers sell to small shops.
- Karachi Bakery, Haldiram's, A2B, Sri Krishna Sweets, Milky Mist, Dodla, Heritage "fresh" ready-food B2B programmes: no public B2B terms found (A2B has 145–165+ outlets per [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adyar_Ananda_Bhavan) but no central-kitchen or wholesale data).

## KQ2. Trade terms: sale-or-return vs firm sale, credit, MOQ, delivery windows, packaging, labelling liability, redundancy

### Takeaway
Branded bread and batter are supplied on a returns model (shopkeepers return expired bread to distributors; iD Fresh took back unsold packets), general-trade FMCG has a formal brand/distributor expiry-returns matrix, and dairy franchisors (Heritage) supply strictly cash-and-carry with no credit. No written evidence was found for puffs/samosa sale-or-return from local bakeries, so that practice must be treated as anecdotal/industry-common rather than documented.

### Cited Findings
- (Dec 2022, Bombay HC PIL) Shopkeepers "told the petitioner that after the expiry of use by date, they **return the bread packets to the distributors**"; a bread manufacturer said it sells "expired and market-returned breads" to vendors for cattle feed and "all manufacturers adopt the same method" — [LiveLaw, 16 Dec 2022](https://www.livelaw.in/news-updates/bombay-high-court-bread-manufacturers-bakery-items-stale-unhygienic-best-before-216942)
- (2020) iD Fresh's early batter supply: 90 of 100 packets came back unsold; at scale, 500 kg of a 4,000 kg dispatch returned (12.5%) — [30 Stades](https://30stades.com/2020/03/11/how-a-daily-wage-earner-set-up-rs-250-crore-breakfast-company-id-foods)
- (Mar 2026, general-trade FMCG) Returns categories: transit damage, expiry returns, wrong SKU, quality claims. Responsibility matrix: **brand bears cost if over-distribution caused expiry; distributor bears it if it held stock too long; shared where both contributed**. Claims within 48 h for transit damage with photo/video and batch number; a 6–8% return rate in a new state equals ₹30–40 lakh on ₹5 crore sales — [Kirana Club, FMCG returns guide](https://kirana.club/resources/fmcg-returns-expiry-damages-gt)
- (2025–26) AICPDF (distributors' federation) has called for a national protocol for collection, withdrawal and destruction of expired/damaged food — [Agro & Food Processing](https://agronfoodprocessing.com/aicpdf-calls-for-nationwide-protocol-to-keep-expired-food-out-of-supply-chain/) (search snippet)
- (2025–26) Heritage Parlours: 859 parlours in Hyderabad, Visakhapatnam, Rajahmundry, Vijayawada, Tirupati, Chennai; 100 sq ft minimum; ₹10,000 interest-free refundable deposit; three-year agreement; "delivery of products will be done through the regular channel of the company on a **cash and carry basis** (we do not have a policy of supply on credit)"; franchisee buys its own deep freezer (300–400 L) and chest cooler/visi cooler; range includes curd, lassi, buttermilk, paneer, sweets (doodh peda, milk cake, gulab jamun, rasagulla) — [Heritage Foods, Heritage Parlour](https://www.heritagefoods.in/heritage-parlour)
- (2025–26) Chai Point channel-partner deck: distributors earn **20% of revenue** on Brewing Bot placements; distributor one-time investment ₹1.75 lakh, bot security deposit ₹10,000 per bot (₹5 lakh for 50 bots), working capital = 2 months of sales; "capability to invest ₹25 lakh in first year" and 1.2–2.5 lakh for an experience centre — [Chai Point Prospective Channel Partner Deck (PDF)](https://distributor.chaipoint.com/documents/Prospective%20Channel%20Partner%20Deck_New_Model.pdf)
- (2025) Arogya ready idli/vada: bulk only, minimum ₹500 per order, pre-order, "prepared fresh every morning", free delivery within 5 km — [Arogya Batters](https://arogyabatters.odoo.com/our-products)
- (2025 tender) Caterer paid "on actual plates delivered"; "number of plates per day may vary" — i.e., firm daily order with volume flexibility, no returns — [SBI tender sheet](https://sbi.bank.in/webapp/webfiles/uploads/files_2425/080120251650-Total%20Menu%20Cost%20For%20The%20Contract%20Perod%20pdf.pdf)
- (2021, FSSAI) Chapter 4, reg. 10: every packaged food meant for **non-retail sale** (e.g. a vendor's bulk tray to a shop) must carry name of food, net quantity, **FSSAI logo and licence number**, date marking, lot number, name/address of manufacturer or packer; ingredient list may be in accompanying documents — [FSSAI Compendium, Labelling & Display Regulations 2020, v.II 29.12.2021 (PDF)](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Labelling_Display_04_01_2022.pdf)
- (2023, FSSAI FAQ) The FSSAI licence number of the **brand owner** goes on the label; if the manufacturer/packer is different from the brand owner, their licence number is also displayed — [FSSAI FAQ on Labelling & Display, 01.11.2023 (PDF)](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FAQ_Labelling_Display_01_11_2023.pdf)
- (Oct 2021 onward) The 14-digit FSSAI licence/registration number must appear on every cash receipt, invoice and bill — [Petpooja, FSSAI logo rules](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/fssai-logo-rules-packaging-menu/)

### Inferences
- Three contract archetypes exist in the Indian market and the store will meet all three: (1) **consignment / returns** — bread, batter, branded bakery (cost padded into price; iD-style 10–15% return allowance at steady state); (2) **firm daily order, pay on delivered quantity** — canteen caterers and tiffin kitchens (no returns, 5% annual escalation is the norm written into tenders); (3) **cash-and-carry, no credit** — dairy franchisors. For hot tiffin the realistic ask is archetype 2 with a small return allowance on the first 30 days.
- Labelling liability splits cleanly: a vendor delivering sealed, labelled packs is the "brand owner/packer" and carries the label; the store is a retailer. A vendor delivering loose trays that the store portions and packs makes the **store the packer** — its licence number, date marking and veg/non-veg logo go on the pack and the store carries the liability. Choose the former for everything that can be packed at source.
- Two-vendor redundancy is cheap in this market because batter and idli makers already run multi-shop routes (15–20 shops per micro-supplier per note 06; 30,000 outlets for iD). Keep a chilled "day-2" fallback (iD batter + a steamer-less reheat plan, or Heritage/Vijaya curd-rice/sweets) rather than a second hot vendor.

### Gaps
- No documentary evidence for puffs/samosa/cake sale-or-return from local bakeries to kiranas or tea stalls; this is widely described as common practice but no source could be retrieved (searches returned only supplier directories). Treat as anecdotal; write a return % into any bakery contract instead of assuming it.
- No published credit-term norms for tiffin/caterer supply to shops (bank tenders imply monthly billing; small vendors likely want daily UPI). No MOQ data beyond Arogya's ₹500 minimum.

## KQ3. Own small central/dark kitchen (B): setup cost, running cost, cost per plate, licences, rental alternatives, published cloud-kitchen economics

### Takeaway
A 200–400 sq ft delivery kitchen in a Tier-2 location costs ₹5–8 lakh to open (₹10–15 lakh in a metro), runs on 2–3 staff with rent of ₹15–25k, and the industry rule is food cost at 28–32% of revenue; 25–30% of such kitchens close in year one. Shared kitchens rent at roughly ₹25–60k per month per slot and a full chain-grade central kitchen costs ₹1.6–2.1 crore, so the realistic "own kitchen" for a one-store founder is the ₹5–8 lakh dark kitchen, not a central kitchen.

**Variable cost per plate (materials + pack):** idli plate (3 pcs + chutney/sambar) ₹12–15; vada (2) ₹10–12; upma/pulihora/curd rice 250 g ₹12–16; samosa ₹5–6; biryani 350 g ₹40–50 (assumptions anchored on ₹60/kg batter, ₹2/idli visible cost, 28–32% industry food cost).

**Break-even store count (the decision arithmetic):**
- Take an average hot-food plate retailing at ₹35. Vendor firm price ≈ ₹20 (57% of retail, mid-point of the inferred B2B ranges). Own-kitchen variable cost ≈ ₹11 (31%). **Saving ≈ ₹9 per plate.**
- Own-kitchen fixed cost ₹1.15 lakh/month ÷ ₹9 = **12,800 plates/month ≈ 425 plates/day** to break even against vendors.
- Sensitivity: saving ₹6/plate → 640 plates/day; saving ₹12/plate → 320 plates/day.
- If one store sells 100–150 hot-food plates/day (assumption; a 600–800 sq ft store with a 3-tray hot case), the kitchen breaks even at **3–4 stores (range 2–6)**. Below that, the vendor is cheaper *and* carries the waste and staffing risk.
- Two things shorten the path: (a) selling kitchen output to third parties (other kiranas, offices, tea stalls — the same 15–20-shop route a batter micro-vendor runs) counts as "plates", so 200 plates/day of outside sales is worth two stores; (b) a shared kitchen slot (₹25–60k all-in, no capex) converts ₹6.5 lakh capex and ₹13k/month depreciation into rent, which lowers the break-even to roughly 250–350 plates/day but caps growth.
- Industry evidence agrees: Inc42's "three kitchens to survive" and Petpooja's 40–60 orders/day break-even are both statements that a single small kitchen does not clear its fixed cost on one outlet's demand.

### Gaps
- No Hyderabad-suburb commercial rent per sq ft for Beeramguda/Patancheru/Ameenpur was retrievable (search quota); ₹15–25k for 200–400 sq ft is the national Tier-2 band.
- No published price list for Kitchens Centre or Speed Kitchen in Hyderabad; the ₹25–60k and ₹50k–1.5 lakh figures are national and partly historic.

## KQ7. Risk and quality: vendor dependency, price hikes, hygiene incidents, FSSAI liability and labelling for loose vs pre-packed food

### Takeaway
The seller of food is the licensed FBO facing the consumer: the store must show veg/non-veg logos and allergen information on its menu boards (calorie counts only once it has a central licence or 10+ outlets), print its FSSAI number on every bill, and — if it packs vendor food itself — carry the pack label (name, date of manufacture, "use by", lot, licence number). Vendor risk is best managed by two makers per category, a written spec, a 5% annual escalation clause (the norm in tenders) and keeping pre-packed branded items as the fallback.

### Cited Findings
- (FSSAI 2020 regs, v.II Dec 2021) **Chapter 3, reg. 9:** (1) calorific value per serving on menus only for food service establishments with a central licence or outlets at 10+ locations; (2) **all** food service establishments shall mention on menu cards or boards (a) allergen information per reg. 5(14), which may be shown by symbols, and (b) the veg/non-veg logo; (3) exemptions for event caterers operating <60 days/year, free condiments, special orders; (4) nutritional information on request — [FSSAI Compendium (PDF)](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Labelling_Display_04_01_2022.pdf)
- (FSSAI 2020 regs) Pre-packaged food: "Date of manufacture or packaging" and "Expiry/Use by" shall be declared; "Best before" is optional in addition; special storage conditions must accompany the use-by; allowed abbreviations include "Use By", "UB", "UBD" and "BB" — [FSSAI Compendium (PDF)](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Compendium_Labelling_Display_04_01_2022.pdf); [FSSAI FAQ, Nov 2023 (PDF)](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FAQ_Labelling_Display_01_11_2023.pdf)
- (FSSAI FAQ 2023) Veg/non-veg logo cannot be exempted even on packs under 100 cm² "as this is a culturally sensitive issue"; the brand owner's licence number goes on the label and the packer's too if different — [FSSAI FAQ (PDF)](https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/FAQ_Labelling_Display_01_11_2023.pdf)
- (2020 regs) Non-veg symbol is now a brown triangle in a brown-outlined square; anything containing egg is non-veg; compliance deadline was 17 Nov 2021 — [Freyr Solutions](https://freyrsolutions.com/blog/decode-the-fssais-new-labeling-display-regulations) (search snippet); [Petpooja FSSAI logo rules](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/fssai-logo-rules-packaging-menu/)
- (2021→) FSSAI 14-digit number on every receipt since 1 Oct 2021; licence copy displayed on premises — [Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/finance-compliance/fssai-logo-rules-packaging-menu/)
- (2025 tender) Price revision norm: "rate per plate will be increased by 5% on yearly basis if agreement is renewed" — [SBI tender sheet](https://sbi.bank.in/webapp/webfiles/uploads/files_2425/080120251650-Total%20Menu%20Cost%20For%20The%20Contract%20Perod%20pdf.pdf)
- (2026) Returns responsibility: brand bears over-distribution expiry, distributor bears stock held too long; claims need photo, batch and delivery note within set windows — [Kirana Club](https://kirana.club/resources/fmcg-returns-expiry-damages-gt)
- (2025) Zepto Café's two stated failure modes were supply-chain strain and kitchen-staff shortage — [Franchise India, Jul 2025](https://www.franchiseindia.com/index.php/insights/en/news/zepto-caf-scales-down-amid-supply-chain-strain-kitchen-staff-shortage.56982)
- (2026) 25–30% of cloud kitchens close in year one — [Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/growth-scaling/start-profitable-cloud-kitchen-india/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2026/02/indian-cloud-kitchen-boom)

### Inferences
- **Loose vs pre-packed, in practice:** food displayed loose in the hot case and sold in a paper bag is "food service" — the store's board must carry the veg/non-veg logo and allergen symbols per item, and the store is the FBO of record. Food in a sealed container with a label is "pre-packaged" — the label must carry name, ingredients, net quantity, veg/non-veg, FSSAI logo and number of the packer, date of packing and use-by. Whoever seals the pack is the packer. The cheapest liability position is **vendor seals and labels at source** for anything that travels (pulihora, curd rice, biryani, sweets, sandwiches) and **loose hot-case display with a board** only for items eaten within hours (idli, vada, samosa, puff).
- Vendor-dependency controls that cost nothing: two makers per hot category (one primary, one 20% share so the backup route exists), a one-page spec (recipe, weight, pack, label, temperature at delivery, delivery times, return %, escalation 5%/yr), a chilled branded fallback (iD batter/parotta, Heritage curd and sweets), and the POS pull-by rule from note 04.
- The failure mode seen at Zepto/Bistro/Snacc — labour and consistency — is exactly what a vendor contract transfers away from the store; that transfer is worth paying the 20–25 percentage-point food-cost premium for until volume justifies a kitchen.

### Gaps
- No FSSAI text retrieved on whether a retailer repacking a vendor's bulk tray must also carry the original manufacturer's licence number; the FAQ only addresses brand-owner vs packer on a single label.
- No Telangana Food Safety enforcement data (inspection frequency, typical penalties for hot-case stores) was found.

## KQ9. Twenty-eight decision questions on food sourcing, each with a recommended default

### Takeaway
Default to vendors for everything in year one, with written specs and sealed-at-source packs for anything that travels; revisit the kitchen decision only on a demand trigger (≥ 300–400 plates/day across stores and B2B) rather than on a date.

### Cited Findings
- Defaults derive from KQ1–KQ8 (sources cited there).

### Inferences

1. **Vendor or kitchen at store 1?** Default: vendor (A). Trigger to revisit: 300–400 plates/day of combined demand or store 3 within 8 km.
2. **Which items from a hot-tiffin vendor vs a bakery vs branded chilled?** Default: tiffin kitchen → idli, vada, upma, pulihora, curd rice, biryani; bakery → puffs, samosa, cakes, sandwiches; branded chilled → iD batter/parotta, Heritage/Vijaya curd, sweets, Haldiram's/ITC frozen snacks for the late daypart.
3. **How many vendors per category?** Default: two (primary 80%, backup 20%) for hot tiffin; one bakery plus a branded frozen fallback; one dairy.
4. **Firm sale or sale-or-return?** Default: firm daily order on hot tiffin with a 10% return allowance for the first 30 days, then firm; sale-or-return on bakery (ask; bread/branded bakery already runs on returns); cash-and-carry on dairy (Heritage's own rule).
5. **Who absorbs unsold food after the return window?** Default: the store, budgeted at 2–3% of hot-food sales (note 04), with a 20:00 WhatsApp discount and an NGO hand-off.
6. **Price basis?** Default: per-piece wholesale price list fixed for 12 months with a 5%/year escalation clause (the tender norm); no monthly repricing.
7. **Credit terms?** Default: daily UPI settlement for the first 60 days, then weekly; never monthly with a small vendor.
8. **Minimum order quantities?** Default: commit to a daily minimum (e.g. 60 idli, 30 vada, 20 pulihora cups) that is 70% of forecast; order the rest by 20:00 the previous evening on WhatsApp.
9. **Delivery windows?** Default: 06:30 and 15:30 drops; a 19:00 top-up only if evening demand proves it.
10. **Delivery radius?** Default: vendor within 5–8 km (free-delivery norm is 5 km); if none exists, pay ₹150–250 per drop to a rider.
11. **Who owns the hot boxes?** Default: Consto buys 4–6 insulated boxes and lends them to the vendor against a deposit, so pack and temperature standards are Consto's.
12. **Packaging format?** Default: sealed labelled cups/trays at source for rice items, sweets, sandwiches, biryani; loose in steam trays for idli/vada/samosa/puff into the hot case.
13. **Who labels?** Default: vendor labels every sealed pack (name, ingredients, veg/non-veg, net qty, packed-on date and time, use-by date and time, vendor FSSAI number); Consto prints its own menu board with veg/non-veg and allergen symbols for loose items.
14. **Whose FSSAI number on the pack?** Default: vendor's (vendor is packer and brand owner); Consto's number appears on the bill and the premises licence. Do not co-brand packs until there is a written co-packing agreement.
15. **Use-by resolution?** Default: hour-level use-by on every pack, pull-by = use-by minus 2 h, enforced by POS (note 04).
16. **Hot-holding limits?** Default: 4 h fried, 6 h steamed/baked, ≥65 °C logged twice a shift (note 04).
17. **Reheat-to-order or hold hot?** Default: hold hot for the two peak windows; microwave/griller from chilled for 20:00–23:00.
18. **Chai: premix machine rent, buy, or fresh-brew vendor?** Default: rent a premix machine (₹700–2,500/month) for 3 months, buy (₹14.5–25k) once sales pass ~80 cups/day; add a Chai Point-type fresh-milk bot only if a B2B office account justifies it.
19. **Recipe ownership?** Default: Consto writes the spec (weight, spice level, oil, salt, chutney/sambar volumes); vendor cooks to it; one-page spec signed by both.
20. **Quality checks?** Default: probe temperature at receipt (≥60 °C hot, ≤5 °C chilled), weight check on 3 packs per drop, weekly taste panel, photo log on WhatsApp.
21. **Hygiene evidence from vendors?** Default: copy of vendor's FSSAI licence, medical fitness certificates for handlers, pest-control record, and one unannounced visit per quarter.
22. **Exclusivity?** Default: none asked, none given; instead a "first-call" clause so Consto gets priority on the morning batch.
23. **What triggers a kitchen?** Default: three conditions together — ≥ 300 plates/day combined demand, ≥ 2 stores open within 8 km with a third signed, and ₹10 lakh of spare cash after store working capital.
24. **Own kitchen or shared slot first?** Default: shared slot (if available within 8 km) for 6–12 months to prove the menu and the cook team, then own kitchen.
25. **Which items move in-house first?** Default: batter-based items (idli, vada, upma) and rice items (pulihora, curd rice) — highest volume, simplest process, biggest vendor markup; keep bakery and biryani outsourced longest.
26. **Should the kitchen sell to third parties?** Default: yes from day one — target 15–20 outside shops/offices on the same route (the batter micro-vendor model), because outside plates count toward the 300–400/day break-even.
27. **Staffing model for the kitchen?** Default: 2 cooks + 1 helper at ₹15k each with a documented recipe book so a cook can be replaced in a week; founder is not the cook.
28. **Kill criteria for the kitchen?** Default: if plates/day stay below 250 for 3 consecutive months or food cost exceeds 35% of retail after month 3, revert the kitchen to a vendor contract or sell its output B2B and stop supplying the stores.

### Gaps
- Defaults for items 2, 6, 8 and 23 rest on inferred wholesale prices and assumed per-store plates/day; the founder should replace them with three vendor quotes and the first 4 weeks of POS data.
