# 7-Eleven and FamilyMart: Country-by-Country Adaptation, and the 7-Eleven India Story (2019–2026)

Research date: 6 October 2026. All figures carry the date of the source. The India section reflects the breaking news (5–6 Oct 2026) that the Reliance–7-Eleven master franchise has ended and the last stores have closed.

---

## Key Question 1 — What is different, country by country, for 7-Eleven (Japan, Thailand, Taiwan, South Korea, Philippines, Malaysia, Indonesia, Australia, USA)?

### Takeaway
Every 7-Eleven market is run by a different local licensee (except the US/Canada and, since April 2024, Australia, which 7-Eleven International owns outright), and each one builds its store around a local hot-food hero (onigiri, toasties, tea eggs, triangle kimbap, siopao, Slurpee/halal snacks) plus a local services layer (bill pay, parcels, e-wallet top-ups). Store counts (end-2025 unless noted): Japan ~21,500, Thailand 15,945, South Korea ~12,000, Taiwan >7,300, Philippines 4,491, Malaysia ~2,650–2,700, Australia 763, USA ~12,000–13,500; Indonesia exited in June 2017.

### Cited Findings

**Global frame**
- 7-Eleven is the largest convenience chain on earth at roughly 85,000 stores in 20 countries/territories (Aug 2024) — [Wikipedia: 7-Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven); [Mappr](https://www.mappr.co/?p=75323)
- Seven & i targets ~100,000 stores in 30 countries by 2030; by store count (Aug 2025): Japan ~21,600, Thailand ~15,400, USA ~12,400, South Korea ~12,000 — [Bloomberg, 6 Aug 2025](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-08-06/seven-i-details-store-opening-targets-for-standalone-plan)
- 7-Eleven International (the Seven & i unit that handles everything outside Japan and North America) wants 11 new countries by FY2030 and is "specifically looking to find franchisee candidates" in Europe; it opened its first Israeli store in Jan 2023 — [C-Store Dive](https://www.cstoredive.com/news/7-eleven-to-expand-to-11-more-countries-by-2030/646999/)
- Seven & i was formed 1 Sept 2005 (7-Eleven + Ito-Yokado + Denny's Japan); Seven-Eleven Japan is the direct parent of 7-Eleven, Inc. (Irving, Texas) — [Wikipedia: 7-Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven)
- Franchise models vary: corporate-operated (Canada), licensed master franchises (most international markets), mixed company/franchise (Australia, Philippines, Thailand) — [Wikipedia: 7-Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven)

### Inferences
- The "7-Eleven" that Indian commentators compare against is really the Japan/Taiwan/Thailand model (fresh food, services counter, 24h, tiny box, dense network). The US model is a forecourt business that is itself being pulled toward the Asian model.
- Every durable market pairs a dominant domestic conglomerate licensee (CP, Uni-President, Lotte, Berjaya-era Malaysia) with 10+ years of patient build; markets with a weaker or distracted licensee (Indonesia's Modern Internasional, Future Retail, Reliance's five-year attempt) exit.
- Store-count maturity correlates with local hot food and services, not with imported icons; Slurpee/Big Gulp survive everywhere but are never the reason the store exists.

### Gaps
- Typical store size for Thailand, Taiwan, Korea, Philippines, Malaysia was not found in reliable English sources; only Japan (100–200 m²) and the India flagship (1,700 sq ft) are sourced.
- Exact Korea Seven store count at end-2025 was not found; press estimates say ~11,000 after closures.
- Thai franchise fee/royalty details come from a franchise-advisory blog, not CP All; treat as indicative.
- The controlling shareholder of 7-Eleven Malaysia Holdings (widely reported as Berjaya group) was not confirmed in the pages fetched.

## Key Question 2 — What is different for FamilyMart (Japan, Taiwan, Thailand, Philippines, Malaysia, Vietnam, Indonesia, China), including the Thailand exit?

### Takeaway
FamilyMart (Itochu-owned since 2020; 24,620 stores at Feb 2025, 25,263 at Feb 2026) grows through local joint-venture partners and has exited where the partner lost interest or money: South Korea (2014, became CU), Thailand (2020 stake sale, brand gone by end-2023, converted to Central's Tops Daily), and effectively the Philippines (Phoenix Petroleum let the network shrink from 2022). Its strongest adaptations are Taiwan (bill-pay leader, bento, halal-friendly SE Asian ranges), Malaysia (soft-serve, no alcohol, JAKIM-certified FamiCafé) and Indonesia (Wings Group, 270+ stores).

### Inferences
- FamilyMart's exits (Korea, Thailand, Philippines, US) are partner-driven: when the local partner can run the store without the brand (BGF→CU, Central→Tops Daily) or runs out of money (Phoenix/Udenna), the Japanese brand leaves and the boxes stay open under a local name. The brand licence is therefore a weaker moat than the local supply chain.
- Malaysia shows the cleanest "religious-first" adaptation in either chain: drop alcohol entirely, certify the café, and let JAKIM dictate layout.

- The Wikipedia claim that all Philippine FamilyMart stores closed by ~2025 could not be confirmed by a news source.

## Key Question 3 — 7-Eleven India under Reliance (Oct 2021 – Oct 2026): locations, store count, products, hours, pricing, economics, why it failed, and what analysts/customers said

### Takeaway
Reliance Retail's 7-India Convenience Retail Ltd opened the first store in Andheri East on 9 Oct 2021 under a five-year Greater Mumbai master franchise, peaked at roughly 55–63 stores (49 Mumbai + 6 Pune in July 2024; Navi Mumbai, Thane belt and a Bengaluru presence reported later), and closed the last 31 stores by end-September 2026 as the agreement expired. FY2025-26 revenue was ~₹92 crore against a ~₹90 crore loss; cumulative losses over FY23–FY26 exceed ₹200 crore. The format sold Indian hot snacks (samosa, vada pav, sandwiches, rice bowls, chai, coffee) alongside Slurpee/7-Select, but could not cover rent, staff and distribution with 60 stores against kiranas (75% of packaged-goods sales) and 10-minute quick commerce.

- Reliance never left the Greater Mumbai master-franchise territory in a meaningful way: 55 of 55 stores were in Mumbai/Pune in July 2024, and even the 2026 closure list is Mumbai, Navi Mumbai, Pune plus a Bengaluru mention. Density (the Asian model's precondition) was never achieved: Japan has ~2,800 stores in Tokyo alone versus ~50 in Mumbai.
- The P&L shows a fixed-cost format: in FY25, rent, electricity and store running were ~44% of revenue and employee cost ~29%, before distribution and marketing. Per-store revenue was roughly ₹1.1–1.2 cr/year (₹63.6 cr across ~55 stores), i.e. ~₹30,000–35,000/day — an order of magnitude below Japan's ~¥650,000 (~₹3.5–4 lakh)/day.
- "Income from services" collapsing from ₹18.5 cr (FY24) to ₹1.2 cr (FY25) suggests a services/fee line (possibly intra-group or launch-related) was discontinued, leaving pure merchandise sales; the services counter that anchors Asian 7-Elevens never materialised in India.
- The 24h-to-midnight retreat is the clearest symptom: the Asian model's night economics do not hold where kiranas close late and q-commerce runs to 1 am.

## Key Question 5 — How do the Asian chains handle vegetarian, halal and religious adaptation (Malaysia, Indonesia, Taiwan, India)?

### Takeaway
Halal is handled at three levels: product sourcing (Malaysia 7-Eleven uses halal-certified suppliers but, because it also sells non-halal items, cannot itself be JAKIM-certified), format redesign (FamilyMart Malaysia removed alcohol chain-wide and certified its FamiCafé counters under JAKIM-guided layouts), and regulation shocks (Indonesia's 2015 minimarket alcohol ban hurt 7-Eleven's hangout model). Vegetarian adaptation is visible in Thailand's vegan ready-meals and Taiwan FamilyMart's pork-free lines; India's 7-Eleven ran veg and non-veg hot snacks side by side.

### Cited Findings
- Malaysia (7-Eleven): JAKIM rule — "companies that produce halal and non-halal products ... are not eligible to apply for Malaysian halal certification"; in the Nov 2022 Carlsberg Nutrimalt controversy 7-Eleven said the manufacturer confirmed the product was non-alcoholic and that all products "undergo extensive processing that ensures safety for consumption by all of its customers" — [Says.my](https://says.com/my/news/7-eleven-clarifies-issues-of-selling-non-halal-carlsberg-nutrimalt-drink)
- Malaysia: Muslim population >60%; Malaysian halal standards perceived as stricter than other Islamic countries; "pork-free" labels are not halal and the government says certification remains voluntary (Nov 2024) — [Bank of Scotland trade guide](https://www.bankofscotlandtrade.co.uk/en/market-potential/malaysia/distribution); [Malay Mail, Nov 2024](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2024/11/13/govt-has-no-plans-to-enforce-halal-cert-for-pork-and-alcohol-free-restaurants-says-it-remains-voluntary-says-religious-affairs-minister/156689)
- Malaysia (FamilyMart): alcohol discontinued in all 360 stores; FamiCafé Shah Alam first JAKIM-certified convenience-store café; target 300 certified FamiCafés by 2025; new FamiCafés adopt JAKIM-guided layouts; halal-only ingredients in food service — [Says.my](https://says.com/my/news/familymart-discontinues-the-sale-of-alcoholic-beverages-across-all-outlets-in-malaysia); [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/familymart-famicaf%C3%A9s-is-malaysia-s-first-halal-certified-convenience-store); [Wikipedia: FamilyMart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FamilyMart)
- Indonesia: the 2015 ban on alcohol sales at minimarkets intensified competition and removed a key margin line for 7-Eleven's hangout format — [Marketing-Interactive](https://www.marketing-interactive.com/7-eleven-indonesia-operator-to-shutter-all-outlets-in-the-country); FamilyMart Indonesia (Wings Group) continues to expand to 270+ stores — [Wikipedia: FamilyMart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FamilyMart)
- Taiwan: FamilyMart sells Southeast Asian foods and "halal-friendly, pork-free options" for migrant workers — [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.asia/2025/09/19/family-mart-to-open-100-stores-in-taiwan/)
- Thailand: 7-Eleven's ready-to-eat upgrade "extends to vegan and plant-based meals" — [Inside Retail](https://insideretail.asia/2024/06/26/from-drab-to-trendy-7-eleven-strives-for-relevance/)
- India: veg items (samosa, veg vada pav, veg cheese sandwich, paneer tikka sandwich) and non-veg (chicken pops) sold in the same store — [Zomato Pune](https://www.zomato.com/pune/7-eleven-1-viman-nagar); [Zomato Andheri East](https://www.zomato.com/mumbai/7eleven-1-near-andheri-east-station/reviews)

### Inferences
- The chains adapt by subtraction (no alcohol, no pork) and by certification of the hot-food counter, not by changing the brand. In India the analogous move would be a certified pure-veg hot counter, which 7-Eleven India did not attempt.

### Gaps
- No source found on 7-Eleven Indonesia's restaurant-licence issue or on vegetarian-specific merchandising rules in India.

## Key Question 6 — What stays the same across countries, and what are the recurring rules of localisation?

### Takeaway
Constant everywhere: the brand/stripes, a tiny box, long hours, fresh-food emphasis, item-by-item ordering from POS data, daily commissary deliveries, and a handful of icon products (Slurpee, Big Gulp/Big Bite). Variable everywhere: the hot-food hero, the services counter, the payment rail, and who owns the licence. The documented rule is not "70/30" but "national core + 30–50% region-limited items, chosen store by store".

### Cited Findings
- Core system: POS-driven ordering since 1982 (Japan); "tanpin kanri" keeps a consistent national selection alongside location-specific items; region-limited items are 30–40% of the lineup, heading to 50% — [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01982/seven-eleven%E2%80%99s-japanese-half-century-a-store-on-every-corner.html); [NACS 2020](https://www.convenience.org/Media/Daily/2020/Jul/28/6-One-Size-Doesnt-Fit-All-CStores_International)
- In the US the same principle is the "Retailer Initiative": consumer and sales data plus the store owner's community knowledge, item by item; national staples (pizza, wings, Big Bite) plus local heroes; commissaries/bakeries deliver fresh daily — [NACS, Nov 2025](https://www.convenience.org/stay-current/news/2025/november/20/1-7-eleven-local-approach-global-food_fs)
- Brandon Brown (SVP fresh foods): "Catering to local tastes is key to delivering a great customer experience" — [NACS, Nov 2025](https://www.convenience.org/stay-current/news/2025/november/20/1-7-eleven-local-approach-global-food_fs)
- Winners travel: Korean bestsellers brought to Canada; 7-Eleven US borrowing international hits — [Retail Insider](https://retail-insider.com/?p=207498); [NACS, Jul 2024](https://www.convenience.org/Media/Daily/2024/July/31/5-7-Eleven-Looks-to-International-Hits_FS)
- Local payment rails are standard: Octopus in Hong Kong (from July 1999), GCash in the Philippines (Feb 2020), Touch 'n Go reloads in Malaysia, CLiQQ app in the Philippines — [Wikipedia: 7-Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven); [7-Eleven Malaysia](https://www.7eleven.com.my/all-about-7eleven/); [FreshPlaza](https://www.freshplaza.com/asia/article/2103648/philippines-7-eleven-expands-to-food-business/)
- Local services counter is standard in Asia: Japan bills/ATM/documents; Thailand Counter Service; Taiwan ibon; Philippines CLiQQ/ATM; Malaysia bill pay/reloads — sources as cited in Q1
- Licence sits with a domestic conglomerate: CP All (Thailand, plus Cambodia/Laos), PCSC/Uni-President (Taiwan, majority of Philippine Seven), Lotte's Korea Seven, DFI (Singapore/HK), Reliance (India 2021–26), QL Resources (FamilyMart Malaysia), Wings Group (FamilyMart Indonesia), Central (FamilyMart Thailand until 2023) — [Wikipedia: 7-Eleven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven); [Wikipedia: FamilyMart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FamilyMart); [Franchising.com](https://australia.franchising.com/news/20200901_7eleven_inc_continues_global_expansion_signs_agreement_with_cp_all_to_opera.html)
- Hot-food hero is always local and often predates the parent's approval: Taiwan tea eggs (1981) over US objections; Japan onigiri (1978); Korea triangle kimbap; Thailand toasties/salapao; Philippines siopao/rice meals; Australia $1 coffee and Krispy Kreme; India samosa/vada pav — sources as cited in Q1 and Q3
- Hours: 24h is the Asian default (Japan, Thailand mandatory for franchisees, Malaysia "largest 24-hour operator", Philippines majority); Australia mixed; India retreated to midnight — [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01982/seven-eleven%E2%80%99s-japanese-half-century-a-store-on-every-corner.html); [Aster of Asia](https://asterofasia.com/blog/7-eleven-franchise-thailand-investment-guide-tg1227); [7-Eleven Malaysia](https://www.7eleven.com.my/all-about-7eleven/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7-Eleven)
- Exits share a pattern: Indonesia (licensee out of cash, failed sale), India 2019 (licensee out of cash, zero stores), India 2026 (licensee chose q-commerce), FamilyMart Thailand/Korea (partner kept the boxes, dropped the brand) — sources as cited in Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4

**Table 4 — Recurring localisation rules (evidence-based)**

| Rule | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Keep a national core, flex 30–50% by store | NACS 2020 (Japan region-limited 30–40% → 50%) |
| Local hot food is the anchor, launched in year 1–7 | Taiwan tea eggs 1981; Japan onigiri 1978; Korea kimbap; PH siopao |
| Services counter on local rails (bills, tickets, e-wallets, ATM) | Japan 1987+, Taiwan ibon 2006, Thailand Counter Service, PH CLiQQ/GCash, MY Touch 'n Go |
| Licence held by a deep-pocketed domestic group that will fund 10+ years of density | CP All, Uni-President, Lotte, DFI; failures where this was absent (Modern Internasional, Future, Reliance's 5-year cap) |
| 24h or near-24h | Japan, Thailand (mandatory), Malaysia, Philippines |
| Religious adaptation by subtraction and counter certification | FamilyMart Malaysia (no alcohol, JAKIM FamiCafé); Taiwan pork-free |
| Icons kept but not relied on | Slurpee/Big Gulp present in MY, PH, ID, IN; none of those markets grew on them |
| Density before breadth | Japan 2,824 stores in Tokyo; Reliance's "every nook and corner of Mumbai and Pune" plan never got past ~50 |

### Inferences
- The "70% global / 30% local" formulation often quoted in Indian commentary has no primary-source basis in this research; the documented numbers are the inverse for fresh/regional items (30–50% local) with an even higher local share in food.
- For an Indian neighbourhood format the transferable rules are: a certified local hot counter, a services/payments layer on UPI rails, 16–24h hours, and a licence-owner-style commitment to a 10-year cluster build — all four of which 7-Eleven India lacked by 2026.

### Gaps
- No Seven & i document was found that defines a global "core" product list or a global/local percentage; the 30–50% figures are for Japan's domestic chain and the US examples are qualitative.
- Thailand's "All Online"/7-Delivery and Korea's delivery services were not covered by fetched sources.
