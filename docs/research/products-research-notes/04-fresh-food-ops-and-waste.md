# Fresh and ready-to-eat food operations and food waste at Asian convenience chains (7-Eleven JP/TH/TW, FamilyMart, Lawson) — with the India angle

Scope note: researched 2026-10-06. Every number carries a year and country. "Standard" = chain-wide practice; "Pilot" = trial or regional. Where a figure comes only from a secondary/aggregator source or from staff testimony, it is flagged. Several primary pages (Japan Today, SCMP, CP All English site, FSSAI Schedule 4 PDF) were blocked or image-only, so some items are listed as gaps rather than guessed.

---

## KQ1. Central kitchens / dedicated factories — who owns them, how many, how far

### Takeaway
Japan's model is **third-party-owned but dedicated** plants (Seven-Eleven's NDF network of ~170 factories, 93% of them serving SEJ only), not in-house kitchens. Thailand's model is the opposite — **CP All owns CPRAM**, a captive ready-meal manufacturer. In both, food is made daily and shipped the same day through temperature-controlled combined distribution centres.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan, 1979→2024) Seven-Eleven Japan (SEJ) set up the Nihon Delica Foods Association (NDF) in 1979 as a club of independent food makers; NDF had **172 factories** nationwide as of Feb 2024 — [Seven & i, "Further Strengthening the Domestic CVS Food Sector", Aug 2024](https://www.7andi.com/en/company/qr/163/1.html)
- (Japan, FY2017) Original daily foods are made in facilities "operated by independent collaborating companies" and shipped from combined distribution centres with multiple temperature zones; **165 dedicated facilities = 93.2% of all facilities** served SEJ only — [Seven & i, Group Merchandising Initiatives 2018 (PDF)](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_template_/_res/en/ir/library/co/pdf/2018_05.pdf)
- (Japan) SEJ's logistics page: distribution centres are "positioned near store locations to minimise delivery time"; one truck carries multiple temperature zones; ~68% of fresh-food delivery vehicles are multi-temperature — [SEJ, 安全とおいしさをお届けする物流](https://www.sej.co.jp/products/anshin/quality/quality_delivery/)
- (Thailand) CPRAM is CP All's ready-meal arm; its Chonburi plant/innovation centre is described as one of Thailand's most modern food factories — [Manufacturing.net, "CPRAM's Chonburi Province Facility"](https://www.manufacturing.net/operations/article/13166824/fm-in-focus-cprams-chonburi-province-facility)
- (Thailand, c.2018–2020) CPRAM had **five active factories** and planned to expand three and add two more; a later CPRAM company history says it planned to expand **6 existing factories and build 7 new ones** to support 7-Eleven's shift to ready meals — [The Nation (CPALL)](https://www.nationthailand.com/life/30195939); [CPRAM 35-year e-book, p.71](https://www.cpram.co.th/en/ebook/35-year-cpram-en/files/basic-html/page71.html) (page returned 404 on fetch; figure is from the search index — treat as unverified)
- (Thailand) CPRAM "produces fresh food every day" and 7-Eleven outlets "receive the products daily"; CPRAM makes chilled and frozen ready meals that are microwaved — [SCMP country report 2018](https://www.scmp.com/country-reports/country-reports/topics/thailand-business-report-2018/article/2159760/cpram-elevates) (page blocked on fetch; from search snippet)
- (Taiwan) Uni-President Enterprises runs 7-Eleven Taiwan via President Chain Store Corp (PCSC) and is Taiwan's largest retail operator — [Taipei Times, 17 Oct 2022](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2022/10/17/2003787193)
- (Taiwan) Fresh food (鮮食) at convenience stores has a shelf life of "only 24 hours" and accounts for 80–90% of the waste reported by major supermarkets and convenience stores; monthly fresh-food waste exceeds 500 t per Taiwan EPA statistics — [FoodNext (Taiwan)](https://www.foodnext.net/news/newstrack/paper/5098690421) (search snippet; year not stated)

### Inferences
- The Japanese "dedicated third-party" structure means the chain never owns a kitchen but dictates recipe, hygiene and delivery spec — closest to the founder's "no cooking in store, vendor/central kitchen" plan. The chain's leverage comes from volume and from NDF-style shared standards, not ownership.
- Thailand's captive-CPRAM model is what a chain does once it has thousands of stores; at one store it is irrelevant.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** start with 2–4 dedicated-ish vendors (a tiffin/cloud kitchen, a bakery, a snack fryer) on a written spec (recipe, pack, label, delivery time/temperature). Distance matters less than daily production and the number of delivery windows.

### Gaps
- No source gave average factory-to-store distance or transit time for SEJ, FamilyMart or Lawson; FamilyMart's and Lawson's dedicated-plant counts were not found in this pass.
- Uni-President's fresh-food plant count and delivery structure (統一武藏野 etc.) was not found.

## KQ2. Delivery frequency and temperature zones

### Takeaway
SEJ runs **four temperature bands** (rice/~20°C, ambient, chilled, frozen); rice items moved to **3 deliveries/day in 1987**, trucks per store fell from **~70/day (1974) to 9/day (since 2015)**, and from 2019 SEJ has been moving rice items to **2 deliveries/day** by shifting bento to chilled (5°C) with ~48 h more life.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan) SEJ products are managed in **four temperature divisions: rice items (米飯), ambient (常温), chilled, frozen** — [SEJ sustainability, 配送の効率化](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/action/000123/)
- (Japan, 1987) Joint delivery of rice items at **3 deliveries/day** started March 1987 — [Seven & i, 2018 company feature](https://www.7andi.com/company/challenge/2989/2.html)
- (Japan, 1974→2015) About **70 trucks/store/day at founding (1974)**; **9 trucks/store/day since 2015** — [SEJ logistics page](https://www.sej.co.jp/products/anshin/quality/quality_delivery/) and [SEJ sustainability](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/action/000123/)
- (Japan, 2025) ~3/4 of SEJ's ~21,000 stores are served by the 9-truck pattern — [SEJ logistics page](https://www.sej.co.jp/products/anshin/quality/quality_delivery/)
- (Japan, 2019, Pilot→rollout) From July 2019 SEJ began in Okinawa a shift from 3 to **2 daily-food deliveries**: daily-food SKUs cut from 120 to 80–100, order cut-off moved 11:00→09:00, trucks per store **9→6**; onigiri and sandwiches kept 3 deliveries; plan to extend to all ~20,000 stores if successful — [MD Next](https://md-next.jp/13575)
- (Japan, 1987 vs 2019) In 1987 **zero** daily items had >24 h shelf life after store arrival; by 2019 **>70%** did — [MD Next](https://md-next.jp/13575)
- (Japan, 2019) Technology enabler: moving bento from **~20°C "rice temperature" (~15 h life) to 5°C chilled added ~48 h** of sellable life — [MD Next](https://md-next.jp/13575)
- (Japan, May 2023) Nikkei reported SEJ cutting deliveries from 4 to 3 per day ahead of the 2024 trucking-hours problem — [Nikkei, 1 May 2023](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOUC015AE0R00C23A5000000/) (headline only; paywalled); JOQR radio reported onigiri/bento production reductions and 3→2 deliveries — [JOQR](https://www.joqr.co.jp/qr/article/174803/) (headline only)
- (Thailand) 7-Eleven Thailand stores all have a dedicated frozen cabinet; frozen meals are heated in store by staff on request, each pack with a sauce sachet and fork — [CK Travels, 7-Eleven Thailand](https://www.cktravels.com/thailand-7-11-eleven-what-you-can-buy/) (travel blog; practice confirmation only)

### Inferences
- The direction of travel in Japan is **fewer, colder deliveries** — chilled (5°C) and frozen formats are replacing the 20°C "fresh rice" band because labour and trucks are scarce. Thailand went straight to chilled+frozen with counter microwaving.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** plan for **2 delivery windows/day** (before breakfast and before evening), not 3; standardise on **two bands only — chilled ≤5°C and hot-case** — with frozen as a third once a microwave counter exists. Do not try to run a 20°C "fresh rice" band; it has the shortest life and the highest waste.

### Gaps
- Exact °C for the SEJ "rice temperature" band is 20°C per MD Next; SEJ's own page does not publish band temperatures.
- FamilyMart and Lawson delivery frequencies per band were not located.

## KQ3. Shelf life, time-stamping, sales-deadline rules, the 1/3 rule and the chilled-bento shift

### Takeaway
Japanese chains stamp a **consumption deadline (消費期限, hour-level)** on daily food and pull items from shelf at a chain-set **sales deadline — SEJ: 2 hours before**. The chains have driven waste down mainly by **lengthening shelf life** (87% of SEJ daily items now >24 h) and by relaxing the **1/3 rule to 1/2** for ambient packaged goods.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan, Standard) SEJ: "Some foods such as onigiri, bento, sandwiches, sweets and bread have a sales deadline set **2 hours before the consumption deadline**" — [SEJ customer FAQ](https://faq.sej.co.jp/article/?knowledge_id=cidqbpkvr0h8m5o3rj00)
- (Japan) Example: an onigiri expiring 04:00 on the 16th is removed at 02:00; each chain fixes its own removal offset — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News Japan](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/d11fe52f5b51f7b18fc63a4ee76efff48d7b081e)
- (Japan) Consumption deadline (消費期限) = safety limit, used for items that keep ≤ ~5 days (bento, onigiri, sandwiches, deli, cream cakes); best-before (賞味期限) = quality limit — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News Japan](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/d11fe52f5b51f7b18fc63a4ee76efff48d7b081e)
- (Japan, Standard) Staff check perishables **3–4 times a day** by reading the back label (e.g. "(16)2" = discard 16th, 02:00); a full pass takes "ten-odd minutes"; ambient snacks use month/year dates and chain rules like "pull 2 weeks before expiry" — [Monomax, ex-store-manager explainer](https://monomax.jp/archives/183313/)
- (Japan, Feb 2025) **~87%** of SEJ original daily products now have a sales deadline more than 24 h away — [Seven & i, Food loss measures](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)
- (Japan, 2009→) SEJ has extended freshness of chilled bento, onigiri, gratin/doria, pasta, sandwiches since 2009 by changing processes and temperature/hygiene control, without preservatives — [Seven & i, Food loss measures](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)
- (Japan, 2025) SEJ extended the consumption deadline of a chilled pork-cutlet rice bowl by **one day**; uses MALDI-TOF MS microbial ID with Kyushu Sangyo University to identify spoilage bacteria "in hours instead of weeks" — [IBTimes Japan](https://jp.ibtimes.com/seven-eleven-japan-signs-kyushu-sangyo-pact-after-1-day-shelf-life-gain-102269)
- (Japan) SEJ has shifted specific bento (beef short-rib, salt-pork) to chilled format and extended deadlines on 5 hand-rolled onigiri items — [SEJ sustainability, 食品ロス](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/esgp/environment/foodloss/)
- (Japan) Lawson: modified salad containers extend shelf life ~1 day; frozen onigiri in 14,000 stores by Jan 2026 — [Lawson, 廃棄物削減](https://www.lawson.co.jp/company/activity/environment/preservation/waste/)
- (Japan, 1990s→) The **1/3 rule**: makers must deliver within the first third of the manufacture-to-expiry window, retailers must pull unsold stock by the second third — [Consumer Affairs Agency white paper 2020](https://www.caa.go.jp/en/publication/annual_report/2020/white_paper_summary_07.html)
- (Japan, 2012→FY2023, Standard) Seven & i relaxed delivery deadlines from 1/3 to **1/2** of the window from 2012; extended to **all non-refrigerated processed foods by FY2023**; Seven Premium labels moved from day to year-month on ~70% of products by FY2023 — [Seven & i, Food loss measures](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)
- (Japan, 2013) MAFF trial of 1/3→1/2 for snacks and drinks estimated ~¥87 bn savings — [Japan student web contest page](https://contest.japias.jp/tqj22/220370H/en/3-1rule.html) (low-authority source; figure widely repeated but not verified against MAFF here)
- (Taiwan) Convenience-store fresh food shelf life "only 24 hours" — [FoodNext](https://www.foodnext.net/news/newstrack/paper/5098690421)

### Inferences
- Two deadlines, not one, is the operational trick: the **consumption deadline** protects the customer, the earlier **sales deadline** protects the store from selling something that expires in the customer's bag. The 2-hour gap is a chain policy, not law.
- The big waste lever was not discounting but **reformulation to chilled with longer life**; discounting came later (2019–2024) as a residual fix.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** print an hour-level **"use by"** and an earlier **"pull by"** (use-by minus 2 h) on every vendor pack; have the POS block sale after pull-by. Push vendors toward chilled formats with ≥24 h life and keep hot-case items to a short list.

### Gaps
- Typical hour-level consumption deadlines by category (onigiri ~X h, sandwiches 1–2 days, chilled noodles, desserts) were not found in a primary source in this pass; only the structural rule (2 h pull) and shelf-life shares (87% >24 h) are sourced.
- FamilyMart's and Lawson's sales-deadline offsets were not located.

## KQ4. In-store food safety: hot case, chiller, holding times, microwaves, HACCP

### Takeaway
Japan regulates store food safety through an **MHLW HACCP-style hygiene-plan guide for convenience stores** (simple cooking such as frying); hot-snack discard windows are chain manuals — **fried items ~4 h, steamed buns/baked ~6 h, Famichiki 6 h** (staff testimony). Hot showcase product temperature guidance is 50–65°C (equipment makers) versus the international ≥63°C norm.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan) MHLW publishes a convenience-store guide for hygiene-management plans "incorporating HACCP thinking", covering simple in-store cooking such as frying — [MHLW guide (PDF)](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11130500/000767996.pdf)
- (Japan, chain manual via staff) FamilyMart's Famichiki is discarded **6 hours after cooking**; Famichiki carries no printed best-before because it is meant to be eaten immediately/same day — [kisetsumimiyori.com](https://kisetsumimiyori.com/famichikisyomikigen/) (secondary site)
- (Japan, chain manual via staff) Store-staff accounts: fried items **4 h**, steamed buns **6 h**; "fried 4 h, grilled/baked 6 h" is said to be written in major-chain manuals — [Merumo](https://news.merumo.ne.jp/article/genre/3519713); [Yahoo Shigoto Catalog Q&A](https://contents.jobcatalog.yahoo.co.jp/qa/list/11182501581/) (testimony, not official)
- (Japan) Ex-SEJ store manager: steamed buns held ~6 h; production timed to peaks; **markdowns prohibited at company-operated stores**; frankfurters visibly deteriorate (wrinkle) near the limit — [note.com, 元セブン店長](https://note.com/motoseven/n/n1d57c1a64f60)
- (Japan) Self-service hot-food showcase guidance: target product temperature **50–65°C** (equipment maker spec) — [Heaterkiki SCS62H spec (PDF)](https://heaterkiki.co.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/SCS62H-2_202401.pdf); international hot-holding norm cited as **≥63°C (145°F)** — [YourPilla](https://yourpilla.com/answers/hot-holding-prevent-food-poisoning)
- (Japan) SEJ reviewed factory processes and temperature/hygiene control to extend deadlines **without preservatives** — [Seven & i, Food loss measures](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)
- (Thailand) Frozen ready meals are heated at the counter by staff in microwaves — [CK Travels](https://www.cktravels.com/thailand-7-11-eleven-what-you-can-buy/); CPRAM dishes are "cooked and frozen... heated up in microwave ovens" — [SCMP 2018](https://www.scmp.com/country-reports/country-reports/topics/thailand-business-report-2018/article/2159760/cpram-elevates)
- (Japan, hot-case waste reality) Night and early morning (22:00–06:00) is when most hot-snack and daily-food discards occur because footfall is lowest — [Conveni-now](https://conveni-now.com/column/conveni-food-waste/) (secondary)

### Inferences
- The holding-time rule is a **timer-per-batch** discipline, not a temperature-only one: hot case ≥63°C limits bacteria, but the 4 h/6 h clocks exist for quality (texture) and for a defensible safety margin.
- Hot-snack waste is structurally concentrated at night — chains fight it by cooking in small batches timed to peaks, not by over-stocking the case.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** run the hot case at **≥63–65°C** with a probe thermometer logged twice a shift; tag every tray with a **cook/load time and a 4 h (fried) or 6 h (steamed/baked) discard time**; load small batches near 08:00, 12:30, 18:30; microwave-to-order from chilled/frozen rather than holding hot beyond 4 h.

### Gaps
- No official chain document stating Famichiki/karaage hold times was found; all are staff or secondary testimony.
- SEJ's chiller set-point and its barcode-reading microwave rollout (Japan Today article) could not be fetched (site blocked).
- Frequency of public-health inspections of konbini in Japan/Thailand/Taiwan was not found.
- Oden holding-time rules not found.

## KQ5. Forecasting to minimise waste: Tanpin Kanri → AI ordering

### Takeaway
All three Japanese chains now put AI in the ordering loop: SEJ's AI proposes orders using 13 factors (2020 pilot → 2023 all-store support, ~40% less ordering time); Lawson's **AI.CO** (semi-auto since 2015, nationwide March 2024, ~14,500 stores) also **recommends discount timing and amount**; Taiwan's 7-Eleven has used **expiry "smart tags" since 2014** and weather-aware ordering.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan, Jan 2020, Pilot) SEJ AI-ordering trial from 31 Jan 2020 in ~1,100 Chiba stores: AI weighs **13 internal/external factors** (sales, promotions, weather forecast and actuals); ~2,500 SKUs = ~80% of inventory, **excluding daily perishables**; earlier test in ~150 company stores cut ordering time **80→45 min/day** and lifted AI-covered sales **+2.5%**; staff keep final approval — [Ryutsuu](https://www.ryutsuu.biz/it/m012941.html)
- (Japan, 2023, Standard) SEJ rolled out AI ordering support to all stores in spring 2023; ordering time cut ~40% (Saitama test store: 10.5 h/week → 6.5 h), sales +3% — [Shiga Keiei summary](https://r.goope.jp/shigakeiei/info/4972325) (aggregator of a Nikkei report; treat as secondary)
- (Japan, 2015→2024, Standard) Lawson began semi-automatic ordering in 2015; **AI.CO** rolled out nationwide **March 2024** to ~14,500 stores; covers short-life items (bento, onigiri, prepared bread, noodles, deli, desserts); recommends order quantity **and discount timing/amount** — [Newswitch, 2024](https://newswitch.jp/p/39768); [CIO Japan](https://www.cio.com/article/644265/lawson_ai_order.html)
- (Japan) Lawson: semi-auto ordering plus discounting cut **per-store disposal value ~10% YoY** — [Newswitch](https://newswitch.jp/p/22165); semi-auto + planned ordering save ~2 h/person/day — [Newswitch](https://newswitch.jp/p/22165)
- (Japan, FY2025) Lawson credits AI ordering for cost reductions behind record operating profit — [Japan Times, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/04/17/lawson-family-mart-record-profit/)
- (Taiwan, 2014→) 7-Eleven Taiwan "smart tag" system since 2014 alerts stores to items about to expire; ordering model uses past sales and weather — [Taipei Times, 2022](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2022/10/17/2003787193)
- (Taiwan, 2022) FamilyMart Taiwan introduced AI-based fresh-food ordering in 2022 — [Taipei Times, 2022](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2022/10/17/2003787193)
- (Thailand, 2024) 7-Eleven Thailand uses the handheld **Graphic Ordering Terminal (GOT)** to check stock and adjust orders; in 2024 losses from product write-offs fell **1.60%, worth THB 178 million** — [CP All, Sustainable Waste Management](https://www.cpall.co.th/en/sustain/environmental-dimension/sustainable-waste-management/) (page blocked on fetch; figure from search index)

### Inferences
- Japanese AI ordering started with **ambient goods** (SEJ 2020) and only later touched daily perishables, because perishables need discount logic too — which is why Lawson's AI.CO bundles ordering with markdown timing.
- The inputs are mundane: last weeks' sales by hour, weather, local events, promotions. A small store can approximate this with a spreadsheet before buying software.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** the Consto POS should record **sell-through by item by hour** from day one and produce next-day order suggestions by weekday + weather; add a "mark down at hh:mm" prompt once there is data. This is a Phase-2 feature in the POS PRD, not a launch-day one.

### Gaps
- FamilyMart Japan's AI ordering specifics were not found in this pass.
- No waste-reduction % attributable to SEJ's AI ordering was found.

## KQ6. Scale of food waste and who bears the cost (Japan, Taiwan, Thailand)

### Takeaway
Japan: **~¥4.68 million per store per year** of disposed food (JFTC 2020 median), ~20% of a store's operating expenses, **10–15 kg/store/day**, ~200–300k t/year across chains (3–5% of Japan's food loss) — and the **franchisee bears 80–90%** of it because royalties are computed on gross profit before waste. Taiwan: ~6.56 t total waste/store/year at 7-Eleven (2025). Thailand: CP Group 41,513 t food waste (2024).

### Cited Findings
- (Japan, Sept 2020) JFTC survey of 57,524 franchisees (12,093 responses, ~21%): median food disposal **¥4.68 million/store/year** — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/af29bd9baa36a05997ae20f8b7434b58a3a138fd); survey scope — [White & Case alert](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/japan-fair-trade-commission-jftc-published-market-research-report-re-convenience)
- (Japan, 2020) Disposal loss (¥4.68M) ≈ **20% of store operating expenses**, second only to staff wages (¥15M, 65%) — [Kimura, Aichi Univ. journal no.117 (PDF)](https://leo.aichi-u.ac.jp/~keisoken/research/journal/no117/a/001-117_KIMURA.pdf)
- (Japan, 2020) HQ instruction quoted: "an order with zero waste means you're under-stocked; treat ~¥15,000/day as your waste budget" — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/af29bd9baa36a05997ae20f8b7434b58a3a138fd)
- (Japan, 2020) 51.1% of franchisees said HQ pushed them to buy stock against their judgement; 47.5% were pressed to buy unnecessary quantities — [Ryutsuu](https://www.ryutsuu.biz/government/m090220.html)
- (Japan, 2020) Per store **10–15 kg/day** of food discarded; **~200,000–300,000 t/year** across konbini; **3–5% of Japan's food loss**; waste typically budgeted at **2–3% of sales**; cites METI "New Convenience Store Study Group" report Feb 2020 — [Advertimes](https://www.advertimes.com/20200804/article320704/)
- (Japan) Franchisees bear **80%+** of disposal cost; HQ bears **~10–20%**, up from 0% after the 2009 JFTC order on markdown restrictions — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/af29bd9baa36a05997ae20f8b7434b58a3a138fd)
- (Japan) SEJ bears **15%** of the cost of disposing unsold items, franchisees **85%** — reported in [Japan Today](https://japantoday.com/category/business/seven-eleven-lawson-to-discount-foods-close-to-expiry-to-cut-waste) / [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/business/business-trends/japan-s-seven-eleven-to-start-discounting-unsold-food) (both pages blocked on fetch; figure from search snippet)
- (Japan, 2019) Lawson system-wide food waste ~**44,000 t/year** — [Impress Watch, 2019](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1185017.html)
- (Japan, FY2025) Lawson: total store waste **41.6 kg/store/day**, of which food waste **8.6 kg/store/day** — [Lawson, 廃棄物削減](https://www.lawson.co.jp/company/activity/environment/preservation/waste/)
- (Japan, FY2024) Seven & i group food waste **16.68 kg per ¥1 million sales** (FY2023 17.68; FY2022 19.83) — [Seven & i](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)
- (Japan, 2024) Japan discards ~5 million t of food a year (context figure) — [News On Japan](https://newsonjapan.com/article/141794.php)
- (Taiwan, 2025) 7-Eleven Taiwan waste **6.56 t/store/year**, −22.22% vs 2019 — [PCSC ESG](https://www.7-11.com.tw/company/csr/esgfocus_02.aspx)
- (Taiwan) Fresh food = 80–90% of reported retail waste; >500 t/month — [FoodNext](https://www.foodnext.net/news/newstrack/paper/5098690421)
- (Thailand, 2024) CP Group food waste baseline **41,513 t**; 2030 target **5,480 t** — [The Nation, CP circular loop](https://www.nationthailand.com/sustainability/esg/40068529)

### Inferences
- ¥4.68M of waste at the quoted 2–3%-of-sales budget implies roughly ¥160–230M sales/store/year, consistent with konbini averages; i.e. **waste ≈ 2–3% of sales is the planning norm** even after decades of optimisation.
- The accounting matters more than the tonnage: when HQ is paid on gross margin before waste, HQ wants full shelves and the franchisee eats the loss.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** budget **2–3% of fresh-food sales as planned waste** and treat zero waste as a stock-out signal; if you later franchise or take vendor consignment, write the waste split into the contract.

### Gaps
- No per-store waste figure for FamilyMart Japan, 7-Eleven Thailand or 7-Eleven Taiwan **food** (as opposed to total) waste was found.
- The exact JFTC report text on disposal yen was not fetchable; the ¥4.68M figure is cited via Ide and Kimura who quote it.

## KQ7. Mitigations: discounting, reformulation, donation, recycling, targets

### Takeaway
Every chain now runs a **near-expiry discount** (Japan via points 2019–2020, then price stickers 2021–2024; Taiwan via time-window 30–35% off with app maps; Thailand mainly via ordering tools and donation), plus **shelf-life reformulation**, **food drives/food banks** and **feed/compost recycling**, under 2030 targets of **−50% food waste** (Seven & i, FamilyMart, Lawson, all vs 2013 or 2018).

### Cited Findings — discounting
| Chain | Programme | Mechanism | Started | Status |

## KQ8. What physically happens to discarded food

### Takeaway
In Japan, SEJ runs a proprietary **"Eco-distribution" (エコ物流)** waste-collection network (with Mitsui, since 1994) that collects store waste sorted into three streams and sends unsold food to feed/compost; Lawson trucks unsold food to recycling plants in covered regions; the remainder is municipal incineration (national recycling rates ~57–62%). Thailand's CP Group routes to fertiliser, feed, insect protein and energy.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan, 1994→) SEJ's "Eco-distribution" waste collection system, built with Mitsui & Co. in 1994: store waste separated into **combustible, non-combustible, recyclable valuables** and collected/processed through the network — [SEJ sustainability, 食品リサイクル](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/action/000108/)
- (Japan) Seven & i describes Eco Distribution as SEJ's proprietary recycling system converting food waste into **feed and compost** — [Seven & i](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)
- (Japan, 2014→) Lawson Fukuoka: store unsold food transported to a food-recycling plant → crushed → vacuum-dried → feed for pig farms/feed makers — [Lawson](https://www.lawson.co.jp/company/activity/environment/preservation/waste/)
- (Japan, FY2025) Lawson: compost/feed recycling at 2,336 stores (of ~14,000); recycling rate 57.4% — [Lawson](https://www.lawson.co.jp/company/activity/environment/preservation/waste/)
- (Japan) Fryer oil at SEJ is 100% recycled into feed etc. — [SEJ sustainability](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/esgp/environment/foodloss/)
- (Thailand, 2025) CP Group diversion streams: fertiliser 25,207 t; animal feed 13,586 t; black-soldier-fly 6,436 t; energy 4,290 t — [The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/sustainability/esg/40068529)
- (Japan) Ex-SEJ manager: staff at company stores cannot mark down; discards are thrown away (the author bought near-expiry frankfurters himself rather than discard) — [note.com](https://note.com/motoseven/n/n1d57c1a64f60)

### Inferences
- "Collected by the delivery truck on the return trip" is **not** what the sources describe; collection is by a **separate waste-logistics network** (SEJ/Mitsui) or by contracted recyclers (Lawson). The delivery truck stays clean for food.
- Because recycling rates are ~60%, roughly **40% of konbini food waste in Japan is still incinerated** as municipal waste.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** in Beeramguda there is no recycler network; the realistic chain is (1) sell by discount, (2) donate same-day within FSSAI surplus-food rules, (3) vendor take-back for compost/feed (local dairies and piggeries take cooked rice/bread), (4) municipal wet-waste. Keep a one-line discard log (item, qty, reason) — it is the input for ordering.

### Gaps
- No source states what share of SEJ stores are covered by feed/compost recycling, or SEJ's own food-recycling % (only group-level targets).
- No source on Taiwan's physical disposal routes for 7-Eleven beyond the "69.48% reuse" figure.

## KQ9. Franchisee economics and the 2019–2020 reforms

### Takeaway
The "convenience-store accounting" problem is that royalties are charged on gross profit **before** deducting disposal loss, so HQ gains from full shelves while the franchisee funds the waste; after the **2009 JFTC order** against markdown restrictions and the **2020 JFTC survey**, HQs moved to **10–20% waste-cost subsidies** (SEJ 15%) and formally permitted markdowns.

### Cited Findings
- (Japan, 2009) JFTC ordered SEJ to stop restricting franchisees' markdown (見切り) sales; HQ waste subsidies rose from 0% to ~10–20% afterwards — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/af29bd9baa36a05997ae20f8b7434b58a3a138fd)
- (Japan) Academic critique of HQ "disposal-loss subsidy" schemes and konbini accounting — [Kimura, Aichi Univ. journal no.109 (PDF)](https://leo.aichi-u.ac.jp/~keisoken/research/journal/no109/a/02_KIMURA.pdf) (PDF not text-extractable; title and framing only)
- (Japan, 2020) JFTC: 51.1% of franchisees pushed to over-order; 66.8% wanted shorter hours; 67.2% said too many competing stores; JFTC asked 8 chains to submit corrective plans under the Antimonopoly Act ("abuse of superior bargaining position") — [Ryutsuu](https://www.ryutsuu.biz/government/m090220.html); [ABS-CBN/AFP](https://news.abs-cbn.com/business/09/03/20/japans-convenience-stores-told-to-stop-pushing-24-hour-schedules)
- (Japan) SEJ bears 15% / franchisee 85% of disposal cost — [Japan Today](https://japantoday.com/category/business/seven-eleven-lawson-to-discount-foods-close-to-expiry-to-cut-waste) (snippet; page blocked)
- (Japan, 2020) HQ "waste budget" guidance of ~¥15,000/day quoted to franchisees — [Rumi Ide, Yahoo News](https://news.yahoo.co.jp/expert/articles/af29bd9baa36a05997ae20f8b7434b58a3a138fd)
- (Japan) Waste budgeted at 2–3% of sales — [Advertimes](https://www.advertimes.com/20200804/article320704/)
- (Japan, 2024) SEJ HQ now sends stores "recommended discount timings" with the Eco Discount sticker — [News On Japan](https://newsonjapan.com/article/141794.php)

### Inferences
- The reforms did not change the accounting base; they added a **partial subsidy + permitted markdowns + AI timing**. Waste remains mostly the store's problem.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** if vendors supply on consignment or sale-or-return, the waste risk moves to the vendor and they will pad prices; if the store buys outright, it must own markdowns. Decide this per vendor and write the return/credit % into the vendor SOP.

### Gaps
- FamilyMart's and Lawson's exact HQ waste-subsidy percentages were not found in a primary source (Kimura PDF unreadable).

## KQ10. India angle: FSSAI rules and current Indian practice for unsold fresh food

### Takeaway
FSSAI requires a licence/registration, **"use by/expiry" (mandatory) with DD/MM/YY for ≤3-month shelf life**, Schedule 4 hygiene (hot ≥60°C, cold ≤5°C, reheat 75°C), and since 2019 a **Surplus Food regulation** that legitimises donation via registered agencies (IFSA, 85 NGOs). There is no Indian "Too Good To Go" at scale; practice is NGO pick-up (Robin Hood Army, Feeding India) and informal vendor take-back.

### Cited Findings — rules
- (India, 2020 regs) Labelling & Display Regulations: "Expiry/Use by" is **mandatory**; "Best before" is optional/additional; products with shelf life **≤3 months must use DD/MM/YY**; exemptions include unpeeled fresh produce, vinegar, boiled sweets, chewing gum; food-service chains with central licence or **10+ outlets must display calories and allergens** — [FoodSafetyStandard.in summary](https://foodsafetystandard.in/food-safety-and-standards-labelling-and-display-regulations/); official compendium — [FSSAI Labelling & Display compendium v.VII, Apr 2025 (PDF)](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Comp_Labelling%20Display_Version%20VII_03042025.pdf)
- (India) FSSAI temperature guidance as summarised by a training consultancy: danger zone **5–60°C**; cold display **≤5°C**; hot display **≥60°C**; cook to **75°C** core; reheat to **75°C for ≥2 min**; reheated food held **≥63°C** — [Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/are-your-foods-at-safe-zone-temperature-requirements-for-food-to-be-safe/) (secondary; official Schedule 4 PDF was image-only on fetch — [Delhi Govt copy](https://foodsafety.delhi.gov.in/sites/default/files/generic_multiple_files/general_hygienic_and_sanitary_practices_to_be_followed_by_food_business_operators_schedule_4.pdf))
- (India) A compliance-checklist vendor lists Schedule 4 items: medical fitness certificates for handlers, temperature records, thermometer calibration, pest-control records — [PopProbe FSSAI checklist](https://www.popprobe.com/checklist-library/food-hospitality/kitchen-operations/india-fssai-food-safety-checklist) (secondary)
- (India, 2019) Food Safety and Standards (Recovery and Distribution of Surplus Food) Regulations, 2019: gazetted **26 Jul 2019**, compliance from **1 Jul 2020**; sets duties of donating FBOs and of surplus-food distribution organisations on handling, transport, storage, labelling — [Packaging South Asia](https://packagingsouthasia.com/type-of-article/industry-news/fssai-notifies-food-recovery-and-distribution-of-surplus-food-regulations-2019/); [S.S. Rana summary](https://ssrana.in/articles/fssai-food-donation-regulations/)
- (India) Indian Food Sharing Alliance (IFSA): pan-India network of **85 NGOs** connecting trained recovery agencies with food businesses — [Nutrition Connect / GAIN](https://nutritionconnect.org/food-safety-standards-authority-india-fssai-guiding-optimisation-surplus-food-donation); portal — [sharefood.eatrightindia.gov.in](https://sharefood.eatrightindia.gov.in/) (SSL expired on fetch)

### Cited Findings — practice
- (India, 2014→) Robin Hood Army: volunteer, zero-funds, collects unsold food from restaurants/events; chapters in 200+ cities, 16 countries — [Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/youth/how-the-robin-hood-army-is-feeding-indias-hungry/)
- (India) Zomato "Food Rescue": cancelled orders (~**4 lakh/month**) resold at a discount — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=485748)
- (India) "Last Bite" markets itself as India's first surplus-food delivery app — [SaaS Browser listing](https://saasbrowser.com/saas/960283/last-bite) (directory listing; scale unknown)
- (India, 2024) 24Seven convenience chain (Delhi NCR etc.) closed and was acquired by The New Shop — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/24seven-may-return-under-the-new-shop-banner-as-acquisition-talks-begin-124080800378_1.html); no public documentation of its fresh-food waste practices found
- (General) In European retail, bread take-back agreements shift most bread waste to the supplier; the most extreme take-back arrangements are in bread — [Resources, Conservation & Recycling, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921344917300411)

### Inferences
- The FSSAI stack a no-cook store needs: **State licence (or registration if turnover <₹12 lakh)**, vendor packs carrying **"use by" date and time** (hour-level is permitted and advisable even if the regulation only mandates the date), a **hot/cold display log**, FoSTaC-trained supervisor, and a **surplus-food SOP** naming an IFSA/RHA partner so donation is legal and documented.
- Indian bakery and snack vendors commonly accept unsold returns informally; formalise it as a **credit % per returned unit** rather than assume it.
- **What this means for a no-cook Indian store:** (1) POS enforces use-by/pull-by; (2) hot case ≥60°C (FSSAI) — set 65°C to be safe; chiller ≤5°C; (3) 20:00 discount window on WhatsApp; (4) 22:00 hand-off of remaining safe food to an RHA/Feeding India volunteer with a signed log; (5) wet waste to vendor/municipal. All of this is cheap and fits a 600–800 sq ft store.

### Gaps
- Could not extract the verbatim Schedule 4 Part II/IV text (PDF image-only); temperatures above are from reputable secondary training sources and should be checked against the FSSAI compendium before being put in an SOP.
- No Indian retail chain (D-Mart, Reliance Smart Bazaar/7-Eleven India, Ratnadeep, Namdhari's) publishes fresh-food waste rates or donation volumes that I could find.
- No evidence of a functioning Indian near-expiry marketplace at scale (Too Good To Go is absent; Last Bite scale unknown).
