# India's cost-conscious, fresh-first convenience landscape (Telangana / Hyderabad suburb / Tier-2 focus)

Research note for Consto (600–800 sq ft, AI-backed, no in-store cooking; hot case + chiller). Compiled 2026-10-06. Every number carries its year and source. "Add / Remove / Keep" lines are implications vs a 7-Eleven/FamilyMart template.

Method note: ~60 web searches/fetches across NIQ, Kantar, Bain, FSSAI/GST/Legal Metrology sources, Telangana GOs, company DRHPs and trade press. The session's search budget ran out before a few Hyderabad-specific items (Beeramguda QC coverage, Milky Mist DRHP, Amul parlour counts, evening-snack street prices) could be closed; these are listed under Gaps.

## KQ1. The sachet / small-pack economy and "magic price points"

### Takeaway
Low-unit packs (≤₹10/₹20) are 30–40% of all Indian FMCG sales and 44–79% of sales in the categories a convenience store lives on (shampoo, biscuits, chocolate, salty snacks). GST 2.0 (22 Sep 2025) broke the ₹5/₹10 round-number anchors (Parle-G ₹5 → ₹4.45), which makes exact-change UPI and precise POS pricing more important, not less.

### Cited Findings
- Up to 40% of total FMCG sales in India come from low-unit packs (FICCI estimate); other estimates put small packs at 30–40% of FMCG sales (2025) — [The Consumer Daily](https://theconsumerdaily.co/news/small-packs-surge-as-indian-shoppers-cut-spending-9f5bdeea); [Performonks "The India Playbook: the shrinking packs"](https://performonks.substack.com/p/the-india-playbook-the-shrinking)
- Share of category sales from low-unit packs (Sep 2025): shampoo 79%, biscuits 64%, chocolates 58%, salty snacks 44%, toothpaste 29% — [A2Z Taxcorp summary of ET report, 24 Sep 2025](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gst-2-0-reform-kicks-in-no-more-magic-price-points-fmcg-drops-popular-rs-5-rs-10-packs/)
- Parle Products derives ~70% of revenue and volume from ≤₹10 packs; low-unit packs are an estimated 40–65% of sales across biscuits, soaps, snacks, noodles (2025) — [Angel One](https://oga-prod.angelone.in/news/economy/the-magic-of-rs-5-and-rs-10-goods-why-are-they-the-heartbeat-of-indias-mass-market)
- HUL's CFO Ritesh Tiwari calls ₹1, ₹5, ₹10 "magic price points"; the industry protects ₹5/₹10/₹20 "sacred price points" (2025) — [Business Standard, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/fmcg-brands-magic-price-points-gst-reforms-consumer-impact-125092300431_1.html)
- Post-GST 2.0 (effective 22 Sep 2025) price-point changes: Parle-G ₹5 → ₹4.45 (first change in ~20 years); Oreo ₹10 → ₹8.90; Bournvita ₹30 → ₹26.69; Gems/5Star ₹20 → ₹17.80; ₹1 candy → 88 paise; ₹2 shampoo sachet → ₹1.77 — [A2Z Taxcorp, 24 Sep 2025](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/gst-2-0-reform-kicks-in-no-more-magic-price-points-fmcg-drops-popular-rs-5-rs-10-packs/)
- Shrinkflation is the preferred lever: Parle cut grammage at ₹5/₹10 biscuit price points and cut chip pack sizes at ₹30/₹50 rather than raise price (2022) — [Business Standard podcast](https://www.business-standard.com/podcast/current-affairs/how-is-shrinkflation-helping-fmcg-companies-tide-over-rising-input-costs-122051800074_1.html)
- NIQ: FMCG volume +5.4% with 7.1% price growth, unit growth outpacing volume = stronger small-pack preference (2025) — [NielsenIQ, 2025](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2025/niq-gst-2-0-sparks-short-term-dip-in-fmcg-demand/)
- Kantar FY25: FMCG packs bought per household rose 13% to 226; average pack size +16%; 156 shopping trips per person per year (≈ once every two days); unbranded FMCG grew 6.1% vs branded 3.9% in Q4 FY25 — [Kantar via Agro & Food Processing, 2025](https://agronfoodprocessing.com/kantar-report-shows-bigger-grocery-baskets-in-fy25/)
- Small packs sold 4–10 percentage points faster than large packs since April (2025) — [The Consumer Daily](https://theconsumerdaily.co/news/small-packs-surge-as-indian-shoppers-cut-spending-9f5bdeea)

### Inferences
- **Keep / Add:** a dense "₹5–₹20 wall" near checkout (biscuits, chips, chocolate, shampoo sachets, ₹10 Maggi, ₹5 candies). In a 7-Eleven template this is a single rack; in India it should be 15–20% of facings.
- **Add:** POS must handle odd post-GST prices (₹4.45, ₹8.90) and auto-round-and-display correctly on UPI QR; cash rounding rules need a policy (customers notice 55 paise).
- **Keep:** "156 trips/year" means the Hyderabad shopper is already a top-up shopper; the AI agent's job is to be the default for the every-other-day trip, not the monthly list.
- Unbranded growing faster than branded (6.1% vs 3.9%) is the strongest single data point for a Consto own-brand/loose line in staples and snacks.

### Gaps
- No 2024–26 Telangana-specific small-pack share; all data is India-wide.
- The Business Standard article itself returned 403; figures are from its syndicated summary.

## KQ2. MRP, Legal Metrology and where small Indian retailers actually earn margin

### Takeaway
MRP is a hard ceiling (fines ₹25k/₹50k/₹1 lakh + jail), so a convenience premium on packaged goods is illegal in India; packaged FMCG yields 8–15% to a kirana and ~20% to modern trade. Margin therefore has to come from private label (35–40% at SuperK), fresh/bakery/own-brand (30% of Ratnadeep sales; 28% of revenue from PL), services and loose goods.

### Cited Findings
- Section 36 Legal Metrology Act: selling above MRP → fine up to ₹25,000 (1st), ₹50,000 (2nd), ₹1 lakh and/or 1 year jail (subsequent) — [Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/charging-above-mrp-is-an-unfair-trade-practice-your-rights-when-a-seller-overcharges-in-india)
- Telangana enforcement example: a Nalgonda hotel fined ₹25,000 for charging above MRP on a water bottle — [Deccan Chronicle, 2019](https://deccanchronicle.com/nation/current-affairs/230519/legal-metrology-fines-shops-for-overcharging)
- Dual MRP (higher MRP for airports/malls/cinemas) banned from 1 Jan 2018 — [The Better India](https://www.thebetterindia.com/107173/gst-airports-malls-cinema-halls-no-extra-charge-2018/); [Tribune](https://tribuneindia.com/news/a-pre-packaged-product-cannot-have-two-mrps-369496)
- Kirana typically buys at ~₹85 on an MRP of ₹100 (≈15%); packaged biscuits, namkeen, soap, shampoo, toothpaste carry high-single-digit to mid-teens margins (2025) — [Kirana Club](https://kirana.club/resources/fmcg-distributor-kirana-margins); [Accountune category-wise kirana margins, 2026](https://accountune.com/kirana-store-profit-margin)
- Modern trade buys at ~₹80 (20% margin) while a kirana buying via distributor at ~₹90 makes ~10% — [eChai Ventures](https://echai.ventures/d2c/d2c-offline-retail/general-trade-vs-modern-trade-start-margins)
- General trade gross margin 8–10% vs modern trade ~20%; traditional MT operating cost >7–8% of sales (rent + staff) vs kirana <3–4%; SuperK pairs MT margin with kirana-level opex (2025) — [The Indian Pivot, "Monetising India 2"](https://theindianpivot.substack.com/p/monetising-india-2-modern-retail)
- SuperK: private-label margin 35–40%; staples (dal/rice) low margin; confectionery up to 20% (Aug 2025) — [Third Eyesight / SuperK playbook](https://www.thirdeyesight.in/tag/convenience-stores/)
- Ratnadeep (DRHP, FY26): 30,000+ SKUs; 2,321 private-label SKUs = 28.23% of revenue; mix FMCG 52.5%, staples 25.7%, fresh produce 13.1%, GM/apparel 8.65%; EBITDA margin 5.01%, PAT 1.65%; revenue ₹2,223 cr FY26 (₹1,988 cr FY25) — [HDFC Sky IPO page](https://hdfcsky.com/ipo/ratnadeep-retail-ipo)
- Ratnadeep's own brand + farm-to-fork fresh produce = 30% of overall sales (2023) — [Indian Retailer](https://www.indianretailer.com/news/retail-india-news-ratnadeep-reaches-150-store-milestone-four-retail-openings-bengaluru-and)
- DMart's private-label arm Align Retail more than doubled in two years to ₹3,322 cr in FY25; private label is 10–12% of India's retail product market (2025) — [Third Eyesight](https://www.thirdeyesight.in/indias-retail-sector-witnesses-rising-demand-for-private-labels/)
- Vijaya (Telangana co-op) gives the lowest dealer margin on packet milk, ₹2.5/litre, vs Arokya ₹4.5/litre (Hyderabad study, 2019) — [SASAPJAS study](https://sasapjas.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/123.pdf)
- Small paan shops sell 15–20 cigarette packs/day at ₹10–20 margin per pack (older industry deck, undated ~2012) — [SlideShare: cigarette marketing & distribution](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/cigarette-marketing-and-distribution/11944986)

### Margin map (for a 700 sq ft Consto)

| Category | Typical gross margin | Source/year | Consto implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Packaged FMCG via distributor | 8–15% | Kirana Club 2025; Accountune 2026 | **Keep** as footfall driver, never as profit centre |
| Packaged FMCG via direct/MT terms | ~20% | eChai; Indian Pivot 2025 | Needs volume or a SuperK-style aggregator |
| Staples (dal/rice/atta) | low single digit–~8% | SuperK (Third Eyesight 2025) | **Remove** bulk staples; keep 1 kg top-up packs only |
| Confectionery/impulse | up to 20% | SuperK 2025 | **Keep** at checkout |
| Private label (podi, pickles, namkeen, pulses) | 35–40% | SuperK 2025 | **Add** — the single biggest margin lever |
| Fresh + own brand combined | 30% of sales at Ratnadeep | Indian Retailer 2023 | **Add** hot case / chiller as a margin, not just traffic, category |
| Packet milk (Vijaya) | ₹2.5/L (~4–5%) | 2019 study | **Keep** as morning anchor only; margin is in curd/buttermilk/paneer |
| Cigarettes | ₹10–20/pack | undated deck | See KQ6 — footfall, licence risk |

### Inferences
- The 7-Eleven model (price premium for convenience) is structurally unavailable in India for anything with an MRP. The premium must be captured on **non-MRP items**: hot food, cut fruit, tea, loose goods, own-brand, and services.
- A store at Ratnadeep's 5% EBITDA on a ₹2,223 cr base shows even the best Hyderabad chain is thin; a 700 sq ft store cannot win on packaged goods and must get ≥30% of sales from fresh/own-brand/services.

### Gaps
- No public per-store P&L for a Hyderabad convenience store; DMart/Reliance private-label gross-margin percentages not disclosed.
- Current (2025–26) milk dealer margins for Vijaya/Heritage/Jersey/Dodla not found; the ₹2.5/L figure is 2019.

## KQ3. Kirana behaviours Indians expect, top-up trips, and quick commerce in Hyderabad suburbs

### Takeaway
A third of kirana customers buy on credit, 86% choose a kirana for location and 42% for the relationship; India already shops every other day. Quick commerce is big in Hyderabad (406–484 dark stores, Zepto-led) but coverage is core-city; Ameenpur shows only one Instamart store and no Blinkit/Zepto, which is the opening for a physical AI-backed store in Beeramguda.

### Cited Findings
- 33% of surveyed kirana customers regularly use store credit; factors driving value at kiranas: location 86%, product quality/variety 51%, relationship with owner 42%; buying loose and on credit are distinguishing features (survey of 200 stores, 198 customers, 2022) — [Flourish Ventures, Digitizing the Corner Shop – India](https://flourishventures.com/research/digitizing-the-corner-shop/india-spotlight/)
- Kiranas are ~80% of India's ~$520 bn grocery market (2021) — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/finance/fintech-s-buy-now-pay-later-credit-for-kiranas-is-changing-livelihoods-121100700093_1.html); 15 million+ kiranas = 88% of retail; consumers visit 2–3 times a week — [ISB Executive Education](https://execed.isb.edu/en/ep/research-perspectives/article/the-dual-channel-strategy--cpg-firms-and-the-importance-of-kiran.html)
- Kantar: 156 shopping trips per person per year in FY25 — [Agro & Food Processing, 2025](https://agronfoodprocessing.com/kantar-report-shows-bigger-grocery-baskets-in-fy25/)
- NIQ 2024: weekly "main" shopping trips rose from 12% (2023) to 32% (2024) of shoppers; 40% of Indian shoppers switch stores for better deals; 87% believe food prices are still rising — [NielsenIQ, Sep 2024](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/insights/analysis/2024/five-key-trends-shaping-the-future-of-grocery-shopping-in-india)
- Bain (Apr 2026): quick-commerce GMV $10–11 bn in 2025, doubling annually since 2023, 16–17% of e-retail; online grocery still only ~1.5% of total grocery (6–7% in metros); household essentials 85–90% of QC GMV; QC sessions "sub-five-minute", 8× visit-to-order conversion; missions are "predetermined and top-up"; Tier-2+ cities = ~50% of incremental e-retail orders in 2025 — [Bain, How India Shops Online 2026](https://www.bain.com/insights/how-india-shops-online-2026/)
- Quick commerce = ~two-thirds of online grocery orders and 70–75% of e-grocery GMV (2024) — [Outlook Business / Redseer](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/quick-commerce-captures-nearly-half-of-kirana-sales-market-projected-to-hit-40-billion-by-2030)
- Hyderabad dark stores: 484 across major platforms, 3rd after Bengaluru and Delhi; Zepto leads in Hyderabad (2025) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/flipkart-minutes-overtakes-swiggy-instamart-in-dark-stores-across-top-10-cities)
- Hyderabad (data as of 10 Jul 2026): Zepto 110 stores / 35 neighbourhoods, Blinkit 94 / 31, Instamart 77 / 23; 406 stores across 128 neighbourhoods for the big three — [QuickCommerceMap Hyderabad](https://quickcommercemap.com/compare/hyderabad)
- Ameenpur: 1 dark store, Swiggy Instamart only (no Blinkit, no Zepto listed) — [QuickCommerceMap availability](https://quickcommercemap.com/availability)
- Dark stores serve a 3–5 km radius — [Actowiz pincode serviceability](https://www.actowizsolutions.com/pincode-serviceability-blinkit-zepto.php)
- Blinkit crossed 1,000 dark stores nationally; India expected to have 5,000–5,500 dark stores by FY26 — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/blinkit-crosses-1000-dark-store-milestone-as-quick-commerce-race-heats-up); [Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/news-and-trends/indias-quick-commerce-landscape-to-transform-with-5000/486062)

- 63% of Indian shoppers willing to buy store brands/private label; 42% tried new brands to cut cost (EY, 2022) — [Business Standard / EY](https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/inflationary-pressures-on-consumers-amidst-a-positive-economic-outlook-ey-122091900827_1.html)

### Behaviour checklist vs 7-Eleven

| Indian expectation | Evidence | 7-Eleven has it? | Consto: Add / Keep / Remove |
|---|---|---|---|
| Udhaar / khata credit | 33% of kirana customers (Flourish 2022) | No | **Add** — digital khata inside Consto Agent, capped per customer, WhatsApp reminders |

### Inferences
- Beeramguda/Patancheru are not yet shown as served by Blinkit/Zepto on the coverage map (only Ameenpur with a single Instamart node). Consto's window is the 2–3 years before dark stores push past the ORR into Patancheru mandal.
- Bain's "top-up mission, household essentials 85–90%" describes exactly a convenience-store basket; QC is winning the top-up mission in the core city. In suburbs, the physical store + WhatsApp agent can hold it if it matches QC's two advantages: knowing what you buy and removing friction.

### Gaps
- No quantified Hyderabad household QC penetration (orders/household/month). Bain gives metro e-grocery at 6–7% but no city split.
- Beeramguda and Patancheru specifically are not named on any serviceability page found; needs an in-app pincode check (502032/502319).
- No Bain/NIQ figure found for the share of trips that are top-up vs stock-up at India level (only NIQ's weekly-main-trip share and Kantar trip counts).
- Udhaar's share of kirana *sales* (vs share of customers) not found.

Hyderabad's tiffin economy runs at ₹20–45 a plate and chai at ₹15–35; the packaged-fresh supply chain that lets a store sell this without a kitchen already exists in the city (iD Fresh has a Hyderabad factory; Heritage runs 859 parlours; Vijaya is adding 400–500). ## KQ5. Price sensitivity, DMart effect and value vs convenience

### Takeaway
Indian shoppers are measurably value-first: 91% rank value-for-money as the top store-choice factor, 40% switch stores for better deals, 87% think food prices are still rising, 67% will buy store brands. DMart (449 stores, Feb 2026, Gachibowli opening) sets the price reference in Hyderabad, so a convenience store should match on a known-value basket and earn elsewhere.

### Cited Findings
- 91% rank value-for-money as the top criterion when choosing a grocery store, above location (Nielsen, undated older study) — [afaqs](https://www.afaqs.com/20096_value-for-money-top-while-selecting-grocery-store-nielsen-study)

## KQ6. Indian things 7-Eleven doesn't have: paan/tobacco, pooja, festivals, milk rush, services, water cans, OTC

### Takeaway
Several Indian "traffic generators" are low-margin or regulated (cigarettes ₹10–20/pack and COTPA-restricted; milk ₹2.5/L), while others are near-free to add and sticky (AEPS/recharge/bill-pay, 20 L water, pooja items, Schedule-K OTC medicines). The right design is: keep the ones that create daily visits and are legal to automate, and route the rest to the AI agent as "services".

### Cited Findings
- COTPA Section 6(b): no tobacco sales within 100 yards of educational institutions — [Tobacco Control Laws](https://www.tobaccocontrollaws.org/legislation/india/sales-restrictions); PIB confirms Section 6 and that violating shopkeepers' licences are to be cancelled (2025) — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2101735&reg=48&lang=2)
- Telangana field study: 25% of surveyed educational institutions had tobacco sales within 100 yards (2025) — [J Family Med Prim Care / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12633962/)
- Loose-cigarette sales: several states ban them because each stick lacks the statutory warning; shopkeepers are warned against loose sales — [Indus Dispatch](https://indusdispatch.in/cotpa-no-shops-can-sell-tobacco-products-within-100-yards-of-educational-institutions/); [Ladakh enforcement example](https://ladakh.gov.in/enforcement-action-against-illegal-tobacco-sales-near-schools/)
- India cigarette market $29.22 bn (2025); tobacco shops are 40% of the channel — [IMARC](https://imarcgroup.com/india-cigarette-market); paan shops 15–20 packs/day at ₹10–20/pack margin (undated deck) — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/cigarette-marketing-and-distribution/11944986)
- Hyderabad: tobacco-serving businesses need a GHMC trade licence; multiple 2024–25 Telangana HC writs on COTPA compliance — [SupremeToday case listing](https://supremetoday.ai/doc/judgement/INDTEL00000280662)
- Schedule K of Drugs Rules 1945: 33 household remedies (aspirin, paracetamol, analgesics, antacids, disinfectants) may be sold without a drug licence/prescription — [IAS Gyan](https://www.iasgyan.in/daily-current-affairs/schedule-k-of-the-drugs-rules-1945-exemptions-in-drug-regulation-and-public-health-significance); proposed 16-drug OTC expansion (paracetamol 500 mg, diclofenac gel, clotrimazole, calamine, etc.) — [Medical Dialogues](https://medicaldialogues.in/news/industry/pharma/16-drugs-including-diclofenac-clotrimazole-now-otc-93560); note this was a draft notification in 2022 — [CliniExperts](https://cliniexperts.com/draft-notification-to-add-16-drugs-as-otc-under-schedule-k-of-drugs-rules-1945/)

- Pooja samagri = 3% of Diwali spend; food & grocery 13% (2023) — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1537624/india-diwali-expenditure-share-by-category)
- Vijaya milk dealer margin ₹2.5/L (2019) — [SASAPJAS](https://sasapjas.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/123.pdf)

## KQ7. Regulatory realities: FSSAI, GST on ready food, 24×7 permission, Legal Metrology, drug licence

### Takeaway
Telangana explicitly allows 24×7 shops under G.O.Ms.No.4 (4 Apr 2023) for a ₹10,000 annual fee plus staff-welfare conditions; FSSAI Basic Registration now covers turnover up to ₹1.5 cr (from 1 Apr 2026); supplied hot food sold as restaurant service is 5% GST without ITC, while most packaged snacks are 5% and Indian breads/paneer/UHT milk are nil after GST 2.0.

### Cited Findings
- 24×7 operation: G.O.Ms.No.4, Labour Employment Training & Factories (LAB-I) Dept, dated 04.04.2023, exempts all shops & establishments from Section 7 (opening/closing hours) of the Telangana Shops & Establishments Act 1988, subject to ID cards, weekly off, working-hour limits, overtime, compensatory holidays, women's safety/consent/transport for night shifts, record-keeping, and an annual fee of ₹10,000 per store (DD) — [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/telangana-shops-and-establishments-act-guidelines-for-exemption-to-all-shops-and-establishments-for-operating-24-7-in-telangana-state/); [Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/shops-establishments-to-be-open-247-in-telangana-guidelines-issued-2562849/); pro-forma application — [Ascent HR](https://ascent-hr.com/en-ae/notification/pro-forma-application-for-seeking-an-exemption-to-operate-shops-and-establishments-24-7/)
- G.O.Rt.No.282 dated 5 Jul 2025 further relaxed working-hour limits for commercial establishments (excluding shops) — [King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/labour-employment/telangana-expands-working-hour-flexibility/)
- FSSAI thresholds: until 31 Mar 2026 Basic Registration ≤₹12 lakh, State Licence ₹12 lakh–₹20 cr, Central >₹20 cr; from 1 Apr 2026 Basic ≤₹1.5 cr, State ₹1.5 cr–₹50 cr, Central >₹50 cr — [S.S. Rana & Co](https://ssrana.in/articles/fssai-raises-turnover-limits-for-food-business-registration-and-licensing/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/1801924/fssai-raises-turnover-limits-for-food-business-registration-and-licensing)
- GST on restaurant/takeaway food service: 5% without ITC for standalone restaurants (dine-in and takeaway); 18% with ITC only in hotels with room tariff >₹7,500 — [Razorpay, 2026](https://razorpay.com/learn/gst-on-food-and-restaurants/); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/impact-gst-food-services-restaurant-business)
- GST 2.0 (22 Sep 2025): slabs 0/5/18/40, 12% removed; namkeen, bhujia, chips, biscuits, chocolate, ice cream, mithai at 5%; Indian breads, UHT milk, pre-packaged paneer at nil — [Wikipedia GST 2.0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goods_and_Services_Tax_2.0); [Accountune 2026 kirana GST list](https://www.accountune.com/fmcg-gst-rate-list-kirana-store-2026)

- Drug licence: Schedule K items sellable without licence; everything else needs a Form 20/21 retail drug licence and a pharmacist — [IAS Gyan](https://www.iasgyan.in/daily-current-affairs/schedule-k-of-the-drugs-rules-1945-exemptions-in-drug-regulation-and-public-health-significance); [BnB Legal on the OTC void](https://bnblegal.com/article/deciphering-the-regulatory-void-of-over-the-counter-medicines-in-india/)
- COTPA: 100-yard school rule; licence cancellation on violation — [PIB 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2101735&reg=48&lang=2)
