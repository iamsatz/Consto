# Products

**What 7-Eleven and FamilyMart sell, how they change it country by country, and what Consto should add, keep, remove or test for a growing suburb or Tier-2/3 town anywhere in India.**

**Where this applies.** The report is written for a *type* of place, not one address: a fast-growing suburb 5–20 km from a city core, or a district town, with mixed apartments and independent houses, working families, kiranas and tiffin centres already present, quick commerce absent or thin, and rents at a fraction of a metro high street. Hyderabad examples are used for prices and food names because the first store is in Telangana; swap the food names (idli, pulihora, Osmania biscuit) for the local equivalents in another state and the rules still hold. State-level law differs (24x7 shop permission, lottery, tobacco licence, liquor), so where this report cites a Telangana order, read it as "check your state's equivalent".

Research date: 6 October 2026. Every number carries its year. "Est." means a figure derived from published numbers, not a published figure itself. "Secondary" means the source is a summary or press report rather than a primary document.

---

**The short version.** 7-Eleven and FamilyMart are food shops with a services counter attached. In Japan, food is **68% of Seven-Eleven's merchandise sales and about 80% of its gross profit (FY2024)**, because fresh and processed food earns 35–41% gross margin while the non-food shelf (mostly cigarettes) earns under 20% ([Seven & i Corporate Outline 2025](https://www.7andi.com/en/ir/library/co_financial/2025/convenience_store.html)). In Thailand the split is **76% food and drink to 24% non-food (2024)** ([CP All MD&A FY2024](https://www.cpall.co.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/MDA-EN-FY2024-V2.pdf)). Every successful market swaps the hot-food hero (onigiri, toasties, tea eggs, kimbap, siopao) and the services layer (bill pay, parcels, e-wallet top-ups) while keeping the tiny box, long hours, item-by-item ordering and daily deliveries. The two things that do not travel are the price premium and the imported icon: India's MRP rule makes a convenience mark-up illegal on packaged goods, and **7-Eleven India closed its last 31 stores by end-September 2026 after losing roughly ₹90 crore on ₹92 crore of revenue in FY2025-26**, per Reuters-based reports of 5–6 October 2026 ([Yahoo Finance/Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/small-business/articles/reliance-retail-7-eleven-end-094939001.html); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/seven-i-shuts-31-india-stores-ending-partnership-with-reliance)). For Consto the lesson is to copy the structure (hot case + chiller + shelf, two deliveries a day, one hero, 1–3 brands per need, a services counter, a strict pull-by clock, a planned waste budget) and to replace the content with what the neighbourhood already eats and pays for (Hyderabad example): idli at ₹20–40, chai under the Irani ₹25, curd rice, majjiga, Osmania biscuits, podis and pickles under the Consto name, and a WhatsApp agent doing the job that the Japanese field counsellor and the Thai loyalty app do. Twelve numbered sections below answer the twelve questions in order; the last section is a checklist Sateesh can fill in line by line.

## 1. What kind of products do 7-Eleven and FamilyMart sell?

**Direct answer.** They sell ready-to-eat food first, drinks and snacks second, and daily goods, tobacco and services third. In Japan a store carries about 3,000 items in about 143 square metres and replaces roughly 70% of them within a year. The single biggest money-maker is the fresh-food counter and chilled shelf (onigiri, bento, sandwiches, fried chicken, coffee), not the packaged aisle.

The pattern: a hero is cheap (¥100–250, NT$10, ฿10–20), sells 20–300 units per store per day, and is bought by habit. The tea egg is the most Consto-like example: tiny revenue, large habit. India's equivalent is the boiled or masala egg, the ₹10 chai, or the ₹20 idli plate.

### 1.4 Private label, range size and churn

- **Seven Premium** (Seven & i group) sold **¥1.5 trillion in FY2024 across 3,460 items**, with 315 items each selling over ¥1 bn a year; it started in May 2007 with 49 items ([Seven & i, 16 Apr 2025](https://www.7andi.com/company/news/release/202504161100___.html)). The value tier "Seven The Price" went from 11 to 211 items between Sep 2022 and Nov 2024 ([Seven & i, Dec 2024](https://www.7andi.com/company/news/release/202412111300.html)).
- **Famimaru** (FamilyMart) has 800+ items since Oct 2021 ([FamilyMart sustainability page](https://www.family.co.jp/english/sustainability/material_issues/needs/added_value.html)); its **Convenience Wear** socks, t-shirts and underwear passed ¥13 bn in FY2024, up more than 60% ([AdverTimes](https://www.advertimes.com/20250815/article508684/)).
- **CP All** private brands are about 10% of 7-Eleven Thailand sales (2025 analyst estimate) ([Thanachart Securities, Sep 2025](https://tnsthinkwebcontent.thanachartsec.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/RETAIL250911E.pdf)).
- **7-Select** (US) passed US$1 bn in annual sales ([Convenience Store News](https://csnews.com/7-eleven-reaches-1b-milestone-private-brand-sales)).
- A Japanese store carries **about 3,000 items**, receives roughly 80–100 new items a week, and **replaces about 70% of its range within a year** ([Wikipedia: Seven-Eleven Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven-Eleven_Japan); [UT Dallas case facts](https://personal.utdallas.edu/~metin/Or6366/Folios/IntroStrategy/scseveneleven.pdf); [TJF Takarabako](https://tjf.or.jp/takarabako/japaneseculture/28konbini.htm)). Soft drinks are the only hard data point on depth: about 50 facings chosen from 200+ brands on the market, which works out to 2–4 brands per flavour, not the 8–10 an Indian supermarket carries ([Lal 2011](https://gwern.net/doc/japan/2011-lal.pdf)).
- At Japan's density (3,000 items in 143 sqm, about 21 items per sqm) a 600–800 sq ft Consto (56–74 sqm) would hold **1,300–1,600 items** (est.). The category master's target of 1,500–2,500 is at the top of that range; the lower end is more realistic once the rest corner and celebrate zone take their 20–24% of floor.

## 2. What changes do they make in each country?

**Direct answer.** The box, the stripes, the long hours, the daily deliveries and the item-by-item ordering stay the same. The hot-food hero, the services counter, the payment rail and the owner of the licence change every time. Region-limited items are already 30–40% of the Japanese lineup and heading to 50%; the "70% global, 30% local" formula quoted in Indian commentary has no primary-source basis ([NACS, Jul 2020](https://www.convenience.org/Media/Daily/2020/Jul/28/6-One-Size-Doesnt-Fit-All-CStores_International); [Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/japan-data/h01982/seven-eleven%E2%80%99s-japanese-half-century-a-store-on-every-corner.html)).

### 2.3 The recurring rules of localisation

## 3. What is different about 7-Eleven India?

**Direct answer.** 7-Eleven India was a metro, company-owned, high-rent format that never reached density, never built a services counter, and never found a price premium it was legally allowed to charge. Reliance's five-year master franchise expired, **the last 31 stores closed by end-September 2026, and Reuters-based reports on 5–6 October 2026 confirm the partnership is over**; FY2025-26 revenue was about ₹92 crore against a loss of about ₹90 crore ([Yahoo Finance/Reuters, 5 Oct 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/small-business/articles/reliance-retail-7-eleven-end-094939001.html); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/news/seven-i-shuts-31-india-stores-ending-partnership-with-reliance); [HDFC Sky, 6 Oct 2026](https://hdfcsky.com/news/reliance-retail-shutters-7-eleven-stores-in-india-after-franchise-pact-ends)). Reliance has issued no official statement.

### 3.5 What this means for Consto

1. **Do not pay metro rent for a convenience-store basket.** Rent plus power plus running costs at 44% of sales is fatal. Suburb or Tier-2 town rents and a 600–800 sq ft box are the right starting point; the rulebook's target of staff under 8% of sales is the right discipline.
2. **Build the services counter 7-Eleven India never had.** Every Asian market that works has one; the India P&L shows services income vanishing.
3. **Density or nothing.** Fifty stores spread over Mumbai and Pune could not share trucks or staff. Rulebook L3 (Stores 2–5 within 3–5 km) is the correct answer; so is a central kitchen only at Store 3+.
4. **Do not compete with 10-minute delivery; be the two-minute walk.** Section 11 shows that suburbs of this type are still outside or on the edge of the dark-store ring.
5. **Food quality complaints kill a convenience brand faster than price.** "Stale food" reviews are the reason Section 8's pull-by clock matters more than any marketing.
6. **7-Eleven may look for a new Indian partner.** That is not Consto's problem to solve; it is a reminder that the brand licence was never the hard part.

## 4. How do they select brands?

**Direct answer.** In Japan, headquarters decides *what can be listed* (a catalogue of ~3,000 live items, with 100–200 swapped every week) and the store owner decides *what and how much to order*, item by item, using a hypothesis → order → check loop on the POS data. This is "Tanpin Kanri", item-by-item management. Poor sellers are simply dropped. Nobody pays to be on the shelf; the leverage runs the other way.

### 4.2 Private label and vendors

- Seven & i's product staff are organised in **6 sections and 30 subcommittees** for joint development with manufacturers (daily foods 6, fresh and deli 7, processed 5, household 8, pharmaceuticals 1, apparel 3); **about 50% of Seven Premium's better-selling items are renewed every year**; 192 items sold over ¥1 bn in FY2017 ([Seven & i Corporate Outline 2017](https://www.7andi.com/library/dbps_data/_template_/_res/en/ir/library/co/pdf/2017_05.pdf)).

### 4.3 Listing fees

- No source found that Japanese konbini charge slotting or listing fees; the merchandising guide notes "No listing fees mentioned" ([TOO International](https://www.toointernational.com/post/key-merchandising-practices-of-japanese-convenience-store-chains)). - Contrast, US grocery: a new product's slotting fee is "around $250–$1,000 per item per store" ([Repsly](https://www.repsly.com/blog/consumer-goods/slotting-fee-negotiation-strategy)).

### 4.4 Thailand: the supplier floor

CP All's network is 15,245 stores (2024): 7,743 corporate, 6,594 franchise, 908 sub-area licence ([CP All One Report 2024](https://www.cpall.co.th/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/CP_ALL_56-1_-OneReport_2024_EN_25MAR-Final.pdf)). ### 4.5 What a one-store operator can copy

| Japanese practice | Consto version (ties to rulebook R1, R2, R5) |
|---|---|
| HQ catalogue vs store order quantity | Sateesh owns the catalogue (what may be stocked); the shift lead owns daily order quantity per item, with the Consto Agent playing the counsellor (weather, festival, "Lakshmi aunty's grandson's exams") |
| Hypothesis → order → verify per item | POS forces a one-line "why" on every new item and shows sell-through by hour and day |
| 70% churn a year | At ~1,500 items that is roughly 20 items reviewed and dropped per week; rulebook R1 (zero sales in 4 weeks = out) is the right cadence |
| 2–4 brands per need | Rulebook R2: best seller + one cheaper + one premium, at most |
| Private label where a hero can clear a volume floor; renew half yearly | 10 Consto items to start (Section 12.6), each with a weekly volume floor, half refreshed every year |
| Vendor floor (licence, label, traceability) | FSSAI number, batch and use-by on every pack, a named contact; no exceptions for the local bakery or batter maker |
| No listing fees | Model slotting at zero; the only "fee" to ask for is free-fill or sale-or-return on a first order |

A caution: the "sell X units a week or delist" threshold is retail folklore; no primary 7-Eleven document states a number. Consto should set its own and treat it as a hypothesis.

## 5. Do they run 24-hour stores?

**Direct answer.** Yes, almost everywhere in Asia: about 90% of Japan's three big chains are 24-hour (Yano Research, Apr 2025), Thailand makes it mandatory for franchisees, and Taiwan, Korea, Malaysia and the Philippines default to it. But Japan has been quietly retreating since a 2019 franchisee revolt, and the share is expected to drop below 80% within five years. In India, 7-Eleven opened as "24-hour" and ran 7am–11pm. Telangana has allowed any shop to run 24x7 since April 2023 for a ₹10,000 annual fee plus staff-welfare conditions.

## 6. Do they have medical shops or medicines inside?

**Direct answer.** Mostly no, and where they tried, a "qualified person on premises" rule killed it. In Japan only 0.7% of ~57,000 konbini could sell over-the-counter (OTC) drugs as of Feb 2023; a May 2025 law will let stores hand over OTC without a pharmacist from about spring 2027 after an online consultation. Korea allows a closed list of 13 "safety household medicines" in convenience stores since 2012 (11 actually on sale). Taiwan's Class B OTC needs no licence. Thailand's CP All runs 460+ pharmacist-staffed eXta Plus pharmacies next to 7-Elevens and sells "household remedies" inside. India already lets an unlicensed shop sell a short Schedule K list (ORS, bandages, repellents, petroleum jelly, nicotine gum, zinc), but paracetamol and antacids without a licence are allowed only in villages of 1,000 people or fewer with no chemist, which a suburb or Tier-2 town is not.

## 8. How do they maintain food (handle, keep safe, time-stamp)?

**Direct answer.** They never cook in the store. Food is made daily in dedicated outside factories (Japan: ~172 plants, 93% serving Seven-Eleven only; Thailand: CP All's own CPRAM), shipped in temperature-separated trucks two to three times a day, stamped with an hour-level consumption deadline, and pulled from the shelf two hours before that deadline. Hot-counter items run on a timer per batch (fried about 4 hours, steamed about 6 hours) at or above 63°C. Ordering is now AI-assisted in all three Japanese chains. This is the exact shape of Consto's "no cooking, no gas, hot case + chiller + shelf" rule.

### 8.2 Deliveries and temperature bands

- Seven-Eleven Japan runs **four temperature bands: rice items (~20°C), ambient, chilled, frozen** ([SEJ sustainability](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/action/000123/)). - From July 2019 the chain began moving daily food to **two deliveries a day** by shifting bento from the 20°C band (~15 h life) to 5°C chilled, which added about 48 hours of sellable life; in 1987 no daily item lasted more than 24 h after arrival, by 2019 over 70% did ([MD Next](https://md-next.jp/13575)). By Feb 2025 **87% of SEJ's daily items have a sales deadline more than 24 hours away** ([Seven & i, Food loss measures](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)).
- Thailand went straight to chilled and frozen with counter microwaving; every store has a frozen cabinet ([CK Travels](https://www.cktravels.com/thailand-7-11-eleven-what-you-can-buy/)).

### 8.4 Hot case and in-store safety

- Japan's health ministry publishes a HACCP-style hygiene-plan guide for convenience stores covering simple in-store cooking such as frying ([MHLW guide](https://www.mhlw.go.jp/content/11130500/000767996.pdf)).
- Hold times are chain manuals reported by staff, not official documents: **fried items 4 hours, steamed buns and baked items 6 hours**; Famichiki is discarded 6 hours after cooking and carries no printed date ([Merumo](https://news.merumo.ne.jp/article/genre/3519713); [kisetsumimiyori.com](https://kisetsumimiyori.com/famichikisyomikigen/)).
- Hot-showcase product temperature guidance is 50–65°C by equipment makers versus the international ≥63°C norm ([Heaterkiki spec](https://heaterkiki.co.jp/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/SCS62H-2_202401.pdf); [YourPilla](https://yourpilla.com/answers/hot-holding-prevent-food-poisoning)).
- Most hot-snack discards happen 22:00–06:00 when footfall is lowest; chains fight it by cooking small batches timed to peaks ([Conveni-now](https://conveni-now.com/column/conveni-food-waste/)).

### 8.6 The India rules Consto must build in

- FSSAI labelling: "Expiry/Use by" is **mandatory**, "best before" optional; products with ≤3 months' life must use DD/MM/YY; chains with 10+ outlets must display calories and allergens ([FSSAI Labelling & Display compendium, Apr 2025](https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Comp_Labelling%20Display_Version%20VII_03042025.pdf); [FoodSafetyStandard.in](https://foodsafetystandard.in/food-safety-and-standards-labelling-and-display-regulations/)).
- FSSAI Schedule 4 temperatures as summarised by a training consultancy (official PDF was image-only): danger zone 5–60°C; **cold display ≤5°C; hot display ≥60°C**; reheat to 75°C for ≥2 minutes; reheated food held ≥63°C ([Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/insights/are-your-foods-at-safe-zone-temperature-requirements-for-food-to-be-safe/)). Check against the compendium before writing the SOP.
- FSSAI Basic Registration covers turnover up to ₹1.5 cr from 1 Apr 2026 (State Licence above that) ([S.S. Rana & Co](https://ssrana.in/articles/fssai-raises-turnover-limits-for-food-business-registration-and-licensing/)). A store doing ₹30–40 lakh a month crosses that in a few months, so plan a State Licence from day one.

## 9. What do they do with waste food?

**Direct answer.** First they try not to make it (longer shelf life, AI ordering). Then they discount it in a fixed evening window (Taiwan: 30–35% off from 5pm or 8pm; Japan: points from 2020, price stickers from 2021–24). Then they donate a little and recycle most of the rest into animal feed and compost through a separate waste-logistics network. Even so, a Japanese store still throws away about ¥4.68 million of food a year, roughly 2–3% of sales, and the franchisee pays 80–90% of it.

### 9.3 Donation, recycling, targets

- SEJ's "Eco-distribution" network (with Mitsui, since 1994) collects store waste in three streams and sends unsold food to feed and compost; fryer oil is 100% recycled ([SEJ sustainability](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/action/000108/); [SEJ food loss](https://sustainability.sej.co.jp/esgp/environment/foodloss/)). - Lawson trucks unsold food to a recycling plant in Fukuoka for pig feed (since 2014); compost or feed recycling at 2,336 stores; recycling rate 57.4% (FY2025); "Flapbanks" donated ~1.25 m items to food banks ([Lawson](https://www.lawson.co.jp/company/activity/environment/preservation/waste/)). About 40% of konbini food waste in Japan is still incinerated (est. from the recycling rate).
- Donation is small: SEJ had food-drive boxes in 92 stores in FY2024 ([Seven & i](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/theme/theme3/recycle.html)).
- Targets: Seven & i −50% food waste by 2030 and −75% by 2050 vs FY2013; FamilyMart −50% by 2030 vs 2018 (2023 actual −28.9%); Lawson −50% by 2030 ([Seven & i GREEN CHALLENGE 2050](https://www.7andi.com/en/sustainability/policy/environment_03.html); [FamilyMart](https://www.family.co.jp/company/news_releases/2024/20241225_01.html)).
- Thailand: CP Group routes 25,207 t to fertiliser, 13,586 t to animal feed, 6,436 t to black-soldier-fly protein, 4,290 t to energy (2025) ([The Nation](https://www.nationthailand.com/sustainability/esg/40068529)).

### 9.4 India: the rules and the practice

- The **Food Safety and Standards (Recovery and Distribution of Surplus Food) Regulations, 2019** (gazetted 26 Jul 2019, in force 1 Jul 2020) set the duties of donating businesses and of surplus-food distribution organisations on handling, transport, storage and labelling ([Packaging South Asia](https://packagingsouthasia.com/type-of-article/industry-news/fssai-notifies-food-recovery-and-distribution-of-surplus-food-regulations-2019/); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/fssai-food-donation-regulations/)). The Indian Food Sharing Alliance links 85 NGOs to food businesses ([Nutrition Connect / GAIN](https://nutritionconnect.org/food-safety-standards-authority-india-fssai-guiding-optimisation-surplus-food-donation)).
- Practice: Robin Hood Army collects unsold food from restaurants and bakeries in 200+ cities with zero funds ([Global Indian](https://www.globalindian.com/youth/how-the-robin-hood-army-is-feeding-indias-hungry/)); Zomato "Food Rescue" resells ~4 lakh cancelled orders a month at a discount ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=485748)). There is no Indian "Too Good To Go" at scale.
- No Indian retail chain (DMart, Ratnadeep, Reliance) publishes fresh-food waste rates. Indian bakery and snack vendors commonly take unsold stock back informally; nobody has written it down.

### 9.5 Consto's waste chain, in order (ties to rulebook F4, F5)

1. **Order less, sell out, reorder** (the daily log feeds tomorrow's order).
2. **Discount window**: one fixed evening slot, e.g. 8pm, 30–35% off hot-case and chilled items tagged at pull-by minus ~5 h, announced on WhatsApp to opted-in customers (Consto Agent, under 40 words, name first).
3. **Vendor sale-or-return**: a written credit percentage per returned unit for bread, buns, batter and snacks; Europe's bread take-back shows the supplier can carry most of this ([Resources, Conservation & Recycling, 2017](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0921344917300411)).
4. **Donation at 10pm** of still-safe food to a Robin Hood Army or IFSA partner, with a signed hand-over log, under the 2019 surplus-food regulations.
5. **Wet waste** to a vendor who takes cooked rice and bread for cattle or pigs, then municipal.
6. **Budget**: plan 2–3% of fresh-food sales as waste (the Japanese norm after 40 years of optimisation); the rulebook's "under 5%" is a fair ceiling for the first 90 days, then tighten to 3%. Zero waste means the case was empty.

### 10.3 The pattern

Winners share three things: a local owner with a food or FMCG supply chain (CP, Uni-President, FEMSA, Salim); a smaller, cheaper, more grocery-heavy box than the Japanese template; and a store that doubles as a payments and services counter. Indonesia is the cautionary tale for Consto's rest corner: customers "hung out without buying much" and prepared food spoiled ([Momentum Works](https://thelowdown.momentum.asia/unique-battle-between-international-and-local-convenience-stores-in-indonesia/)). ## 11. What is unique about India, where people are cost-conscious?

**Direct answer.** Three things make India different from every 7-Eleven market. First, the MRP printed on the pack is a legal ceiling, so the "pay a little more for nearness" model is illegal on packaged goods; margin must come from food, own brand and services. Second, the shopper already visits a shop every other day, buys ₹5–₹20 packs, expects credit and free delivery from the kirana, and switches shops for a ₹5 difference. Third, quick commerce has taken the top-up trip in the city core but has not yet reached most suburbs and Tier-2 towns of this type.

### 12.1 Hero product

| Option | Holds 3–4 h? | Non-MRP? | Margin | Unique vs kirana and tiffin centre? | No-gas fit | Daily habit? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. Machine chai (₹10–15) + Osmania biscuit (₹8) | Yes (made per cup) | Yes | 50–70% | Partly (tea stalls exist, but not clean, fast and ₹10) | Premix machine | Yes, every hour of the day |
| B. Consto toastie (₹30–50) | Yes (toasted to order) | Yes | 30–45% (est.) | Yes, nobody local does it | Electric toaster | Evening and office habit, not morning |
| C. Ghee podi idli (₹25–40) | Yes, in a covered tray | Yes | 25–40% (vendor keeps part) | Partly (tiffin centres at ₹20–40), but podi ties to the Consto pantry | Hot case only | Morning habit; competes head-on on price |
| D. Boiled / masala eggs (₹10–15) | Yes | Yes | 30–40% | Partly | Hot case | Small, daily, India's tea egg |

**Recommendation:** make **A (machine chai + Osmania biscuit)** the hero for Store 1 and **B (the toastie)** the signature item it sells alongside. A is the Seven Café / CITY CAFE pattern (about 120 cups a store a day in Japan, NT$17 bn in Taiwan), runs on the cheapest machine, undercuts the ₹25 Irani cup, earns the highest margin in the store and brings people in from 6am to 11pm. B is the Thailand proof that one toasted item can carry a chain, and it is the item no one in a typical Indian suburb sells. Keep C as the hot-case anchor, priced at the street ladder (₹30 for 3 idli with podi and ghee), and D as the ₹10 impulse. Test all four for 60 days (rulebook R6) and keep the two with the best units per day; expect chai to win on units and the toastie on rupees.

### 12.2 Hours

| Option | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| A. 6am–11pm from day one | Kirana closes 10:30; staff cost under 8%; rulebook H1 | Gives up the 11pm–6am 16% (Japanese figure) and the "always open" halo |
| B. 6am–midnight | Catches the 9pm–midnight slot (8–10% of sales in Japan) | One extra hour of a two-person shift |
| C. 24h from day one | Telangana GO makes it legal for ₹10,000 a year; nobody else in the suburb is open | 7-Eleven India and Twenty Four Seven both died on 24h labour; women's night-shift conditions; a single night worker alone with cash |
| D. 24h after 90 days if 9–11pm sales exceed 8% of the day | Decides on data, not theory | Needs the data and a plan for night security |

**Recommendation:** **A for Store 1, measured against D.** Open 6am–11pm, log sales by hour, and file the G.O.Ms.No.4 exemption only when the 9–11pm slot passes 8% of daily sales and the WhatsApp night-order test (orders after 10pm delivered next morning by 6:30) shows demand. Go 24h first in the highway format, where Japan's lesson (24h stays where the store is the only thing open) applies cleanly.

### 12.4 Waste policy

| Element | Options | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| Discount window | Points only (Japan 2020) / price sticker (Japan 2024) / fixed evening window (Taiwan) | **Fixed window: 8pm, 30–35% off**, tagged at pull-by minus 5 h, announced on WhatsApp to opted-in customers; add loyalty points only if margins cannot absorb cuts |
| Vendor terms | Buy outright / sale-or-return with a credit % / consignment | **Sale-or-return with a written credit %** for bread, buns, batter and snacks; buy outright for hot-case items (the vendor will pad prices otherwise) |

### 12.5 Brand-selection rules (Tanpin Kanri for one store with WhatsApp data)

1. **Catalogue vs order.** Sateesh owns the list of what may be stocked; the shift lead owns tomorrow's quantity per item. The Agent plays the Japanese field counsellor: it surfaces weather, festival, school-exam and "Lakshmi aunty asked for 3 Roses" cues.
2. **One line of why.** Every new item enters the POS with a one-line hypothesis (who buys it, when, why). No hypothesis, no listing.
3. **1–3 brands per need** (rulebook R2): best seller, one cheaper, one premium. Never a fourth.
4. **Four-week zero rule** (rulebook R1): no sale in four weeks means out, replaced the same week. At ~1,500 items that is roughly 20 items reviewed a week, matching Japan's 70% yearly churn.
5. **Vendor floor:** FSSAI number, batch and use-by on every pack, a named contact. No exceptions for the bakery down the road.
6. **No listing fees, no free shelf.** Ask distributors for free-fill or sale-or-return on a first order instead; model slotting income at zero.
7. **WhatsApp as the panel.** Before listing a new chilled or hot item, ask 30 opted-in customers in one message (under 40 words, name first). Seven Premium uses a customer web community for the same job.
8. **Own brand only where a hero clears a floor.** Set a weekly volume floor per Consto item; renew half the own-brand list every year, as Seven Premium does.

Price each 10–15% below the nearest national brand and still earn 35–40% (rulebook PR4). The batter and podis also feed the hot case, which is how the chain's own-brand and fresh lines reinforce each other at Ratnadeep (30% of sales) and Seven-Eleven (40.5% processed-food margin).

### 12.7 Two cross-cutting suggestions

- **Services before SKUs.** Every Asian winner and every Indian loser is explained by this. AEPS, BBPS, recharge, parcel holding and pre-orders should be live on day one, not Phase 2; they are also the only way the Agent learns a phone number without asking.
- **One KPI on the wall:** average daily sales per store, split hot food / chilled / packaged / services. SuperK's ₹10 lakh a month (about ₹33,000 a day) is the Tier-2/3 floor to beat; 7-Eleven India's ~₹30,000–35,000 a day in Mumbai is the warning that a bigger, dearer box does not move that number.
