# Consto Rule Book

**Status:** Draft v0.1 for Sateesh's review · September 2026
**What this is:** The rules that make a Consto store a Consto store. Built from how the world's best convenience stores work, then changed for India and for Consto's hybrid idea.
**Related:** `consto-category-master.md` (what we sell), `00-master-prd.md` (the full concept)

Every rule has three parts:
- **Rule**: what we always do
- **World**: which convenience store does this, and where
- **Consto**: how it looks in our store

Numbers from other companies are approximate. Check them before using them in a pitch.

---

## 0. What Consto is

### One sentence

> **Consto is a hybrid convenience store: your daily needs in 3 minutes, a place to sit and rest, and everything for a celebration, from people who remember you.**

### Three layers

| Layer | Share of floor | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Convenience core** | ~75% | Ready food, drinks, snacks, daily essentials, services. Fast in, fast out. |
| **Rest** | ~10–12% | Seats, water, charging, washroom. "Come rest even if you don't buy." |
| **Celebrate** | ~10–12% | Cakes, birthday kits, ready-wrapped gifts, festival kits |

The memory layer (staff and software that remember each customer) runs across all three.

### Supermarket vs convenience store vs Consto

| | Supermarket (DMart, Ratnadeep) | Convenience store (7-Eleven, FamilyMart) | **Consto hybrid** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Visit | Weekly or monthly, 30–60 min | Daily, 3–5 min | Daily, 3–5 min, or stay and rest |
| Items | 8,000–20,000 | 2,500–3,000 | 1,500–2,500 |
| Staples | Yes, bulk | Small packs | **No** |
| Food | Raw ingredients | Ready food, heated | Ready food, heated, **no gas** |
| Price | Below MRP (discounts) | Slightly higher | **At MRP**, never above |
| Staff | Many, one job each | Few, every job | **Few, every job** |
| Seating | No | Some (Taiwan, Korea, Japan) | **Yes, open to all** |
| Celebrations | Cake aisle, generic | Small cakes, seasonal pre-orders | **Birthday kits + pre-orders remembered by the agent** |
| Knows you | No | No | **Yes** |

## 2. Location rules

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | **Be within 5 minutes' walk of your customers.** | Every chain. In Japan, most customers walk. | Pick a site with 2,000+ households within 500 m. |
| L2 | **Be on the way, not out of the way.** Same side of the road as the morning or evening flow. | 7-Eleven studies which side of the road people walk to work on. | Watch the site for a morning and an evening. Which side do people pass on? |
| L3 | **Cluster your stores.** Open Store 2 and 3 close to Store 1. | 7-Eleven Japan's "dominant area" strategy: many stores in one area share trucks, ads and staff. | Stores 2–5 within 3–5 km of Beeramguda. One vendor route, one relief-staff pool. |
| L4 | **Visible from 50 m, with parking for 2-wheelers.** | Bright signage everywhere; parking at US and Thai stores. | Lit sign, glass front, space for 4–6 bikes. |
| L5 | **Know your neighbours.** Count every competitor within 500 m before signing. | Standard site selection for every chain. | Kiranas, bakeries, tea stalls, pharmacies, quick-commerce delivery times. |

## 3. Size and layout rules

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **Small is the point.** | Japan: ~1,000–1,600 sq ft. Korea and Indonesia: often smaller. | 600–800 sq ft (decide once, in writing). |
| S2 | **Daily needs at the back, impulse items at the counter.** People walk past everything to reach milk. | Every chain puts milk and drinks at the back wall. | Fridges (milk, curd, drinks) on the back wall. Snacks, chocolates and gum at the counter. |
| S3 | **Food is the first thing you see.** | Japan and Thailand: hot case and coffee near the entrance or counter. | Hot case, toaster, tea machine near the counter, visible from the door. |
| S4 | **Nothing above eye level that customers need.** Low shelves, clear sight lines. | Standard in Japan (shelves ~1.5 m or less). | Shelves at 1.5 m max; staff can see the whole store from the counter (less theft, fewer staff). |
| S5 | **One aisle loop, no dead ends.** | Standard layout. | Enter → back wall → around → counter. |
| S6 | **Keep a small backroom.** | Japan: minimal backroom; stock goes straight to shelves. | Backroom 10% max. Frequent small deliveries instead of big storage. |
| S7 | **Floor plan split** (starting point for 700 sq ft) | – | Shelves 40%, fridges and freezers 15%, counter + hot case + machine 10%, rest corner 10–12%, celebrate zone 8–10%, backroom 10%, entry and aisle make up the rest. |

## 4. Range rules (what we stock)

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **If it doesn't sell every week, it goes.** | 7-Eleven Japan manages item by item; a large share of items is replaced each year. | Weekly report of items with zero sales in 4 weeks. Replace them. |
| R2 | **1–3 brands per item, not 10.** | Every chain. | Best seller + one cheaper + one premium, at most. |
| R3 | **No staples, no bulk, no loose.** | Convenience stores sell small packs only. | Decided: no rice, atta, oil, dal. Only salt and sugar. See `consto-category-master.md`. |
| R4 | **Small packs win.** ₹5, ₹10, ₹20 packs and sachets sell fastest. | Asia-wide: sachets and single-serve. | Sachets for shampoo, detergent, coffee, snacks. |
| R5 | **Own brand where no one can compare the price.** | Seven Premium, Famimaru, Indomaret brand. | Consto podis, pickles, namkeen, batter, sweets. Start with 10 items. |
| R6 | **One hero product.** Something people cross the road for. | FamilyMart's Famichiki, 7-Eleven Thailand's toastie, Taiwan's tea eggs. | Pick one in the pilot: a Consto toastie, machine chai + Osmania biscuit, ghee podi idli. |
| R7 | **Change with the season and the festival.** | Japan: Christmas cakes, summer drinks, winter oden. | Telangana festival calendar sets the front shelf every month (Sankranti, Ugadi, Bonalu, Bathukamma, Deepavali). |

## 5. Food rules

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | **No cooking. No gas. Heat only.** | Central kitchens cook; stores heat and serve (Japan, Thailand). | Microwave, sandwich toaster, hot-water dispenser. Electric only. |
| F2 | **Two deliveries a day.** | 7-Eleven Japan: up to 3 a day. | Morning (by 6:30 am) and afternoon (by 3 pm). Two vendors, so one late vendor doesn't empty the hot case. |
| F3 | **Only sell food that holds well.** | Every item is designed for its shelf life. | Hot case: idli, pulihora, biryani, puffs, samosa. No dosa, puri or bajji (they go soggy). |
| F4 | **Every batch has a time label. Throw away on time.** | Strict in Japan: food past its time is removed and logged. | Hot case: 4 hours. Chiller: vendor's use-by date. Every thrown-away item is logged. |
| F5 | **Wastage is a number we watch daily.** | 7-Eleven Japan orders item by item to keep waste low. | Target under 5% of food sales. Order less, sell out, then order more. |
| F6 | **Veg and non-veg kept apart.** | – (India-specific) | Separate hot-case trays and labels. Decide if non-veg is sold at all. |
| F7 | **FSSAI licence and clean hands, always.** | Food safety law everywhere. | FSSAI licence before day one. Gloves and tongs for every hot-case item. |

## 6. Low-staff rules

The goal: **run a 6 am–11 pm store with 5 people**, with 2 on each shift and 1 for relief. Staff cost under 8% of sales.

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Everyone does every job.** Cashier, restock, clean, hot case, services. | Japan: often 2 staff per shift doing everything. | No "cashier only" or "cleaner only". Train every person on every job. |
| P2 | **No weighing, cutting or packing in store.** | Everything arrives pre-packed. | Rule R3 + F1 make this possible. |
| P3 | **Vendors fill their own fridges and shelves.** | Common in Asia and India (drinks, ice cream, bread, milk). | Coke, Pepsi, Amul, ice-cream and bread reps restock their own space. Staff only check. |
| P4 | **Fast checkout.** Barcode, UPI QR with sound box, no change counting. | Semi-self registers in Japan (customer pays at the machine). | Barcode scanner + UPI sound box on day one. Self-pay screen later. |
| P5 | **Receive stock by barcode, not by counting.** | Standard with modern POS. | Consto POS / Inventory. |
| P6 | **Checklists, not memory, for routine work.** | Every chain runs shift checklists. | Opening, every-hour, and closing checklists (Consto Ops). |
| P7 | **Software remembers; people are warm.** | – (Consto only) | Staff don't memorise customers. The screen tells them "This is Lakshmi aunty; she likes 3 Roses tea; her grandson's exams are this week." |
| P8 | **Seats and services are self-serve where possible.** | Korea: customers use the microwave and hot water themselves. | Customers can use the water dispenser, charging points and hot water themselves. |

## 7. Rest rules ("Come rest even if you don't buy")

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| RS1 | **A small, clean place to sit.** | Taiwan, Korea and many Japanese stores have eat-in seats. | 6–10 seats: a window counter with stools + one small table. Max 12% of floor. |
| RS2 | **No purchase needed.** | Most eat-in corners expect a purchase; Consto doesn't. | Written on the wall. Staff never ask people to buy. |
| RS3 | **Free water, charging and a fan or AC.** | Korea and Taiwan: charging and water common. | Water dispenser, 4–6 charging points, fan or AC. |
| RS4 | **A clean washroom, if the space allows.** | Japan and Taiwan: washrooms in many stores. | Checked every hour, logged on the checklist. Essential for the highway format. |
| RS5 | **Seats face the food.** Gentle nudge, never pressure. | Eat-in corners sit next to the hot case and coffee. | Seats look at the tea machine and hot case. |
| RS6 | **Priority for elders, drivers and mothers with children.** | – (Consto only) | One priority seat, marked kindly. |

## 8. Celebrate rules (cakes and gifts)

### Cakes: which sizes?

| Size | Price (approx.) | In stock or pre-order | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slices, cup cakes, jar cakes, pastries** | ₹40–₹120 | ★ In stock daily (chiller) | Impulse buys, sell every day, low waste |
| **Small whole cake, 250–500 g** | ₹200–₹450 | ★ In stock, 3–6 a day | The real convenience cake: "I forgot, I need a cake in 5 minutes." Small family, couple, office desk birthday. |
| **1 kg** | ₹500–₹900 | ◐ Keep 1–2 of the best-selling flavour; the rest by pre-order | Test in the first 60 days. If they don't sell within 2 days, move to pre-order only. |
| **2 kg and above, custom, photo cakes** | ₹1,000+ | ✕ Pre-order only (24 hours' notice, from a partner bakery) | Planned parties. Too expensive to waste. Bakeries already do custom work. |

**Always have an eggless option** for every size. Many Indian families are vegetarian.

**The Consto difference:** the agent remembers birthdays. "Priya, Aarav's birthday is Friday. Shall we book his chocolate cake, 1 kg, eggless like last year?" This turns big cakes into **zero-waste pre-orders** that customers are grateful for.

| C2 | **Cakes: small in stock, big by pre-order.** | 7-Eleven Japan: small cakes daily; big seasonal cakes (Christmas) by pre-order. | See the table above. |

## 10. Pricing rules

| # | Rule | World | Consto |
|---|---|---|---|
| PR1 | **Sell at MRP. Never above. Don't fight DMart on price.** | Convenience stores charge more than supermarkets everywhere. | MRP always. Customers pay for nearness, speed and care. |
| PR2 | **Known items must feel fair.** Milk, bread and eggs at the normal price. | Chains keep "price-watched" items competitive. | Never overprice milk, bread, eggs or water. |
| PR3 | **Offers that add, not discounts that cut.** | Korea: "1+1" and "2+1" offers. | "Chai + biscuit ₹20", "2+1 on chips", combo breakfasts. |
| PR4 | **Own brand earns the margin.** | Every chain. | Own-brand items priced 10–15% below national brands, still higher margin. |
| PR5 | **Round prices for fast checkout.** | – | Combos and food at ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹99. |

## 11. Memory rules (Consto only)

No convenience store in the world does this. It's our reason to exist.

| # | Rule | Consto |
|---|---|---|
| M1 | **Consent first.** | Ask before saving anything or sending any WhatsApp message (DPDP Act). |
| M2 | **Remember what helps, not everything.** | Name, family, usual items, birthdays, festivals, dietary needs. Nothing else. |
| M3 | **Greet by name.** | The counter screen shows the customer's name and one useful thing to say. |
| M4 | **Suggest, never spam.** | Messages only when useful (a birthday, an item running out, a festival kit). Under 40 words. Customer's name always. |
| M5 | **Memory belongs to the store, not the staff member.** | If staff leave, the relationship stays. |
| M6 | **Delete on request within 30 days.** | Non-negotiable (DPDP Act). |

## 13. What Consto never does

- Cook with gas, or cook at all
- Sell staples, bulk packs or loose items
- Sell above MRP
- Get into a price war with DMart or quick commerce
- Keep items that don't sell every week
- Sell gutka, lottery tickets or anything else illegal
- Let the washroom or seats get dirty
- Make anyone feel they must buy
- Send a message without consent
- Forget a customer
