# Consto

## A modern Indian convenience store, designed around the way people actually shop.

A complete product and service concept for India's first AI-backed neighbourhood store. The warmth of a kirana, the discipline of a 7-Eleven, and an AI memory layer that remembers each customer like family.

Starting with one store in Beeramguda, Hyderabad.

[Read the full case study →](https://github.com/iamsatz/Consto) · [Read the concept (5-min) →](https://github.com/iamsatz/Consto)

---

## Why I built this

I spent 15 years inside the family kirana in Gadwal, Telangana — from age 7 to 22. I knew every regular customer's children by name. I watched my father extend credit to families who couldn't pay on time, never with embarrassment.

In 2007, I stepped away from the kirana. From that moment, my observation began. For 18+ years I watched Indian retail change. DMart strip the local soul out of retail to scale. Zepto try to win without ever knowing the customer. 200,000 kiranas close in two years.

Earlier this year, in February and March 2026, I travelled to Thailand and Taiwan. I walked into 7-Eleven and FamilyMart stores. Operationally brilliant. Multi-service, fast, well-stocked. But nobody knew my name. Emotionally empty.

*That trip is when every dot connected.* The kirana had warmth but no scale. Convenience stores had scale but no warmth. Quick commerce had speed but no soul. Nobody in India had built the combination.

So I started Consto.

## The problem

Indian suburbs have no retail format that remembers the customer.

| Format | Has | Lacks |
|---|---|---|
| **Kirana** | Warmth, trust, credit, community | 50% stockouts, no modern services, no digital memory |
| **Quick commerce** | Speed, convenience, selection | 20-30% quality failures, zero relationships |
| **Supermarket** | Variety, organisation, consistency | 20-40 min away, staff rotates, customers are numbers |

Nobody owns the customer relationship.

400M+ families in Tier 2-3 India lose 40-60 minutes daily juggling 3-4 apps and shops. The problem isn't access. It's that every format fails them differently.

---

## The opportunity

A $950B+ market. $1.3T projected by 2028. 88% still unorganised.

India has *zero* organised neighbourhood-store chains. Japan has 80,000+ (7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson). Thailand has 13,000+. South Korea 50,000+. Even Indonesia has 20,000+ Indomaret stores.

The format is proven in every Asian market it enters. Nobody has Indianised it.

---

## The solution

A 1,000-1,500 sqft physical neighbourhood store. Three pillars hold it up. Four format variants adapt to where the customer is. One AI memory layer powers everything.

### Three pillars

**Pillar 01 · Memory, Knowledge, Wisdom**
Every customer gets a dedicated AI agent that remembers them. Category heads find patterns across customer groups. Across years and stores, the system compounds into institutional wisdom. Humans deliver the warmth. AI scales the memory. Wisdom guides the decisions.

**Pillar 02 · One-Stop Services**
Groceries, AEPS cash, bill payments, recharges, OTC medicines, fresh batter, festival items. Five daily-life tasks in one 15-minute visit. ~40% of revenue from services at 50-60% margins.

**Pillar 03 · Community Anchoring**
Festival calendar. Monthly Hub Huddle events. *"Come rest even if you do not buy"* policy. Credit with dignity. Policies inherently worth talking about — turning the marketing budget into word-of-mouth.

### Four formats

| Format | Size | Where | Mission |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily** | 400-600 sqft | Highways, IT corridors, offices | Quick top-up, services counter |
| **Home** | 1,000-1,500 sqft | Residential (Beeramguda starts here) | Daily fresh run + complete weekly shop possible |
| **Hub** | 2,000-3,000 sqft | Community clusters | Hub Huddle events, chai counter, neighbourhood anchor |
| **Route** | 200-400 sqft packing hub | Door delivery model | Subscription-based daily delivery (Sid's Farm style) |

Plus one future concept: **Consto Travel** — hygienic home-style food ordered ahead by travellers (train, bus, car), picked up while driving or delivered to railway platforms and bus stops. Phase 4+, not in current plans.

## Inside the Memory Engine

The AI memory layer is the moat. It's not magic — it's a deliberate learning system with two inputs and four outputs.

### Two inputs feed it

- **Company-trained baseline** — brand voice rules, Telugu festival calendar, persona archetypes, cultural sensitivities, inventory and pricing data. The kindergarten the AI graduates from before meeting anyone.

- **Customer behaviour signal** — every purchase, timing pattern, family signal, miss, request, response. No data invented. Everything is what the customer actually did or said.

### The learning curve

| Stage | What the AI knows |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Phone + first name only |
| Month 1 | Shopping rhythm |
| Month 3 | Brand preferences + substitutes |
| Month 6 | Family context + festival routine |
| Year 1 | Life context, anticipates needs days ahead |
| Year 2+ | Knows the customer the way a 30-year shopkeeper does |

### Four outputs

1. **For the customer** — widget content, agent message drafts, in-store nudges, pre-booked festival items. Real-time.
2. **For the central team** — weekly category insights ("27 working moms asked for ragi this week — currently we stock 0"). Cross-store patterns.
3. **For human staff** — AI-suggested tasks. "Mention Aarav's birthday to Priya today." "Restock ragi." "Expand Bonalu range next year."
4. **For the owner and the brand** — Wisdom. Strategic recommendations every quarter. *"Open Store 2 in Manikonda, not Kompally."* *"Drop Bru coffee — across 3 years, only 4% of customers ever bought it."*

The four engagement layers reach customers differently:
- In-store POS as the primary surface (where the relationship happens)
- A lightweight subscriber app for power users
- SMS for transactional (Phase 1.5)
- WhatsApp as an opt-in channel for emotional messaging (Phase 2+)

## What's next

The path from concept to franchise SaaS — staged so each phase validates before the next.

1. **The pilot (next)** — 20-30 known contacts, manual AI-assisted care, 60 days, ₹0 capital. Validates the core thesis without store-build risk.
2. **Store 1** — Consto Home format, 1,000-1,500 sqft in Beeramguda. Soft launch with 50 customers if pilot validates.
3. **Consto OS Phase 1** — POS + Agent. Built in parallel with the store launch.
4. **OS Phase 2-4** — Inventory, Notify, Loyalty, Ops, Predict, HQ. Built as the store generates real data.
5. **Franchise model** — once Store 1 proves unit economics (Month 12+).
6. **Consto OS as SaaS** — once franchise model proves the platform layer is the scalable business. Target: ₹2-5K/month per kirana, $300M+ TAM at 5% penetration of India's 12M kiranas.

## What this exploration taught me

About product:
- *Memory is the moat, not technology.* Year-1 memory is thin. Year-5 memory is irreplaceable.
- *Channels are commodities; relationships compound.* WhatsApp is convenient. Real warmth happens in-store.
- *Format follows customer, not the other way around.* Pure convenience formats fail in Indian residential because the mental model doesn't exist. Hybrid wins.

About strategy:
- *The deepest competitive moat is the one that can't be bought with capital.* DMart cannot buy what Consto builds across years of compounding memory.
- *Honesty about what's tested vs untested earns more trust than over-claiming.*

About building:
- *Architecture decisions made early are nearly impossible to reverse.* Agent embedded in POS, not WhatsApp-first. Hybrid platform (Electron for POS, web for the rest). Sonnet for thinking tasks, Haiku for speed. These choices set the next two years.
